## 1. Regulatory Overview & Key Facts

Minnesota imposes a **layered, high-barrier regulatory environment** on SmartContractor's core product flows. The state enforces strict contractor conduct restrictions, heavily regulates post-loss Assignment of Benefits (AOB), caps consumer loan rates at 50% APR (SF 2744, effective January 1, 2024), and requires money transmitter licensing for virtual currency business activity under the Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA). All SmartContractor flows — token collateral equipment credit, ClaimBridge insurance claim advances, and assignment of claim proceeds — are **BLOCKED** pending state-specific legal review.

|------|--------|
| Primary financial regulator | Minnesota Department of Commerce — Division of Banking |
| Insurance regulator | Minnesota Department of Commerce — Insurance Division |
| Contractor regulator | Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry (DLI) |
| Usury cap | 8% for written contracts under $100,000 (Minn. Stat. § 334.01) |
| Consumer loan APR cap | 50% APR for small/short-term loans (SF 2744, effective 1/1/2024) |
| Money transmitter law | Minn. Stat. Chapter 53B (MTMA, including virtual currency provisions) |

| Consumer protection | Strong — anti-evasion provisions, mandatory disclosures, private right of action |

## 3. Lending, Usury & Finance Rules

### 3.1 Regulated Loan License (Minn. Stat. Ch. 56)

Any person engaging in the business of making loans of money, credit, goods, or things in action in amounts up to $100,000 and charging greater interest than permitted for non-licensees must obtain a **Regulated Loan License** from the MN Department of Commerce (§ 56.01).

- **Unlicensed lending consequence**: Loan is **void**; debtor is not obligated to pay any amounts owing and may recover all amounts paid (§ 56.19, subd. 3).
- **Criminal penalty**: Gross misdemeanor for violating § 56.01 or § 56.18 (§ 56.19, subd. 1).
- **Rate flexibility**: Licensee may contract for interest, finance charges, and other charges as provided in § 47.59 (§ 56.131).

### 3.2 Consumer Small Loans (Minn. Stat. § 47.60)

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Loan amount | $350 or less |
| Term | No more than 30 calendar days |
| Rate cap | Maximum 50% APR (effective Jan 1, 2024 via SF 2744) |
| Other charges | None permitted |
| Lender registration | Required with commissioner |
| Evasion test | Predominant economic interest; totality of circumstances (§ 47.60, subd. 8) |

### 3.3 Consumer Short-Term Loans (Minn. Stat. § 47.601)

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Loan amount | $1,300 or less |
| Minimum payment | >25% of principal within 60 days |
| Rate cap | Maximum 50% APR (§ 47.601, subd. 2(e)) |
| Ability-to-repay | Required for loans over 36% APR (§ 47.603) |
| Licensing | Required; unlicensed loan is void |

### 3.4 General Usury (Minn. Stat. Ch. 334)

- **Written contracts**: Maximum 8% interest for loans under $100,000 (§ 334.01, subd. 1).
- **Business loans over $100,000**: No limitation on interest rate, points, or finance charges (§ 334.01, subd. 2).
- **Exemptions**: State banks, credit unions, savings associations, licensed regulated lenders, and loans secured by savings accounts.

### 3.5 Commercial / Business-Purpose Loans

Loans primarily for business, commercial, or agricultural purposes are generally exempt from consumer lending licensing and rate restrictions if over $100,000 or made by exempt entities. **Critical**: Minnesota has strong anti-evasion provisions (SF 2744, effective 1/1/2024). GCSC must not structure loans to evade consumer protections.

### 3.6 Loan Brokering / Servicing

Any person "arranging" consumer short-term loans is considered a lender subject to regulation (§ 47.601, subd. 1(f)). Collection activities may require a debt collector license under Minn. Stat. § 332.31 et seq.

## 4. Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules

> **NEW SECTION** — Minnesota-specific escrow requirements for contractor advances funded from insurance proceeds.

### 4.1 Escrow Regulatory Framework

The Minnesota Department of Commerce regulates escrow activities in the state. Escrow agents handling insurance loss proceeds must comply with applicable banking and insurance division rules.

### 4.2 Escrow-Backed Advance Structure

An escrow-backed contractor advance allows a contractor to receive partial payment for repair work funded by pending insurance proceeds, with funds held in escrow pending claim resolution.

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Escrow agent | Must be licensed or authorized to hold funds in escrow under Minnesota law |
| Escrow agreement | Must specify conditions for release, return, and dispute resolution |
| Account segregation | Escrow funds must be held in a segregated, non-commingled account |
| Interest | Escrow funds may earn interest per agreement; disclosure required |

### 4.3 Insurance Proceeds Escrow

When insurance loss proceeds are involved:

- **Mortgagee priority**: If a mortgagee is named on the policy, the mortgagee's interest must be satisfied or protected before escrow release (Minn. Stat. § 65A.11).
- **Joint payee status**: Loss draft checks exceeding $40,000 typically name both the insured and mortgagee as joint payees.
- **Escrow release conditions**: Funds may only be released upon verified completion of specified repair milestones, insurer acceptance of scope, or other contractually agreed conditions.
- **Cancellation / return**: If the underlying repair contract is cancelled (e.g., under the AOB 10-business-day cancellation window), escrowed funds must be returned within 10 business days.

### 4.4 Contractor Advance as Loan Characterization

An escrow-backed contractor advance may be characterized as a **loan** under Minnesota law if:

- The contractor receives funds before work is completed and the source of repayment is the insurance proceeds;
- The homeowner is obligated to repay if insurance proceeds are insufficient or denied;
- Finance charges, fees, or interest apply.

If characterized as a loan, the advance requires appropriate lending licensure (Regulated Loan License under Ch. 56 or applicable exemption) and must comply with usury and APR caps.

### 4.5 AOB Integration

If the advance is tied to an Assignment of Benefits:

- The AOB must comply with all § 325E.67 formalities (Section 5).
- The escrow agreement must reference the AOB and incorporate the 10-business-day cancellation right.
- The contractor must deliver the AOB to the insurer within 5 business days of execution.

### 4.6 SmartContractor Implications

- **Escrow-backed advances are BLOCKED** pending confirmation that the escrow structure does not constitute unlicensed lending or money transmission.
- If the advance is a true loan, a Regulated Loan License (Ch. 56) is required.
- If the advance involves transfer or exchange of virtual currency, a Money Transmitter License (Ch. 53B) is required.
- Escrow agent must be vetted for licensure status with the MN Department of Commerce.
- **Status**: `ESCROW_ADVANCE_BLOCKED_PENDING_COUNSEL_REVIEW`

## 5. Insurance Claims, AOB & Adjuster Rules

### 5.1 Unfair Claims Practices Act (Minn. Stat. § 72A.201)

| Timeline | Requirement |
|----------|-------------|
| Acknowledgment | Reply to claim communications within **10 business days** |
| Investigation | Complete within **30 days** unless not reasonably possible |
| Accept / deny decision | Within **30 business days** after claim notification; **60 business days** after proof of loss |
| ALE notification | Notify insured of additional living expense (ALE) coverage if dwelling uninhabitable |
| Settlement payment | Issue within **5 business days** of settlement agreement |
| Explanation | Payments must include explanation of what they are for |

### 5.2 Post-Loss Assignment of Benefits (Minn. Stat. § 325E.67)

Minnesota has a **comprehensive post-loss AOB statute** applicable to residential contractors. AOBs are **permitted** but heavily formalized.

#### Formal Requirements (§ 325E.67, subd. 2)

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Scope limitation | Assignment may only authorize contractor to be named as **co-payee** |
| Itemization | Must include itemized description of work, materials, labor, fees, and total amount |
| 14-point capitalized notice | Required statutory warning (see Section 9, Disclosure 1) |
| 10-business-day cancellation right | Named insured may cancel within 10 business days after receipt of scope of work by insurer; cancellation in writing or comparable digital format; contractor must tender payments/deposits within 10 business days |
| Prominent cancellation notice | 14-point capitalized type near signature line (see Section 9, Disclosure 2) |
| Mortgagee protection | Cannot impair mortgagee interest |
| Insurer communication | Cannot prevent insurer from communicating with insured or mortgagee |

#### Contractor Obligations (§ 325E.67, subd. 3)

- Deliver copy of assignment to insurer within **5 business days** of execution.
- Cooperate with insurer's investigation.
- Comply with § 325E.66 (no deductible payment, no public adjuster activity without license).

#### Void Assignments (§ 325E.67, subd. 4)

An AOB that violates the federal Insured Homeowner's Protection Act of 1998 is **void**.

### 5.3 Mortgagee / Loss Draft Rules (Minn. Stat. § 65A.11)

- When loss is payable to mortgagee(s), insurer shall pay in **order of priority** to the extent of liability.
- Mortgage servicers typically issue joint-payee loss draft checks for claims over $40,000.
- Under $40,000 with current loans: servicers may endorse and return to borrower.
- Over $40,000 or delinquent loans: servicers deposit into escrow and release incrementally.

### 5.4 Public Adjuster Licensing (Minn. Stat. Ch. 72B)

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| License required | No person shall act as a public adjuster without a license (§ 72B.03, subd. 1(a)) |
| Bond | $10,000 surety bond |
| Background check | Fingerprint-based criminal background check |
| Continuing education | 24 hours biennially, including 3 hours ethics |
| Record retention | 5 years |
| Fee | $50 initial / renewal |

**Critical**: Minn. Stat. § 325E.66, subd. 1(a)(3) **expressly prohibits** contractors from interpreting policy provisions, advising on coverages/duties, or adjusting a claim on behalf of the insured **unless** the contractor holds a public adjuster license.

### 5.5 Claim Proceeds Assignment

General assignment of property claim proceeds is permitted under common law, subject to AOB statute § 325E.67. Any assignment to a contractor must comply with all statutory formalities. An "insurance claim advance" product providing funds pending claim payment would likely be characterized as a **loan** requiring appropriate lending licensure.

## 6. Token Collateral & Digital Asset Rules

### 6.1 Minnesota Adopted MTMA with Virtual Currency Provisions

Minnesota enacted the Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA) in whole, including the optional virtual currency provisions (2023 c 57 art 3; 2024 c 114 art 3 s 2–9). Minnesota is one of only two states (along with North Dakota) that enacted the virtual currency portion of the MTMA.

### 6.2 Virtual Currency Business Activity Licensing (Minn. Stat. § 53B.69 et seq.)

**"Virtual-currency business activity"** means:

1. Exchanging, transferring, or storing virtual currency, or engaging in virtual-currency administration;
2. Holding electronic precious metals or certificates on behalf of another;
3. Exchanging digital representations of value from online games for virtual currency or money (§ 53B.69).

**License required**: A person engaging in virtual-currency business activity must be licensed as a money transmitter (§ 53B.71).

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Tangible net worth | Minimum $100,000 or tiered percentage of assets (§ 53B.59) |
| Surety bond | Required (§ 53B.60) |
| Recordkeeping | 5-year retention for all transactions (§ 53B.71) |

### 6.3 Exemptions from Virtual Currency Regulation

- Personal/family/household use of virtual currency (§ 53B.70, subd. 2);
- Contributing only connectivity/software to decentralized virtual currency (§ 53B.70, subd. 1);
- Activities governed by EFTA, Securities Exchange Act, or Commodities Exchange Act (§ 53B.70(a));
- De minimis activity under $5,000 annually.

### 6.4 SmartContractor Token Collateral Implications

- Any token collateral lock, storage, or transfer service provided by SmartContractor to Minnesota residents would likely constitute **virtual-currency business activity** requiring a money transmitter license.
- Token collateral liquidation (converting tokens to fiat) would constitute **exchange activity**.
- A licensed money transmitter may include virtual currency in tangible net worth calculation if corresponding customer liability exists.
- **Status**: `TOKEN_COLLATERAL_BLOCKED_PENDING_MONEY_TRANSMITTER_LICENSE`

## 7. Money Transmitter & Payment Transmission Rules

### 7.1 Money Transmitter Licensing (Minn. Stat. Ch. 53B)

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| License scope | Required for persons engaging in money transmission or virtual currency business activity |
| Net worth | $100,000 minimum tangible net worth (or tiered) |
| Bond | Surety bond required (§ 53B.60) |
| Records | 5-year retention |
| NMLS | License application and management through NMLS |

### 7.2 Covered Activities

Money transmission includes:
- Receiving money or monetary value for transmission;
- Selling or issuing payment instruments;
- Selling or issuing stored value;
- Virtual currency business activity (§ 53B.69).

### 7.3 Exemptions

- Federally insured depository institutions;
- Persons operating payment systems processing payments between licensed transmitters;
- Persons providing only messaging connectivity or hardware;
- Certain designated payment systems.

### 7.4 Payment Routing for SmartContractor

Any platform-mediated flow of funds — including claim proceeds, escrow releases, loan disbursements, and repayments — that involves receiving and transmitting money may constitute money transmission. **Payment routing is BLOCKED pending money transmitter license or confirmation of exemption.**

| Residential remodeler | License (DLI) | Same |

- **No general "general contractor" state license** exists in Minnesota.
- **Bond requirements**: Surety bonds required for licensed contractors (amount varies by license type).
- **Local licensing**: Municipalities may impose additional requirements.

### 8.2 Contractor Insurance Claim Restrictions (Minn. Stat. § 325E.66) — CRITICAL

A residential contractor shall **NOT**:

1. **Advertise or promise to pay any part of an insurance deductible** (including offering compensation for inspections, claims, or referrals) (§ 325E.66, subd. 1(a)(1));
2. **Provide a repair agreement without a good faith estimate** of itemized/detailed costs (effective Aug 1, 2024) (§ 325E.66, subd. 1(a)(2));
3. **Interpret policy provisions, advise on coverages/duties, or adjust a property insurance claim on behalf of the insured UNLESS licensed as a public adjuster under Ch. 72B** (§ 325E.66, subd. 1(a)(3)).

**Consequences of violation**:
- Insurer is not obligated to consider the contractor's estimate;
- Insured/insurer may sue for damages;
- DLI may enforce.

**Disclosure requirement**: Contractor must provide written notification of these requirements with its initial estimate.

### 8.3 SmartContractor Contractor Flow

- Contractors on the platform **CANNOT negotiate with insurance companies on behalf of homeowners** unless separately licensed as public adjusters.
- Any platform feature that enables or facilitates claim negotiation by contractors would violate § 325E.66.
- Contractors **CAN** provide detailed, itemized repair estimates to homeowners.
