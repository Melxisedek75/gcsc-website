**Key State Characteristics**: Alaska has no state income tax and no state sales tax. Contractor licensing varies by municipality (no statewide general contractor license). The Alaska Division of Insurance regulates insurance matters. ## 2. Contractor / Home Improvement Rules

### Contractor Licensing
- Is a state general contractor license required? **No** — Alaska has no statewide general contractor license; licensing is handled at the municipal/local level
- **State-level specialty registration**: Alaska Division of Corporations, Business and Professional Licensing (DCBPL) does register certain specialty contractor types
- **License types** (where required locally): General Contractor, General Contractor with Residential Endorsement, Handyman (contracts under $10,000), Specialty Contractor, Mechanical Contractor
- **Requirements** (state-level): Public liability insurance ($20,000 property damage, $50,000/$100,000 injury), bond ($10,000-$25,000 depending on type), business license under AS 43.70, FEIN
- **Application fee**: $350 where applicable; renewal every two years by September 30 of even-numbered years
- Restoration companies: classified as specialty contractors requiring registration where applicable
- **Key source**: Alaska Department of Commerce, Community, and Economic Development — https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/cbpl

### Home Improvement Contract Rules
- No specific Alaska home improvement contract statute with uniform disclosure requirements found
- Standard contract law applies; written contracts recommended
- Alaska Consumer Protection Act (AS 45.50.471) provides broad protections against unfair/deceptive trade practices
- Municipalities may impose additional requirements
- Source: https://law.alaska.gov/department/civil/consumer/laws.html

## 3. Lending / Credit Rules

### Consumer / Homeowner Lending
- Is a consumer lending license required? **Yes** — under Alaska Small Loans Act (AS 06.20)
- **Applies to**: Loans of $25,000 or less — including both consumer AND commercial loans
- **Rate caps under Small Loans Act**: 36% on balance up to $850; 24% on remainder over $850 but not exceeding $10,000; any agreed rate on remainder over $10,000 (AS 06.20.230)
- **Usury cap**: 10% per annum on loans under $25,000 unless licensed under the Small Loans Act (AS 45.45.010); loans over $25,000 exempt from contracted rate limitation
- **License**: Required from Division of Banking and Securities (DCCED); $2,500 (single location) / $10,000 (multiple location) annual fee
- **Liquid assets requirement**: $50,000; Bond: $50,000 (AS 06.20.030-050)
- **Exemptions**: Banks, credit unions, BIDCOs, insurance companies, federal financial institutions
- Source: https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#06.20

### Commercial / Contractor Lending
- Alaska Small Loans Act applies to business-purpose loans of $25,000 or less — there is no exemption for business-purpose loans
- Loans over $25,000 exempt from the contracted rate limitation
- Pending SB 39/HB 132 (2025) would add "true lender" anti-evasion provisions to the Small Loans Act and implement a 36% APR cap potentially affecting bank-fintech partnership models; passed Legislature as of May 2025, pending Governor signature
- Alaska does not currently require entities that broker, purchase, or service consumer loans to obtain a Small Loans Act license
- Anyone arranging or making loans of $25,000 or less at rates above usury requires a license
- Collection agencies require separate license under AS 08.24
- Source: https://www.akleg.gov/basis/Bill/Detail/34?Root=HB%20132

### Mortgage Lending (AS 06.60)
- Mortgage lenders, mortgage brokers, and mortgage loan originators must be licensed unless exempt
- Exemptions: bona fide nonprofits; seller-financed sellers (up to 5 properties in 12 months)
- All licensing through NMLS
- Examinations at least once every 36 months
- Source: https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#06.60

## 4. Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules (NEW)

### Escrow Statutory Framework
- **Does Alaska have a specific escrow-backed contractor advance statute?** No
- Alaska has no statute specifically governing escrow-backed advances to contractors against future milestone or insurance claim payments
- Commercial lending and general contract law apply; UCC Article 9 governs secured transactions
- Alaska escrow activities may be regulated depending on the nature of the escrow holder's activities

### UCC Article 9 Applicability
- Advances secured by accounts, contract rights, or general intangibles fall under UCC Article 9
- Perfection of security interest requires filing a UCC-1 financing statement with the Alaska Division of Corporations, Business and Professional Licensing
- Priority determined by first-to-file or first-to-perfect rules
- Source: Alaska UCC provisions under AS 45.01 et seq.

### Licensing Considerations for Escrow-Backed Advances
- **It is NOT CONFIRMED** whether an advance against future milestone payments or insurance claim proceeds constitutes "money transmission" under AS 06.55
- If the advance involves holding funds on behalf of others for disbursement upon completion of milestones, money transmission licensing may apply
- If structured as a direct loan (not escrow), the Small Loans Act applies for amounts $25,000 or less
- If structured as a purchase of receivables (true sale), may fall outside lending licensure but requires legal analysis

### Key Implications for GCSC
- **Escrow-backed advance: UNKNOWN — legal review required**
- **STATUS**: Escrow-backed contractor advances against future insurance claim proceeds or contract milestones have NOT been confirmed as permissible without licensing in Alaska
- Counsel must determine whether the advance structure constitutes:
  1. A loan requiring Small Loans Act licensing (if $25,000 or less)
  2. Money transmission requiring licensing under AS 06.55
  3. A regulated escrow activity requiring registration
  4. A commercial factoring/purchase of receivables exempt from lending licensure
- **All escrow-backed advance operations: BLOCKED pending Alaska-specific legal review**

## 5. Token Collateral / Digital Asset Risk

### Money Transmitter Risk
- Does the state regulate virtual currency transmission? **Yes** — explicitly since January 1, 2023
- Alaska Uniform Money Services Act (AS 06.55): "monetary value" defined as "a medium of exchange, whether or not redeemable in money"
- Money transmission defined as "selling or issuing payment instruments or stored value, or receiving money or monetary value for transmission" (AS 06.55.990)
- **A person may not engage in the business of money transmission unless licensed** (AS 06.55.101)
- 3 AAC 13.005: Virtual currency activity or transaction covered as money transmission (effective 1/1/2023)
- 3 AAC 13.006: Virtual currency money transmitters must submit licensing application
- License required through Division of Banking and Securities (DCCED)
- Limited Licensing Agreement (LLA) requirement was eliminated as of January 1, 2023
- Source: http://www.akleg.gov/basis/aac.asp#3.13

### Digital Asset / Token Rules
- Virtual currency defined as "a digital representation of value that is used as a medium of exchange, unit of account, or store of value; and is not money, whether or not denominated in money" (3 AAC 13.990)
- Alaska enacted RUFADAA (AS 13.63, effective October 31, 2017) providing fiduciaries access to digital assets including virtual currency
- HB 99/SB 86 (2025) — Uniform Money Transmission Modernization Act: would modernize statutes to directly include cryptocurrency; adopts CSBS Model Law; passed Senate, carried to 2026 session; would streamline licensing but NOT eliminate requirements
- Source: https://www.akleg.gov/basis/Bill/Detail/34?Root=HB%2099

### Collateral / Liquidation Risk
- Accepting virtual currency as collateral for a loan may constitute "receiving monetary value for transmission" or "stored value"
- Liquidation of token collateral likely requires money transmission license
- Holding virtual currency on behalf of others as collateral may require money transmission license
- Smart contract automation does not eliminate licensing requirements
- **All token collateral operations blocked pending money transmission analysis**
- Source: https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/dbs

## 6. Insurance Claim Advance / ClaimBridge Risk

### Assignment of Benefits (AOB)
- Is AOB allowed, restricted, or prohibited? **Not prohibited but no statutory authorization for property claims**
- Alaska's Assignment of Benefits statutes (AS 21.54.020) are limited to **health insurance only**
- No statute requiring insurers to honor AOBs for property/casualty claims; no statute prohibiting them
- Assignment of post-loss claim proceeds generally permitted under common law
- Assignment governed by: (1) insurance policy terms, and (2) common law
- Risk that insurer rejects AOB and pays insured directly
- Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-21-insurance/ak-st-sect-21-54-020/

### Public Adjuster Restrictions
- Alaska does not license public adjusters
- Division of Insurance explicitly warns that public adjusting "may constitute the practice of law"
- **Christiansen v. Melinda, 857 P.2d 345 (Alaska 1993)**: addressed definition of "practice of law" — relevant for any entity negotiating claims on behalf of homeowners
- Independent adjusters (who work for insurers) are licensed under AS 21.27.830; must pass Alaska exam or hold reciprocal license; 24 hours CE every 2 years
- Only licensed attorneys may represent homeowners in claim negotiations without risk
- GCSC and contractors must not negotiate insurance claims on behalf of homeowners
- **Public adjuster license**: Not required in Alaska, but activities may constitute unauthorized practice of law
- Source: https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/ins/Licensees/LicensesWithSpecialRequirements

### Insurance Claim Proceeds
- Alaska Administrative Code (3 AAC 26): insurer must complete investigation within 30 working days of claim notification (3 AAC 26.050)
- Within 10 working days: written acknowledgement identifying claims handler (3 AAC 26.040(a)(1))
- Undisputed portions must be paid within 30 days of receipt of properly executed proof of loss (3 AAC 26.070(2))
- Standard loss draft procedures: mortgagee named as co-payee on claim checks
- No Alaska-specific statute found governing loss draft procedures or mortgagee check endorsement
- Any claim advance product would likely be characterized as a loan requiring Small Loans Act compliance if $25,000 or less
- **AS 21.36.125(b)**: "The provisions of this section do not create or imply a private cause of action for a violation of this section." (Added 2025 via SB 177)
- Source: https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/ins
