## 2. Contractor / Home Improvement Rules

### Contractor Licensing

- **Is a state general contractor license required?** **No** — Wyoming does NOT issue a general contractor license at the state level.
- **State-level exception**: Electrical contractors ARE licensed at the state level through the Wyoming Department of Fire Prevention & Electrical Safety. Every electrical contractor must have a Wyoming-licensed master electrician as master of record.
- **All other contracting**: Licensing is handled exclusively at the **municipal/county level**. Major municipalities with contractor licensing include:
  - City of Cheyenne (Class A, B, C, D, R licenses)
  - City of Casper (Class 1, 2A, 2B, 3, 4 licenses)
  - City of Gillette
  - City of Green River
  - Town of Jackson
  - City of Douglas
  - Town of Wheatland
- **Multi-jurisdictional work**: Contractors working in multiple cities/counties may need individual licenses for each jurisdiction.
- **Business requirements**: Most Wyoming businesses must obtain a business license from the Attorney General's Office and register with the Secretary of State.

### Home Improvement Contract Rules
- No specific Wyoming home improvement contract statute with uniform disclosure requirements found.
- Standard contract law applies; written contracts recommended.
- Wyoming Consumer Protection Act (W.S. 40-12-101 et seq.) provides broad protections against unfair/deceptive trade practices.
- Municipalities may impose additional requirements including bonding and insurance.

### Implications for GCSC
- GCSC does **not** need to verify state-level contractor licenses for general contractors in Wyoming, but **MUST** verify local municipal licenses where applicable.
- GCSC should require proof of municipal contractor license and general liability insurance before facilitating financing.
- Business-purpose loans to contractors (working capital, equipment financing) are generally **NOT** subject to Wyoming's Consumer Credit Code if truly for business purposes and not "consumer credit" as defined in W.S. 40-14-104.

**Key sources**: Wyoming Secretary of State; Wyoming Department of Fire Prevention & Electrical Safety; respective municipal code websites for Cheyenne, Casper, Gillette, Jackson.

## 3. Lending / Credit Rules

### Consumer / Homeowner Lending
- **Is a consumer lending license required?** **Yes** — under the Wyoming Uniform Consumer Credit Code (W.S. 40-14-101 et seq.).
- **Threshold**: A Consumer Lender License from the Wyoming Division of Banking is required if annually your company extends consumer credit for more than **five (5) transactions secured by a dwelling** or more than **twenty-five (25) transactions not secured by a dwelling** (W.S. 40-14-304(a)).
- **Exclusions**: Loans made for purchasing/acquiring land and structures secured by a first mortgage lien are governed under the Wyoming Residential Mortgage Practices Act (W.S. 40-23-101 et seq.), not the Consumer Credit Code.
- **Fees**: $300 application fee + $50 license fee + $15 credit report fee + $36.25 background check fee. Renewal: $50 annually. Licenses expire December 1 each year.
- **Foreign qualification**: Required for business entities. Registered agent required.
- **Online transactions**: A license is required for every location where business is regularly conducted, **including online transactions**.

### Sales Finance Company License
- Required for entities whose only business is purchasing retail installment sales/lease contracts originated by retail creditors, or providing financing on behalf of sellers for consumer credit sales/lease transactions.

### Post-Dated Check Casher (Payday Lending)
- Wyoming does not have a separate payday loan statute. Short-term lending must comply with general usury limits.
- **Uniform Small Loan Act** (W.S. 40-21-102): Payday loans cannot exceed $1,000 principal. A lender must be a licensed "consumer-credit provider" by the Wyoming Department of Banking.

### Collection Agency Licensing
- Wyoming licenses collection agencies through the Wyoming Collection Agency Board.
- $10,000 surety bond required. Applications filed through NMLS.

### Usury / Interest Rate Limits
- **Default rate**: 7% per annum if no agreement or provision of law for a different rate (W.S. 40-14-106(e)).
- **Written agreements**: Wyoming appears to have **no maximum interest rate** for loans agreed to in writing. W.S. 40-14-107(a) states the act "prescribes maximum charges for all creditors" but W.S. 40-14-121 excludes extensions of credit to government, credit sales, and agricultural loans from usury limits.
- **Judgment interest**: 10% per year from date of rendition until paid (W.S. 1-16-102(a)).
- **Note**: Wyoming's liberal interest rate policy for written agreements provides significant flexibility, but federal usury and unfair lending laws still apply.

### Exemptions from Consumer Credit Code
- Extensions of credit to government/governmental agencies (W.S. 40-14-121(a)(i))
- Credit sales; loans or leases for agricultural purposes (W.S. 40-14-121(a)(ii))
- Sale of insurance by an insurer if premium is not financed (W.S. 40-14-121(a)(ii))

### Commercial / Contractor Lending
- Loans to LLCs, corporations, or other business entities for business purposes typically fall outside the Consumer Credit Code.
- However, if a loan to a contractor is effectively for residential repair work and structured as a consumer transaction, the Code may apply.
- A "consumer credit sale" or "consumer loan" is one in which credit is extended to a "consumer" (natural person) primarily for personal, family, or household purposes.

### Fintech Sandbox
- Wyoming has an active **Financial Technology Sandbox** (W.S. 40-29-101 et seq., created by HB 57 in 2019).
- The sandbox allows waiver of specified statutes/rules for innovative financial products/services.
- Covers consumer credit, money transmission, and other financial services.
- **24-month sandbox period** with possible 12-month extension.
- Applicant must be a domestic Wyoming entity with physical presence.
- $500 application fee + consumer protection bond required.
- Participants deemed to possess appropriate state license for federal law purposes.

**Key sources**: Wyoming Division of Banking — https://wyomingbankingdivision.wyo.gov/consumer-lending/license-and-registration; Wyoming Statutes Title 40.

## 4. Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules (NEW)

### Escrow Statutory Framework
- **Does Wyoming have a specific escrow-backed contractor advance statute?** No.
- Wyoming has no statute specifically governing escrow-backed advances to contractors against future milestone or insurance claim payments.
- Commercial lending and general contract law apply; UCC Article 9 governs secured transactions.

### UCC Article 9 Applicability
- Advances secured by accounts, contract rights, or general intangibles fall under UCC Article 9.
- Perfection of security interest requires filing a UCC-1 financing statement with the Wyoming Secretary of State.
- Priority determined by first-to-file or first-to-perfect rules.
- Wyoming's Digital Assets Act (W.S. 34-29-101) explicitly recognizes digital assets as intangible personal property, enabling UCC Article 9 perfection through control/possession.

### Licensing Considerations for Escrow-Backed Advances
- **It is NOT CONFIRMED** whether an advance against future milestone payments or insurance claim proceeds constitutes "money transmission" under W.S. 40-22-101 et seq.
- Virtual currency activities are explicitly exempt from Wyoming money transmitter licensing (W.S. 40-22-104(a)(vi)), but fiat-currency escrow activities may still require analysis.
- If the advance involves holding fiat funds on behalf of others for disbursement upon completion of milestones, money transmission licensing may apply.
- If structured as a direct loan (not escrow), the Consumer Credit Code applies if consumer-purpose and thresholds are met.
- If structured as a purchase of receivables (true sale/factoring), may fall outside lending licensure but requires legal analysis.

### Fintech Sandbox Alternative Pathway
- Wyoming's Fintech Sandbox (W.S. 40-29-101 et seq.) may provide a viable pathway for escrow-backed contractor advance products.
- The sandbox allows waiver of specified statutes/rules for innovative financial products/services.
- 24-month participation period with possible 12-month extension.
- Participants deemed to possess appropriate state license for federal law purposes.

### Key Implications for GCSC
- **Escrow-backed advance: UNKNOWN — legal review required**
- **STATUS**: Escrow-backed contractor advances against future insurance claim proceeds or contract milestones have NOT been confirmed as permissible without licensing in Wyoming
- Counsel must determine whether the advance structure constitutes:
  1. A loan requiring Consumer Lender License (if consumer-purpose and thresholds met)
  2. Money transmission requiring licensing under W.S. 40-22-101 (if fiat currency involved)
  3. A regulated escrow activity requiring registration
  4. A commercial factoring/purchase of receivables exempt from lending licensure
  5. An activity eligible for the Fintech Sandbox waiver
- **All escrow-backed advance operations: BLOCKED pending Wyoming-specific legal review**

## 5. Token Collateral / Digital Asset Risk

### CRITICAL: Wyoming is the Most Crypto-Friendly State in the United States

Wyoming has enacted the most comprehensive blockchain and cryptocurrency legislation of any US state. The following statutes directly impact GCSC's ability to use token collateral:

#### 1. Wyoming Utility Token Act (HB 70, 2018) — W.S. 17-4-206
- **Open blockchain tokens** ("utility tokens") with consumptive purposes are **exempt from Wyoming securities laws** (W.S. 17-4-301 through 17-4-412) and the **Wyoming Money Transmitter Act** (W.S. 40-22-104(a)(vii)).
- To qualify: token purpose must be consumptive (exchangeable for goods, services, or content), and developer/seller must file a notice of intent with the Secretary of State.
- Persons who facilitate exchange of open blockchain tokens are exempt from broker-dealer registration.
- **Implication for GCSC**: A properly structured GCSC utility token may be exempt from Wyoming securities and money transmission laws.

#### 2. Wyoming Digital Assets Act (SF 125, 2019) — W.S. 34-29-101 et seq.
- Defines three categories of digital assets:
  - **Digital Consumer Asset**: Intangible personal property, general intangible under UCC Article 9
  - **Virtual Currency**: Treated as money under UCC Article 9
  - **Digital Security**: Treated as securities/investment property under UCC Articles 8 and 9
- **Perfection of security interest**: Can be achieved through **control** (possession) of a digital asset, giving priority over non-control security interests.
- Smart contracts may be used by secured parties to achieve control or possession.
- Wyoming courts have **jurisdiction** over claims relating to digital assets.
- Banks may opt in to provide **custodial services** for digital assets.

#### 3. Wyoming Money Transmitter Act Exemption (HB 19, 2018) — W.S. 40-22-104(a)(vi)
- Explicitly exempts: "Buying, selling, issuing, or taking custody of payment instruments in the form of **virtual currency** or receiving **virtual currency** for transmission to a location within or outside the United States by any means."
- **No money transmitter license is required** for pure virtual currency activities in Wyoming.
- **CRITICAL LIMITATION**: If fiat currency is involved (crypto-fiat exchange, fiat wallets), a money transmitter license may be required.

#### 4. Wyoming DAO LLC Act (HB 38, 2021) — W.S. 17-31-101 et seq.
- DAOs may register as **DAO LLCs** in Wyoming.
- Smart contracts are explicitly recognized as valid governance mechanisms.
- Members have limited liability.
- Smart contracts used by DAOs must be capable of being updated/modified.
- **Implication**: GCSC could potentially structure its governance as a Wyoming DAO LLC.

#### 5. Wyoming SPDI (Special Purpose Depository Institution) — HB 74 (2019)
- Wyoming charters **SPDI banks** specifically designed for digital asset custody.
- SPDIs can custody digital assets, provide payment services, and maintain reserves.
- Kraken Bank was the first SPDI charter recipient.
- SPDIs are not FDIC-insured but are regulated by the Wyoming Division of Banking.

#### 6. Smart Contract Recognition
- Wyoming recognizes **smart contracts** as valid automated transactions (W.S. 40-21-102(a)(ii) — Uniform Electronic Transactions Act).
- The DAO LLC Act explicitly defines smart contracts and authorizes their use for governance, asset transfers, and membership votes (W.S. 17-31-102(a)(viii)).
- Digital Asset Act authorizes use of smart contracts for perfecting security interests in digital assets.

#### 7. Wyoming Stable Token Commission (2024-2025)
- Wyoming is actively developing a **state stablecoin** (Frontier Stable Token).
- This demonstrates the state's continued commitment to blockchain innovation.

### TOKEN COLLATERAL ASSESSMENT — LOWEST RISK IN THE US
- **Wyoming law is HIGHLY FAVORABLE** to using digital assets as collateral.
- Security interests in digital assets can be perfected by possession/control under UCC Article 9.
- Token lock/liquidation mechanisms may be enforceable through smart contracts.
- Virtual currency activities require NO money transmitter license in Wyoming.
- Utility tokens may be exempt from securities laws with proper notice filing.
- **However**, **federal securities law** and the laws of **other states** where borrowers may be located still apply.
- **Primary risk vector is federal law and multi-state compliance, NOT Wyoming state law.**

**Key sources**: Wyoming Secretary of State — Securities Division; Wyoming Division of Banking; W.S. 17-4-206; W.S. 34-29-101 et seq.; W.S. 40-22-104(a)(vi); W.S. 17-31-101 et seq.

## 6. Insurance Claim Advance / ClaimBridge Risk

### Claims Handling Timeline
- W.S. 26-15-124(b): Property and casualty insurers must **reject or accept claims for benefits and pay the same within 45 days** after receipt of the claim and supporting bills.
- W.S. 26-13-124 (Unfair Claims Settlement Practices): Prohibits failing to acknowledge/act reasonably promptly upon communications, failing to adopt reasonable standards for prompt investigation, refusing to pay without reasonable investigation, not attempting in good faith to effectuate prompt/fair/equitable settlements, and compelling insureds to institute litigation.

### Emergency Advance Payments
- **No specific Wyoming statute was found** requiring insurers to make emergency advance payments on property/casualty claims.
- Additional Living Expenses (ALE) coverage is a standard homeowners policy feature, but Wyoming does not appear to mandate emergency advance payments by statute.
- However, unfair claim settlement practice rules (W.S. 26-13-124) require insurers to act in good faith and promptly settle claims where liability is reasonably clear.

### Assignment of Benefits (AOB)
- **Wyoming does NOT have a specific Assignment of Benefits statute** for property/casualty insurance claims.
- No required AOB notice, cancellation window, font-size/signature rules, or contractor limits were found in Wyoming statutes.
- The general rule in Wyoming (consistent with majority rule) is that policy anti-assignment clauses are enforceable pre-loss but do not necessarily prevent post-loss assignments.
- **Implication**: AOBs may be legally permissible in Wyoming under general contract law, but there is no statutory framework providing specific protections, procedures, or restrictions.
- **CRITICAL**: Without a specific AOB statute, GCSC must rely on general contract and insurance law principles. Counsel should review any AOB arrangement.

### Loss Draft / Mortgagee Checks
- **Wyoming does not appear to have a specific state statute** governing loss draft/mortgagee check procedures for property insurance claims.
- Standard mortgage clause protections apply: mortgagees named on the policy receive independent contract protections.
- Mortgage servicer guidelines (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, industry standard) govern loss draft disbursement procedures:
  - Claims $40,000 or less: Typically released to borrower in one payment if loan is current
  - Claims over $40,000: Monitored disbursement in increments as repairs progress
  - Delinquent loans: All funds held in escrow regardless of amount

### Insurance Claim Proceeds Assignment
- Wyoming has **no specific statute** addressing assignment of property/casualty insurance claim proceeds to contractors or third parties.
- W.S. 26-15-122 addresses assignment of **life insurance policies** only: "A policy is assignable or not assignable as provided by its terms."
- General contract law principles apply to assignment of claim proceeds.
- **Anti-assignment clauses** in insurance policies are generally enforceable **before a loss** but may not prevent post-loss assignments under Wyoming's majority-rule interpretation (consistent with Couch on Insurance).

### Public Adjuster Licensing
- **License Required**: Yes, public adjusters must be licensed under W.S. 26-9-219.
- **Regulator**: Wyoming Department of Insurance.
- **License Fee**: $100 original license; $100 biennial renewal for residents; $150 biennial renewal for nonresidents.
- **Examination**: Yes, required (with exemptions allowed).
- **Continuing Education**: 24 hours biennially, including at least 3 hours of ethics.
- **Recordkeeping**: Records must be retained for 3 years. Bond not required.
- **Written Contract**: Public adjuster contracts in Wyoming must be in writing and include compensation terms, required disclosures, scope of services, and cancellation rights.

### Who May Negotiate with Insurance Companies
- **Public adjusters** may negotiate with insurance companies on behalf of insureds.
- **Licensed attorneys** are exempt from adjuster licensing requirements.
- **Contractors**: Wyoming law does not explicitly prohibit contractors from assisting insureds with claim documentation. However, acting as a "public adjuster" (for compensation, investigating and negotiating settlement of claims on behalf of the insured) without a license would likely violate W.S. 26-9-219.
- **CRITICAL**: GCSC and its contractor partners MUST NOT negotiate claims settlements with insurance companies on behalf of homeowners unless licensed as public adjusters. GCSC can facilitate documentation and communication but must avoid unauthorized practice of public adjusting.

### Mortgagee Rights
- Standard mortgage clause protections apply in Wyoming. Mortgagees named on the policy have independent contractual rights against the insurer.
- Borrowers cannot unilaterally direct insurance companies to exclude the mortgagee from loss payments.
- **Implication for GCSC**: Any assignment of claim proceeds must account for mortgagee rights. The mortgage servicer's consent or cooperation may be required to direct claim payments to a contractor or GCSC.

**Key sources**: Wyoming Department of Insurance — https://doi.wyo.gov/; Wyoming Statutes Title 26.
