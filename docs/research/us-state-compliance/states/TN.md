# SmartContractor State Compliance: Tennessee (TN)

---

## 1. State Summary

Tennessee is a **blockchain-friendly jurisdiction** with a **mixed regulatory environment** for SmartContractor products. The state enacted landmark legislation in 2018 recognizing blockchain technology and smart contracts as legally valid, and its financial regulator has taken a permissive stance toward virtual currency activity. At the same time, Tennessee maintains an active consumer lending regime, contractor licensing requirements, and insurance claim regulations that require careful navigation.

**Key regulators:**
- **Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance (TDCI)** — Insurance regulation, public adjuster licensing, and the Board for Licensing Contractors (tn.gov/commerce)
- **Tennessee Department of Financial Institutions (TDFI)** — Consumer lending, money transmission, and escrow oversight

**Lending environment:** The Tennessee Industrial Loan and Thrift (TILT) Act provides a licensure path for non-bank consumer lending up to 24% APR, with registration required through NMLS. General usury limits are 10% for unwritten contracts and approximately 10.75% (formula rate) for written contracts.

**Contractor licensing:** The Board for Licensing Contractors, within TDCI, requires a state license for construction projects valued at $25,000 or more (including materials, equipment, and labor). Home improvement licenses are additionally required in certain counties for projects between $3,000 and $25,000.

**Insurance claims:** Tennessee has no statutory Assignment of Benefits (AOB) law for property/casualty claims — the healthcare-only statute (Tenn. Code Ann. § 56-7-120) does not apply to contractors. Post-loss assignments of property insurance claims are governed by **common law**, where Tennessee courts routinely uphold such assignments even against policy anti-assignment clauses. Public adjuster licensing is required for claim negotiation.

**Blockchain/digital assets:** Tennessee is among the most crypto-friendly U.S. states. SB 1662 (2018) makes smart contracts legally valid and enforceable. TDFI has confirmed that virtual currency is **not** "money" under the Tennessee Money Transmitter Act (Tenn. Code Ann. § 45-7-101 et seq.), meaning pure cryptocurrency transfers do not require money transmission licensing.

**All SmartContractor live product features remain BLOCKED pending legal review.** Tennessee's favorable blockchain framework supports robust demo/sandbox activity.

## 3. Lending / Finance Licensing Notes

### Industrial Loan and Thrift (TILT) Companies
- **Statute**: Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 45-5-101 to 45-5-612
- **Regulator**: Tennessee Department of Financial Institutions (TDFI)
- **Registration Required**: Yes, through NMLS. A separate certificate is needed for each business office.
- **Net Worth Minimum**: $25,000 per office location (tangible net worth)
- **Maximum Interest Rate**:
  - Loans under $100: 7.5% per annum discount interest, capped at 18% actuarial
  - Loans of $100+: 24% actuarial
  - Open-end credit plans: 24% actuarial
- **Loan Fees Allowed**: 4% of loan amount or flat $10 service charge (less if under $100); for open-end/real property secured: actual bona fide third-party expenses (title exam, title insurance, surveys, doc prep, credit reports, appraisals); recording fees or non-filing insurance
- **Exemptions**: Banks (other than industrial banks), savings and loan associations, credit unions, insurance companies, other persons supervised by a state/federal agency, licensed pawnbrokers (§ 45-5-104)
- **Who Must Register**: Any person engaged in the business of making loans and imposing interest/charges authorized by Chapter 45-5

### General Usury Limits (Non-TILT Lenders)
- **Statute**: Tenn. Code Ann. § 47-14-103
- Written contracts: applicable formula rate (4% over weekly average prime loan rate; currently **~10.75%** per TDFI as of 2024)
- All other transactions: 10% per annum
- **Wildcard Statute** (§ 45-2-601): Tennessee state-chartered banks may charge the same rates as national banks (up to 24% based on TILT rate)

### Flexible Credit Act (Open-End Credit)
- **Statute**: Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 45-12-101 to 45-12-126
- **Outstanding principal limit**: $4,000
- **License required**: Yes

### Commercial/Business-Purpose Loans
- Tennessee does not appear to require a consumer lending license for **bona fide business-purpose loans**
- If a loan is primarily for business, commercial, or agricultural purpose, it may be exempt from consumer lending statutes
- **CRITICAL**: The determination of business purpose vs. consumer purpose requires legal analysis of each transaction
- Documentation of business purpose must be robust

### Loan Brokering/Referral
- Persons who arrange loans between borrowers and licensed lenders may need to register or be licensed depending on their role
- Pure referral arrangements (without compensation tied to loan terms) may not require licensing

### Escrow Regulation
- The Tennessee Department of Financial Institutions may regulate escrow activities depending on the nature of the arrangement
- Escrow agents holding funds for real property transactions may be subject to TDFI oversight
- **CRITICAL**: Any SmartContractor escrow or custodial function involving client funds requires TDFI compliance analysis

## 4. Tennessee Blockchain & Digital Asset Regulatory Framework *(NEW)*

Tennessee has established one of the most comprehensive and favorable state-level regulatory frameworks for blockchain technology and digital assets in the United States. This section details the foundational laws and regulatory guidance that create the legal infrastructure for SmartContractor's smart contract and token-based products.

### SB 1662 (2018) — Blockchain and Smart Contract Recognition

**Status**: Signed into law March 22, 2018; Public Chapter 591. Passed unanimously in both House (90-0) and Senate (29-0).

**Key Provisions:**
- Recognizes the legal authority to use **distributed ledger technology** and **smart contracts** in conducting electronic transactions
- "No contract relating to a transaction shall be denied legal effect, validity, or enforceability solely because that contract contains a smart contract term"
- Defines blockchain, distributed ledger technology, and smart contracts under Tennessee law
- Amends TCA Title 12; Title 47; Title 48; Title 61; and Title 66
- Provides that a signature secured through blockchain technology is considered an electronic signature under the Tennessee Electronic Transactions Act
- A record or contract secured through blockchain technology is considered an electronic record

**SmartContractor Implication**: Smart contracts executed on the SmartContractor platform have explicit legal recognition in Tennessee. This includes loan agreements, escrow arrangements, and claim proceeds distributions governed by smart contract logic.

### TDFI Virtual Currency Guidance (December 16, 2015)

**Key Determinations:**
- Virtual currency is **NOT** "money" under the Tennessee Money Transmitter Act (Tenn. Code Ann. § 45-7-101 et seq.)
- Receiving cryptocurrency in exchange for a promise to make it available later is **NOT** money transmission
- Transfer of cryptocurrency by itself is NOT money transmission
- Exchange of one cryptocurrency for another is NOT money transmission
- Exchange of cryptocurrency for sovereign currency **between two parties** is NOT money transmission (classified as sale of goods)
- **IS money transmission**: Exchange of cryptocurrency for sovereign currency through a **third-party exchanger** or automated machine (because sovereign currency is involved)

**SmartContractor Implication**: Pure peer-to-peer cryptocurrency transfers and token collateral operations on-chain likely fall outside money transmission requirements. However, liquidation of token collateral to fiat through a third-party exchange may trigger money transmission licensing.

### Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA) — SB 268, Effective January 1, 2024

- Tennessee adopted the CSBS Model Money Transmission Modernization Act in full
- Notably, Tennessee **excluded** the optional virtual currency provisions from the model act
- The TDFI's 2015 guidance (virtual currency is not money) appears to remain in full effect
- The MTMA modernizes definitions, licensing, and supervisory coordination but does not alter the fundamental treatment of cryptocurrency

### Digital Asset Definition
- Tennessee defines "digital asset" as "an electronic record in which an individual has a right or interest. A digital asset does not include an underlying asset or liability unless the asset or liability is itself an electronic record."

### Uniform Unclaimed Property Act
- Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-29-102: "Property" includes virtual currency for unclaimed property purposes
- Custodians of virtual currency must comply with Tennessee's unclaimed property reporting and remittance requirements

### Additional Blockchain-Related Legislation

| Bill | Year | Key Provision |
|------|------|--------------|
| SB 2508 | 2018 | Prohibits trustees of state-defined contribution plans/health benefit plans from investing in cryptocurrency |
| SB 1157 | 2019 | Captive insurance companies may receive premium payments, authorize claim payments, and hold surplus in cryptocurrency |

### Summary: Tennessee's Blockchain Regulatory Position

| Aspect | Tennessee Treatment |
|--------|-------------------|
| Smart contract legal validity | Explicitly recognized (SB 1662) |
| Blockchain signatures | Valid electronic signatures |
| Virtual currency = "money" | No (TDFI 2015 guidance) |
| Crypto-to-crypto exchange | Not money transmission |
| Peer-to-peer crypto/fiat | Not money transmission (sale of goods) |
| Third-party exchange crypto/fiat | Money transmission licensing required |
| MTMA virtual currency provisions | Excluded from adoption |
| Digital asset unclaimed property | Virtual currency is "property" |

### Key Considerations for Token Collateral Products

1. **Lending License Nexus**: If token-backed loans are offered to consumers, TILT Act registration may be required depending on interest rate and structure. Commercial/business-purpose loans may qualify for exemption.

2. **Escrow/Custody**: The Tennessee Department of Financial Institutions may regulate escrow activities. Any custodial holding of token collateral requires TDFI compliance analysis.

3. **Liquidation Risk**: Liquidation of token collateral to USD through a third-party exchange or automated machine likely constitutes money transmission under TDFI guidance. Peer-to-peer arrangements may fall outside this requirement.

4. **Securities Law**: Token collateral that constitutes a security under Tennessee's Blue Sky laws (Tenn. Code Ann. § 48-1-101 et seq.) triggers additional registration and compliance obligations.

5. **Usury Application**: Token-denominated loans converted to fiat must comply with Tennessee usury limits: 10% general; ~10.75% formula rate for written contracts; up to 24% for licensed TILT lenders.

## 6. Contractor Licensing & Financing Notes

### Board for Licensing Contractors

- **State Board**: Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors (BLC), within TDCI
- **Threshold**: State license required for projects **$25,000 or more** (includes materials, equipment, and labor)
- **Home Improvement License**: Required in certain counties (Bradley, Davidson, Hamilton, Haywood, Knox, Marion, Robertson, Rutherford, Shelby) for remodeling projects between **$3,000 and $25,000**
- **License Classifications**:
  - Building Combined (BC)
  - Residential (BC-A)
  - Limited Residential (BC-A/r)
  - Commercial (BC-B)
  - Small Commercial (BC-b(sm))
  - Industrial (BC-C)
- **Reciprocity**: Tennessee has reciprocity agreements with 9 states
- **Insurance**: General liability required, scaled to license limit; workers' compensation required for 1+ employees
- **Financial Statement**: CPA reviewed statement required (under $1.5M); CPA audited (over $1.5M)

### Contractor Financing
- No specific Tennessee statute governing "contractor financing" as a distinct product
- Equipment financing and working capital loans to licensed contractors may fall under commercial lending exemptions
- If financing is offered to the **homeowner** (consumer), consumer lending rules apply
- If financing is offered to the **contractor** (business), commercial lending rules may apply
- Contractor must hold appropriate license for the project scope and dollar amount

### Consumer Protection for Contractors
- Tenn. Code Ann. § 47-18-104(b)(35): Representing that a person is a licensed contractor when not licensed, or acting as a contractor without a license, is an unfair/deceptive act under the Tennessee Consumer Protection Act (TCPA)
- Tenn. Code Ann. § 47-18-104(b)(47): Home improvement services providers must provide written disclosure of criminal penalties for prohibited acts and their true name/address
- **Underlicensed contractor = TCPA violation** per Tennessee Court of Appeals

### Deductible Rebates
- **Tennessee does NOT appear to have a specific statute prohibiting contractors from rebating or waiving insurance deductibles** (unlike many states)
- However, Tenn. Code Ann. § 39-14-154 makes certain home improvement-related acts criminal offenses
- Any deductible rebate arrangement should be reviewed by counsel

### Cancellation Rights
- Tenn. Code § 62-6-602: Home improvement contracts may be cancelled within 3 business days after receiving written notification from the insurer that all or any part of the claim is not a covered loss

## 7. Insurance Claim Advance Notes

### Unfair Claims Settlement Practices
- **Statute**: Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 56-8-101 to 56-8-113
- **Regulation**: Tennessee Rules & Regs. 0780-01-05-.04 et seq.
- **No private right of action** for violations of the Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act (§ 56-8-113)
- Enforcement through TDCI Commissioner only

### Key Claims Handling Requirements
1. **Acknowledgment**: Within 30 days of receiving notice of claim (0780-01-05-.07(1))
2. **Coverage Decision**: Must affirm or deny coverage within a reasonable time after proof of loss completed
3. **Communication**: Must acknowledge and act upon communications reasonably promptly
4. **Claims Forms**: Must provide within 15 days of request
5. **Settlement**: Must attempt in good faith to effectuate prompt, fair, and equitable settlement where liability is reasonably clear
6. **Depreciation**: Upon request, insurer must provide claim file worksheets detailing deductions for depreciation (ACV policies)

### Bad Faith Statute
- **Statute**: Tenn. Code Ann. § 56-7-105
- Provides for penalty (up to 25% of loss amount) and attorney's fees for refusal to pay within 60 days after demand
- Private right of action exists by judicial interpretation

### Additional Living Expenses (ALE)
- ALE is standard coverage in homeowners policies, typically 20-30% of dwelling coverage
- Usually reimbursement-based; policyholder must document expenses
- After disasters, TDCI may require insurers to advance funds for temporary expenses instead of requiring receipts first

### Emergency/Advance Payments
- No specific Tennessee statute mandating emergency advance payments
- Tennessee TDCI has, after past disasters, implemented special rules requiring:
  - Advance of funds for temporary expenses
  - Extended deadlines for proofs of loss
  - Acceptance of less detailed contents inventories
- These are typically **discretionary regulatory actions** during declared emergencies, not standing statutory requirements

### Loss Draft / Mortgagee Checks
- When a mortgagee is listed on the policy, insurance claim checks typically include the mortgagee as co-payee
- Standard industry practice applies; mortgage servicers follow their own loss draft procedures
- Tennessee Housing Development Agency (VMLS) example: 3-installment monitored claim process for repairs

## 8. Assignment of Benefits Notes

### Healthcare AOB (Statutory Only)
- **Statute**: Tenn. Code Ann. § 56-7-120
- Applies **only to healthcare providers**: medicine, osteopathy, dentistry, chiropractic, podiatry, optometry, pharmacy, hospital, home health, infusion therapy, or DME
- Insured may assign benefits to healthcare provider
- Notice of assignment must be in writing to insurer to be effective
- Does **NOT** apply to property/casualty insurance or contractor payments

### Property/Casualty AOB (Common Law)
- **NO Tennessee statute specifically governs assignment of benefits for property insurance claims**
- Tennessee follows the **majority rule**: post-loss assignments are valid even if the insurance policy contains an anti-assignment clause
- Tennessee courts have "routinely ruled on behalf of contractors and purchasers who were assigned the claim after the loss occurred" (McWherter Scott & Bobbitt PLLC analysis)
- Rationale: After loss occurs, the insurer's risk cannot be increased by a change in the insured's identity

### Tennessee Post-Loss Assignment Characteristics

| Element | Status | Source |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Post-loss assignment validity | **Valid** under common law | Tennessee court decisions |
| Anti-assignment clause enforceability post-loss | Generally **NOT enforceable** | Majority rule / Couch on Insurance |
| Written requirement | Recommended but not statutorily mandated | Best practice |
| Notice to insurer | Recommended for contractor protection | Best practice |
| Cancellation window | Not statutorily defined for property AOB | Best practice: include in contract |
| Font size / formatting | Not statutorily required for property AOB | Best practice: conspicuous notice |
| Ability to negotiate claim as assignee | **UNKNOWN** — may constitute public adjusting | See Section 9 |

### Contractor as AOB Assignee
- A contractor can receive an assignment of a homeowner's post-loss insurance benefits
- The assignment allows the contractor to "stand in the shoes of the homeowner for insurance collection purposes"
- **Risk**: If the contractor negotiates the claim with the insurer, this may constitute the unauthorized practice of public adjusting (see Section 9)
- **Risk**: Tennessee courts have held that if the insurance company pays less than the contractor charges, the homeowner may be liable for the difference

### SmartContractor Implications
- Assignment of claim proceeds as a repayment mechanism is **NOT prohibited** by Tennessee statute
- However, the negotiation/adjustment of claims by a non-licensed public adjuster may be restricted
- All AOB-related activities remain **BLOCKED** pending legal review

## 9. Public Adjuster / Insurance Representation Notes

### Licensing Requirements
- **Statute**: Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 56-6-901 et seq. (Tennessee Public Adjuster Licensing Act of 2006)
- **Regulator**: TDCI, Agent Licensing Section
- **License required**: Yes. No person shall act as a public adjuster without a license (§ 56-6-903)
- **Exam**: Yes, required unless exempt
- **Bond**: $50,000 surety bond
- **Fingerprinting**: Required for resident applicants
- **E&O Insurance**: Required (amount not confirmed from official sources)
- **License fee**: $100 application; $100 amendment
- **Renewal**: Biennial

### Public Adjuster Definition
- A public adjuster adjusts losses on behalf of **policyholders (insureds)** exclusively
- Unlike company adjusters or independent adjusters who work for insurers
- Key activities: investigate, appraise, evaluate, advise, advocate, assist in adjusting claims, prepare insurance claims, negotiate with insurers

### What Contractors CAN Do Without a PA License
1. Offer repair/reconstruction services
2. Offer an opinion on whether damage is normally covered by insurance
3. Prepare an estimate and scope of work
4. Discuss the estimate with their customer
5. Recommend that the policyholder file a claim
6. Be present when the insurer's adjuster inspects damage
7. Answer questions about their estimates

### What Contractors CANNOT Do Without a PA License
1. Investigate, appraise, evaluate, give advice, advocate or assist in adjusting a claim
2. Prepare the insurance claim for the customer
3. **Negotiate the claim with the insurance company on the customer's behalf**
4. Review the insurance policy or advise on coverage
5. Advertise as "claim specialists," "claim analysts," or that they can "deal with insurance companies"

### Conflict of Interest / Financial Interest
- **Statute**: Tenn. Code Ann. § 56-6-917(d)
- "A public adjuster shall not have a direct or indirect financial interest in any aspect of the claim, other than the salary, fee, commission or other compensation established in the written contract with the insured."
- Tennessee does **NOT** appear to have an explicit statute prohibiting a public adjuster from also being a contractor on the same claim (unlike Florida's § 626.8795)
- However, the financial interest prohibition would likely apply to prevent dual-role arrangements

### Unauthorized Practice of Public Adjusting (UPPA)
- Tennessee law prohibits acting as a public adjuster without a license (§ 56-6-903)
- Penalties for violation may include fines and regulatory action
- **CRITICAL**: SmartContractor and its contractor partners must NOT negotiate claims with insurers on behalf of homeowners

### SmartContractor Implications
- SmartContractor and contractors on the platform **must not** negotiate insurance claims, advise on coverage, or prepare claims for homeowners
- Any claim assistance must be limited to the "can do" items listed above
- Public adjuster licensing should be considered if SmartContractor intends to offer claim negotiation services
- All claim-related activities remain **BLOCKED** pending legal review
