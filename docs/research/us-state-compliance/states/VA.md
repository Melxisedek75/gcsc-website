# Virginia (VA) State Compliance File

> **SmartContractor Compliance Classification:** HIGH REGULATION (AOB-Restricted) | **Last Updated:** 2025
>
> Virginia's 2025 prohibition on assignment of fire insurance claims (Va. Code § 38.2-2131) renders AOB-dependent products **BLOCKED** for fire/wind/hail claims. The state maintains a 36% APR cap on consumer loans, robust contractor licensing via DPOR, and a crypto-friendly posture (virtual currency excluded from money transmission effective July 1, 2026).

Virginia presents a **HIGH REGULATION** environment for SmartContractor products. However, Virginia's deliberate exclusion of virtual currency from money transmission regulation creates favorable conditions for token-collateral products pending lending law analysis. Contractor financing to properly licensed business entities may be viable with strict business-purpose structuring.

## 2. Lending & Consumer Finance Licensing

### 2.1 Consumer Finance Act (Va. Code §§ 6.2-1500 et seq.)

**License Required.** No person shall engage in the business of making loans to individuals for personal, family, household, or other nonbusiness purposes without obtaining a consumer finance license from the SCC Bureau of Financial Institutions (§ 6.2-1501(A)).

**Key Parameters:**

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Interest Rate Cap | **36% APR maximum** (effective January 1, 2021, per SB421/HB789) |
| Usury Threshold | Unlicensed lenders charging >12% APR violate usury law (§ 6.2-303) |
| Loan Amount Range | $300 to $35,000 |
| Loan Term | 6 to 120 months; substantially equal installments required |
| Minimum Liquid Assets | $25,000-$50,000 depending on locality population (§ 6.2-1507) |
| Bond | Required (§ 6.2-1507) |
| Out-of-State Lenders | Permitted to obtain license regardless of physical presence (2021 reform) |
| Access Partners | Licensees may use "access partners" (§ 6.2-1501) |

**Penalties for Noncompliance:**

- **Class 2 misdemeanor** for violation of § 6.2-1501 (§ 6.2-1540)
- **Unlicensed loans exceeding 12% APR are null and void**; lender cannot collect principal, interest, or charges (§ 6.2-1541)

### 2.2 Short-Term Loans (Va. Code §§ 6.2-1800 et seq.)

- 36% APR cap; maximum $2,500; 4-24 month terms

### 2.3 Motor Vehicle Title Loans (Va. Code §§ 6.2-2200 et seq.)

- 36% APR cap; maximum $2,500; 6-24 month terms; database required

### 2.4 Open-End Credit Plans (Va. Code § 6.2-312)

- 36% APR cap; $50 annual participation fee allowed

### 2.5 Commercial / Business-Purpose Lending

The Consumer Finance Act applies only to loans to **individuals** for "personal, family, household, or other nonbusiness purposes."

- **Business-purpose loans** to corporations, LLCs, or for commercial purposes likely fall **outside** the Consumer Finance Act licensing requirement
- Virginia does **not** have a general commercial lender licensing statute
- **Mortgage broker licensing** required for brokering loans secured by residential real property (1-4 family), even for business purpose, based on Virginia's broad definition of "residential property"

> **⚠️ KEY RISK:** Any loan product offered to an **individual** (including sole proprietor contractor) for non-business purposes exceeding 12% APR requires a consumer finance license. All SmartContractor loan products must be structured as **business-purpose only** and require Virginia-licensed counsel review.

## 3. Contractor Licensing & Recovery Fund

### 3.1 DPOR Contractor Licensing (Va. Code §§ 54.1-1100 et seq.; 18VAC50-22)

**State-level licensing is required** for all contractors performing work over $1,000. Licenses are issued to **business entities**, not individuals. Each firm must designate a Qualified Individual (QI).

| Class | Contract Value | Annual Total | Experience | Net Worth | Exam |
|-------|---------------|--------------|------------|-----------|------|
| **Class A** | $150,000+ per contract | $1,000,000+ | 5 years | $45,000 | Required |
| **Class B** | $30,000-$150,000 per contract | $250,000-$1,000,000 | 3 years | $15,000 | Required |
| **Class C** | $1,000-$30,000 per contract | Under $250,000 | 2 years | None | Not required |

**Additional Requirements:**
- 8-hour pre-license education course required for all classes
- Reciprocity: North Carolina (RBC/CBC), Ohio (business/law exams)

### 3.2 Residential Contract Requirements

Written contract required for all residential work, including:
- Scope of work
- Itemized pricing
- Payment schedule
- Estimated start/completion dates
- License number

### 3.3 Virginia Contractor Transaction Recovery Fund

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Purpose** | Relief for consumers suffering losses from improper/dishonest conduct of licensed residential contractors |
| **Per-Claim Limit** | $20,000-$30,000 (sources vary) |
| **Aggregate Limit** | $40,000-$100,000 per contractor per biennium (sources vary) |
| **Eligibility** | Licensed residential contractors only; consumer must obtain court judgment and exhaust collection efforts first |
| **Required Disclosure** | All residential contracts must include Recovery Fund notice and DPOR contact information |
| **Funding** | Licensee assessments (not tax revenues) |

### 3.4 Penalties for Unlicensed Contracting

| Offense | Penalty |
|---------|---------|
| First/Second offense | Class 1 misdemeanor |
| Third offense within 36 months | **Class 6 felony** |
| Civil penalties | $200-$5,000 per violation; up to $25,000 per year |
| State of emergency violations | Additional $10,000 per violation |

### 3.5 Implications for SmartContractor

- SmartContractor can work with licensed contractors in Virginia
- Equipment credit/financing to **business entities** (corporations, LLCs) for business purposes likely does **NOT** require consumer finance license
- Financing to **individuals** (sole proprietors) requires consumer finance license if over 12% APR
- All contractor financing products must include required Recovery Fund disclosures and comply with the Virginia Consumer Protection Act

## 4. Money Transmitter & Escrow Regulation

> **NEW SECTION — This section consolidates Virginia's money transmission framework under the MTMA and addresses escrow oversight authority relevant to SmartContractor claim proceeds handling.**

### 4.1 Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA) Adoption (Va. Code §§ 6.2-1900 et seq.)

Virginia adopted the Uniform Money Transmission Modernization Act (HB1942, 2025 session), effective **July 1, 2026**. The MTMA replaces Virginia's prior money transmitter framework and aligns the state with the CSBS national model.

**Key Provisions:**
- **License required** for engaging in the business of money transmission
- **Exemptions** include: federally insured depository institutions, payment processing agents, and certain payroll processors
- Virginia's adoption follows the CSBS MTMA model with Virginia-specific modifications

### 4.2 Virtual Currency Exclusion (Crypto-Friendly Framework)

**Critical Finding:** Virginia **explicitly excluded "virtual currency" from the definition of "money"** in its money transmitter statute. This is a deliberate legislative choice to exempt virtual currency transmission from licensing requirements.

- **Statutory definition:** "Virtual currency" means a digital representation of value used as a medium of exchange, unit of account, or store of value that is **not money**, whether or not denominated in money (§ 6.2-1900)
- **Exclusions from definition:** Affinity/rewards program value not exchangeable for money; digital representations used solely within online games
- **Legislative intent:** The exclusion signals a crypto-friendly policy posture—Virginia deliberately declined to regulate virtual currency transmission

**Source:** FinTech Law Blog analysis (May 2025): "Virginia also excluded 'virtual currency' from the definition of money, indicating a legislative intent to exempt virtual currency from the law."

### 4.3 Bank Custody of Virtual Currency (HB263, effective July 1, 2022)

- Virginia-chartered banks are **permitted to provide virtual currency custody services**
- Banks must maintain adequate risk management protocols, insurance, and service provider oversight
- Banks may act in fiduciary or non-fiduciary capacity
- This was the first Virginia statute to define "virtual currency"

### 4.4 Escrow Oversight

The **Virginia Bureau of Financial Institutions** has authority to regulate escrow activities in the Commonwealth:

- Entities holding funds in escrow, particularly in connection with real property transactions, may be subject to Bureau oversight
- **Independent escrow agents** may require licensing or registration depending on transaction structure
- **Title companies** and attorneys customarily handle escrow in Virginia real estate transactions
- **Implications for SmartContractor:** Any product mechanism that holds insurance claim proceeds, contractor payments, or token collateral in escrow should be reviewed for potential Bureau of Financial Institutions registration requirements

### 4.5 Status for SmartContractor

| Activity | Status | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Virtual currency transmission | **EXEMPT** | Deliberately excluded from MTMA; no license required |
| Token collateral lock/liquidation | LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED | Favorable state environment; lending/collateral issues remain |
| Escrow of claim proceeds | LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED | Bureau of Financial Institutions may assert jurisdiction |

## 5. Token Collateral & Cryptocurrency

### 5.1 Virginia's Crypto-Friendly Posture

Virginia has emerged as one of the more crypto-friendly U.S. states, deliberately excluding virtual currency from money transmission regulation and permitting state-chartered banks to custody digital assets. This creates a favorable **state-level** environment for token-collateral products.

### 5.2 Token Collateral for Equipment Financing

While Virginia does not regulate virtual currency **transmission**, the use of virtual currency as **collateral for a loan** raises separate analytical questions:

1. **Is the token collateral arrangement a "loan" subject to the Consumer Finance Act?**
   - Depends on borrower type (individual vs. business entity) and loan purpose
   - Business-purpose loans to LLCs/corporations likely outside CFA scope

2. **Does the liquidation mechanism constitute money transmission when collateral is sold?**
   - Virtual currency exclusion from MTMA likely covers liquidation events
   - Federal analysis still required

3. **Does smart contract-based lock/liquidation create novel regulatory issues?**
   - No Virginia state-level digital asset lending statute has been identified
   - UCC Article 9 (secured transactions) applies
   - Federal securities, commodities, and banking laws apply regardless of Virginia's friendly stance

### 5.3 Practical Status

| Product | Status | Conditions |
|---------|--------|------------|
| Token collateral for **consumer loans** | **BLOCKED** | Pending legal review |
| Token collateral for **commercial/business equipment financing** | LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED | Must confirm business-purpose only; 36% APR cap if deemed consumer loan |
| Token collateral lock | LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED | Virginia exclusion of virtual currency from MTMA is favorable |
| Token liquidation | LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED | Smart contract mechanism requires counsel review under Virginia commercial law |

### 5.4 Required Disclosures for Token Collateral

All token-collateral products must include the **Virtual Currency Risk Disclosure** (see Section 9) and a **Business Purpose Certification** for commercial transactions.

## 6. Insurance Claims, AOB & Loss Draft

### 6.1 Additional Living Expenses (ALE) / Loss of Use

- ALE coverage is standard in Virginia homeowners policies (Coverage D)
- Covers: temporary housing, excess food costs, laundry, pet boarding, transportation, storage
- Does NOT cover: mortgage payments, normal utilities, regular expenses
- Limits: Typically 20-30% of dwelling coverage; may have time limits (often 12 months)
- Generally paid on **reimbursement basis** after proof of expenses

### 6.2 Unfair Claim Settlement Practices (Va. Code § 38.2-510)

Insurers must:
- Acknowledge claims promptly
- Adopt reasonable investigation standards
- Not refuse claims arbitrarily
- Attempt good-faith settlement where liability is clear
- Provide reasonable explanation for denials

> **Important:** This statute does **NOT** create a private cause of action; enforced exclusively by the SCC Bureau of Insurance. Violations must occur "with such frequency as to indicate a general business practice" for Commission action.

### 6.3 Loss Draft / Mortgagee Claims

- When a mortgage exists, the mortgagee is named on the policy via standard mortgage clause
- Claim checks for significant damage are co-payable to insured and mortgage servicer
- Mortgage servicer holds funds in escrow and releases incrementally based on repair progress
- Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac guidelines govern servicer disbursement
- **SmartContractor cannot directly control repayment routing from insurance proceeds held by mortgage servicers**

### 6.4 CRITICAL: Assignment of Benefits Prohibition

**STATUS: PROHIBITED for Fire Insurance Claims (2025)**

This is the **single most critical barrier** to SmartContractor operations in Virginia.

**Statutory Text (Va. Code § 38.2-2131, enacted 2025):**

> "A. Consistent with the provisions of § 38.2-2104, **no person insured under a fire insurance policy or a fire insurance policy in combination with other coverages shall assign or otherwise transfer, in whole or in part, to any other person the duties, rights, or benefits of the insured under the policy arising from a claim or covered loss without written consent of the insurer.**"
>
> "B. **Any contract provision entered in violation of this section shall be void and unenforceable.**"

**Key Provisions:**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Prohibited Acts** | Assignment or transfer of duties, rights, or benefits under a fire insurance policy arising from a claim/covered loss without insurer written consent |
| **Void Contracts** | Any contract provision violating this section is **VOID and UNENFORCEABLE** |
| **Permitted Acts** | (1) Authorizing/directing payment to a person for reasonable services/materials covered by policy; (2) Assigning rights to seek damages for personal injury/tort |
| **Applicability** | All fire insurance policies, including mutual assessment and perpetual insurance |

**Legislative History:**
- Enacted via HB1628 (Delegate Karen Keys-Gamarra) / SB1154 (Senator Mark Obenshain)
- 2025 General Assembly session; chapters 120, 134 of 2025 Acts of Assembly
- Stated purpose: "to help prevent possible fraud"

**What This Means for SmartContractor:**

| Activity | Status |
|----------|--------|
| Assignment of claim proceeds to SmartContractor as repayment | **PROHIBITED** |
| AOB/assignment as repayment mechanism for fire/wind/hail claims | **BLOCKED** |
| Direction of payment to contractor for services rendered | **PERMITTED** (not an assignment) |
| Invoice/direct billing model to insurer | Possible if insurer agrees; NOT guaranteed repayment |

**Public Adjuster Reinforcement:** § 38.2-1845.12(D)(16) prohibits public adjusters from adjusting claims where the policyholder has assigned duties, rights, or benefits contrary to § 38.2-2131.

## 7. Public Adjuster Licensing & Restrictions

### 7.1 Licensing Required

Virginia requires public adjusters to obtain a license from the SCC Bureau of Insurance (§§ 38.2-1845.1 to 38.2-1845.23). No other adjuster type (company, independent) requires licensure.

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Minimum age | 18 |
| Exam | Virginia public adjuster exam within 183 days of application |
| Initial fee | $250 |
| Biennial renewal | $250 |
| **Surety bond** | **$50,000** |
| Continuing education | 24 hours biennially (including 3 hours ethics) |
| Business entities | Must be licensed; designate responsible licensed public adjuster |
| Non-residents | Eligible via reciprocal agreements |

### 7.2 Standards of Conduct (§ 38.2-1845.12)

Public adjusters are **prohibited** from:

- Accepting compensation for investigating/settling claims without a license
- Preparing, completing, or filing insurance claims on behalf of policyholders without license
- Aiding or acting on behalf of policyholder in negotiating settlement without license
- **Adjusting claims where policyholder assigned rights contrary to § 38.2-2131** (D.16)
- Preventing policyholder from communicating with insurer, adjuster, or attorney
- Settling claims without written policyholder approval
- **Choosing repair contractors for policyholder** (L)
- Soliciting during loss-producing occurrence
- Soliciting between 8pm-8am
- Acting as appraiser/umpire on claims they adjusted
- Paying or receiving kickbacks, rebates, or commissions

### 7.3 Contract Requirements (§ 38.2-1845.13)

- Must be **in writing**
- Must include: adjuster name/license number, business address, policyholder name, loss details, services description, compensation terms, signatures
- **Rescission right:** 3 business days (5 days in catastrophic disaster)
- Must provide separate disclosure document to policyholder
- Must provide insurer with notification letter signed by policyholder

### 7.4 Fee Restrictions

- **No fee may be charged before settlement of claim** (§ 38.2-1845.14(A))
- Fee must be stated in written contract; if percentage-based, exact percentage required

### 7.5 Unauthorized Practice (§ 38.2-1845.24, effective 2026)

- Unlawful to engage in public adjusting without a Virginia license
- Unlawful for a PA to aid/abet unlicensed public adjusting
- **No person may evade licensing requirements through a power of attorney** (D)
- Exemptions: Administrative tasks that do not interpret/analyze/explain insurance or advise on public adjusting contracts

### 7.6 Implications for SmartContractor

| Activity | Permitted? |
|----------|-----------|
| Negotiating with insurers on behalf of homeowners | **NO — UNAUTHORIZED PRACTICE** |
| Assisting homeowners in preparing claims | **NO** |
| Estimating losses for insurer negotiation | **NO** |
| Advocating for policyholders before insurers | **NO** |
| Referring homeowners to licensed public adjusters | **YES** |
| Contractors negotiating with insurers (beyond submitting invoices) | **RISK — may be unlicensed public adjusting** |

### 9.4 Key Smart Contract Notes

1. **Assignment of Claim Proceeds:** Smart contract MUST include hard block preventing any assignment of insurance claim rights, proceeds, or benefits. Such assignments are void under Virginia law and expose SmartContractor to regulatory enforcement and civil liability.

2. **Token Collateral Lock:** Virginia's crypto-friendly posture is favorable, but smart contract lock/liquidation still requires analysis under Virginia commercial law, UCC Article 9, and federal securities/commodities law.

3. **Repayment Routing:** Any smart contract mechanism that attempts to intercept or redirect insurance claim proceeds to SmartContractor as repayment MUST be blocked. This would constitute an assignment or transfer prohibited by § 38.2-2131.

4. **Business-Purpose Certification:** Smart contract should record borrower's business-purpose certification with cryptographic hash for audit purposes.

5. **36% APR Cap:** If loan functionality is ever enabled, smart contract must enforce maximum 36% APR cap for any transaction that could be deemed consumer-purpose.

6. **Escrow Handling:** Any escrow functionality must be reviewed for Bureau of Financial Institutions registration requirements before activation.
