## 1. State Summary & Risk Profile

Vermont is a **highly regulated** state for all GCSC product lines. The Department of Financial Regulation (DFR) exercises broad oversight of lending, money transmission, insurance, and virtual-currency activity. The Vermont Attorney General has an established record of aggressive enforcement against online lenders charging rates above state caps. There is **no small-loan or commercial-purpose exemption** from Vermont usury limits, and the state asserts jurisdiction over out-of-state lenders making loans to Vermont residents by mail, telephone, or electronic means.

**Key Determinants:**
- All consumer-purpose lending to Vermont residents requires a DFR lender license (8 V.S.A. Ch. 73).
- Loan solicitation, lead generation, and brokering each require a separate **Loan Solicitation License** (Act 22, 2017).
- Interest rates are capped at **12% general / up to 24% for installment loans** (9 V.S.A. § 41a); excess interest is forfeited in full and carries criminal penalties.
- Vermont’s 2024 amendments added **Subchapter 10** — the most comprehensive state-level virtual-currency business regulation in the U.S. — codifying custody, disclosure, and kiosk requirements (8 V.S.A. §§ 2571–2577).
- **No Assignment of Benefits (AOB) statute** exists; general contract law governs, but DFR may view certain arrangements as unfair claims practices or unlicensed public adjusting.
- Public adjuster license required for anyone negotiating claim settlements on behalf of an insured (8 V.S.A. Ch. 131).
- Residential contractor registration required for construction work ≥ $10,000 (26 V.S.A. Ch. 106).
- Consumer litigation funding companies must **register** with DFR (8 V.S.A. Ch. 74) — the closest analog to a claim-advance regime.

**Overall GCSC Status:** All live lending, claim-advance, token-collateral, and AOB operations remain **BLOCKED** until Vermont-licensed counsel clears each product line.

## 3. Lending & Finance Licensing

### 3.1 Licensed Lender Requirements (8 V.S.A. Ch. 73)
A **Lender License** is required for "any company or person who engages in the business of making loans of money, credit, goods, or things in action and charges, contracts for or receives on any such loan interest, a finance charge, discount, or consideration" (8 V.S.A. § 2201). Key features:
- **Extraterritorial reach:** A loan "solicited or made by mail, telephone or electronic means to a Vermont resident is subject to licensing notwithstanding where the loan is legally made." Out-of-state lenders making loans to Vermont residents must be licensed.
- **Loan Solicitation License** (Act 22, 2017, effective May 4, 2017): Required for lead generation, brokering, or arranging loans. "Lead generation" is defined broadly to include online marketing, direct-response advertising, and telemarketing (8 V.S.A. § 2200(7)).
- **Commercial Lender License:** Required for companies making solely commercial loans. Exemptions exist for loans of $1,000,000+ and certain subordinated loans.
- **Exemptions:** Depository institutions, insurance companies, and sellers of goods/services financing their own sales (8 V.S.A. § 2201(d)).

### 3.2 Litigation Funding Registration (8 V.S.A. Ch. 74)
Consumer litigation funding companies must **register** with DFR. This applies to nonrecourse transactions where a company purchases a contingent right to settlement or judgment proceeds:
- Registration through NMLS, $600 fee, surety bond required.
- Extensive disclosure requirements on funding agreements.
- **No statutory fee cap** currently, but the Commissioner reports fee data to the legislature.
- This statute is the closest existing analog to insurance-claim advance financing in Vermont.

### 3.3 Enforcement Environment
- The Vermont Attorney General has secured multiple large settlements against online lenders charging APRs exceeding Vermont caps.
- Vermont **prohibits origination fees** on loans subject to state law.
- Third-party liability exists for assisting illegal lending activity.

## 4. Escrow, Money Transmission & Virtual Currency Regulation

> **NEW SECTION** — This section consolidates Vermont’s escrow authority, money-transmitter framework, and the landmark 2024 virtual-currency amendments that materially affect token-collateral products.

### 4.1 Escrow Regulation
The **Vermont Department of Financial Regulation** regulates escrow activity. No person may engage in the business of accepting escrow deposits without DFR authorization. For GCSC:
- Any arrangement holding funds in escrow for construction projects, insurance proceeds, or loan collateral must be reviewed for DFR escrow licensing.
- Insurance proceeds held in escrow by a mortgage servicer (loss draft) fall under standard mortgagee rights; no standalone escrow license applies to mortgage servicers acting under contractual authority.
- Third-party escrow agents handling GCSC-related funds likely require DFR escrow registration or licensing.

### 4.2 Money Transmitter Licensing (8 V.S.A. Ch. 79)
Vermont’s money-services statute requires a **Money Transmitter License** for engaging in money transmission:
- **Net worth minimum:** $100,000 (8 V.S.A. § 2540).
- **Security / Bond:** $100,000 minimum, up to $500,000 (Commissioner may increase to $2,000,000) (8 V.S.A. § 2541).
- **Virtual currency business activity** is explicitly included within money transmission.

### 4.3 2024 Virtual Currency Amendments — Subchapter 10
Effective **July 1, 2024**, Vermont enacted comprehensive virtual-currency provisions (8 V.S.A. §§ 2571–2577), making it the most detailed state-level VC regulatory framework in the nation:

| Section | Subject | Key Provision |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| § 2571 | Definitions | Codifies "virtual currency," "virtual-currency business activity," "control of virtual currency," "virtual-currency control-services vendor" |
| § 2572 | Exemptions | Lists limited exemptions; no explicit token-collateral or lending exemption |
| § 2573 | Conditions precedent | Licensing, registration, or notice required before engaging in VC business activity |
| § 2574 | Required disclosures | Fee schedules, exchange rates, reversible vs. irreversible transactions, risk disclosures |
| § 2575 | Property interests | **Custody requirement:** Must hold VC in "identical type and amount" sufficient to satisfy customer entitlements |
| § 2576 | Additional requirements | Capital, compliance, audit, and recordkeeping obligations |
| § 2577 | Kiosk operators | Fee caps (greater of $5 or 15% of transaction value); kiosk registration required |

**Critical Definitions:**
- **"Virtual currency"** (8 V.S.A. § 2503): Stored value that can be a medium of exchange, unit of account, or store of value; has equivalent value in money or acts as a substitute for money; may be centralized or decentralized; can be exchanged for money or other convertible virtual currency.
- **"Control of virtual currency"** (8 V.S.A. § 2503(6)): "The power to execute unilaterally or prevent indefinitely a virtual currency transaction."

### 4.4 Implications for Token Collateral
- Using cryptocurrency as collateral for a loan likely implicates money-transmission licensing **if the lender takes "control"** of the virtual currency.
- A smart-contract lock or liquidation mechanism that gives the lender the unilateral power to execute or prevent a VC transaction **likely constitutes "control"** under § 2503(6).
- "Storage" of virtual currency on behalf of another person is a form of **virtual-currency business activity** requiring a money-transmitter license.
- **No explicit statutory exemption** exists for non-licensed entities to hold virtual currency as loan collateral.
- Vermont treats cryptocurrency as **property**, not currency (12 V.S.A. § 1913 provides blockchain record admissibility; 11 V.S.A. Ch. 25, Subch. 12 authorizes Blockchain-Based LLCs).

## 5. Interest Rate Caps & Usury

Vermont imposes strict interest-rate limitations under **9 V.S.A. § 41a**:

| Loan Type | Maximum Rate |
|-----------|-------------|
| General legal rate | **12% per annum** |
| Single-payment loans by licensed lenders | **18%** |
| Installment loans | **24%** on first $1,000; 12% on balance above $1,000; OR 18% APR overall, whichever is higher |
| Vehicle-secured loans | **18–20%** depending on vehicle age |
| Subordinate-lien real estate | **18%** |
| First-lien real estate | Federal deregulated rate applies |

### Penalties & Remedies
- **Excess interest forfeited:** A person charging above the legal rate forfeits the **entire interest** (9 V.S.A. § 50).
- **Criminal penalties:** Fine up to $500 and/or imprisonment up to 6 months for first offense; up to $1,000 and/or 1 year for subsequent offenses.
- **No commercial-purpose exemption** from usury limits for business loans.

### Implications for GCSC
- Any loan product offered to Vermont borrowers must stay within applicable caps.
- Origination fees are prohibited on state-law loans.
- The Attorney General actively enforces usury limits against out-of-state online lenders.

## 6. Contractor Licensing & Residential Construction

### 6.1 No State-Level General Contractor License
Vermont **does not** require a state-level general contractor license. Some specialty trades (electrical, plumbing, asbestos/lead abatement) require licensing through the Division of Fire Safety. Most municipalities do not require local contractor licenses.

### 6.2 Residential Contractor Registration (26 V.S.A. Ch. 106)
Effective April 1, 2023, registration is required for residential construction on dwellings with 4 or fewer units where estimated value is **$10,000 or more** (inclusive of labor and materials):
- Covers construction, renovation, repair, painting, paving, roofing, weatherization, and system installation/repair.
- **Registration** (not licensure) with the Office of Professional Regulation (OPR).
- Requirements: $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate general liability insurance; written contract; business registration with Secretary of State.
- **Subcontractors** contracted by another contractor (not the homeowner directly) are exempt.
- Fees: $75 individual / $250 business (biennial).
- Written contract must include: price terms, estimated start/end dates, scope of work, and change-order provisions.

### 6.3 Home Improvement Fraud (13 V.S.A. § 2029)
Criminal offense for home-improvement contracts of $500+ with a single owner, or $2,500+ aggregate with multiple owners:
- Offenses include promising performance not intended, misrepresenting material facts, using unfair/deceptive practices, or charging >2× average price during a state of emergency.
- Penalties: up to 2 years / $1,000 (single consumer, <$1,000 loss); up to 3 years / $5,000 (loss $1,000+ or multiple consumers $2,500+); up to 5 years for repeat offenders.
- Person convicted must register with the Department of Labor and may be required to post a $50,000 bond.

### 6.4 Financing Implications
- Equipment financing and working capital loans to contractors likely require a **Commercial Lender License** unless structured through an exempt entity.
- Consumer-purpose contractor financing (e.g., to a homeowner for repairs) likely requires a **Lender License** (8 V.S.A. Ch. 73).
- Any loan solicitation or lead-generation activity requires a **Loan Solicitation License**.

## 7. Insurance Claims, Public Adjusters & Assignment of Benefits

### 7.1 Insurance Claim Handling Timelines
- **DFR Regulation I-79-2 (Revised 2018):** Insurer must acknowledge a claim within **10 business days** and advise of acceptance/denial within **15 business days** after receipt of properly executed proof of loss. If more time is needed, insurer must notify the claimant within 15 business days giving reasons. After settlement is agreed upon, payment must be mailed within **10 business days**.
- **8 V.S.A. § 3665a:** Payment within **10 business days after settlement date**; interest accrues at **12% per annum** on late payments.
- **Additional Living Expenses (ALE):** Not mandated by Vermont statute; typically reimbursement-based. Whether insurers must provide ALE advances is determined by policy terms.
- **Loss Draft / Mortgagee Checks:** Industry-standard practice applies — mortgage company listed as co-payee on insurance checks; standard $40,000 threshold for escrowed claims. No specific Vermont statute governs loss draft procedures.

### 7.2 Public Adjuster Licensing (8 V.S.A. Ch. 131)
Vermont **requires** a public adjuster license for anyone who "investigates claims and negotiates settlement of claims arising under policies of insurance on behalf of the insured" (8 V.S.A. § 4791):
- Must be at least 18, competent, trustworthy, financially responsible; must pass a written examination.
- Experience: 2 years handling loss claims, or special training, or supervised by licensed adjuster for 3+ years.
- License renewal: biennial on March 31 of even-numbered years.
- **Reciprocal licensing** available for adjusters licensed in other states that require examination.

**Key Restriction:** Only licensed public adjusters, attorneys, or the policyholder may negotiate with the insurance company on behalf of the insured. **Contractors may not** negotiate insurance claims on behalf of homeowners unless they hold a public adjuster license. Contractors reviewing damage and preparing repair estimates is acceptable normal business practice; negotiating settlement amounts or coverage determinations crosses into unlicensed public adjusting.

### 7.3 Assignment of Benefits (AOB)
Vermont **does not have a specific AOB statute** for property insurance claims. General contract law governs:
- Insurance claims may be assignable under common law unless the policy prohibits it.
- No AOB cancellation windows, font-size requirements, or form requirements exist.
- **Caution:** DFR could view certain AOB-type arrangements as insurance fraud or unfair claims practices under 8 V.S.A. § 4724(9).
- Any arrangement involving a contractor negotiating with an insurance company on behalf of a homeowner implicates **public adjuster licensing** requirements.

## 8. Litigation Funding & Claim Advance Models

### 8.1 No Specific Claim Advance Statute
Vermont **does not** have a statute explicitly authorizing or regulating insurance claim advances. Standard insurance contract law and DFR regulatory requirements apply.

### 8.2 Consumer Litigation Funding as Closest Analog (8 V.S.A. Ch. 74)
The litigation funding registration regime is the closest existing framework to a claim-advance product:
- Applies to nonrecourse transactions purchasing a contingent right to proceeds from a settlement or judgment.
- Registration through NMLS, $600 fee, surety bond.
- Extensive disclosure requirements including total funded amount, total charges, and annual percentage rate of return.
- **No statutory fee cap**, but Commissioner reports to legislature.

### 8.3 Implications for ClaimBridge / Claim-Advance Products
- Any arrangement providing advances against insurance claims to Vermont consumers likely implicates **lender licensing** (8 V.S.A. Ch. 73) given the broad definition of "loan" and "loan solicitation."
- Interest-rate caps (9 V.S.A. § 41a) at 12–24% would apply to any charge characterized as interest or a finance charge.
- The absence of a specific claim-advance statute creates **regulatory uncertainty**; DFR may apply lending, litigation-funding, or consumer-fraud frameworks.
- **All claim-advance operations BLOCKED** pending legal review of applicable regulatory regime.
