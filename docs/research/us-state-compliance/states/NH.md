## Section 1 — State Summary & Regulatory Overview

New Hampshire presents a **moderate-to-complex regulatory environment** for SmartContractor products. Key highlights:

- **No state-level general contractor licensing** — contractors are regulated at the municipal level, with state-level licensing only for specialty trades (electrical, plumbing, HVAC, asbestos, lead abatement). SmartContractor must verify compliance at the municipal level.
- **Strict small-loan licensing** — RSA 399-A requires a Small Loan Lender license (via NMLS) for any loan of $10,000 or less to consumers for personal/family/household purposes with an APR of 10% or more. Business-purpose loans are generally exempt.
- **10% usury cap for loans under $10,000** (RSA 336:1) unless the lender holds a Small Loan Lender license or another applicable exemption.
- **Post-loss Assignment of Benefits (AOB) is permitted** — the New Hampshire Supreme Court in *Keene Auto Body v. State Farm* (2022) held that ambiguous anti-assignment clauses do not prohibit post-loss assignments. The court left open whether an unambiguous post-loss anti-assignment clause would be enforceable.
- **Public adjusters are heavily regulated** — RSA 402-D requires licensing, a $20,000 bond, written contracts with 3-day rescission rights, and imposes strict conflict-of-interest prohibitions (no financial interest in construction firms, repair shops, etc.).
- **Cryptocurrency is legal and relatively unregulated** — NH amended RSA 399-G to exempt virtual currency transmitters from money transmission licensing, though consumer protection laws still apply. HB 302 (2025) made NH the first state to authorize public fund investment in cryptocurrency.
- **Strong consumer protection** — RSA 358-A provides for treble damages for willful violations and applies broadly to business practices.
- **No specific Assignment of Benefits statute** for property insurance claims exists (unlike Florida's comprehensive AOB statute).
- **Advance payments on claims are protected** — insurers cannot refuse advance payments solely because the claimant retained an attorney (N.H. Admin. Code § Ins 1001.06).
- **Escrow activity** — the New Hampshire Banking Department may regulate escrow activities; no standalone escrow agent statute comparable to other states, but escrow services related to consumer lending may trigger licensing requirements.

**Overall Status**: Both TokenCollateral/EquipmentCredit and ClaimBridge products are **BLOCKED** for live operation pending legal review. New Hampshire's unique combination of permissive crypto policy, permissive AOB case law, strict public adjuster restrictions, aggressive small-loan licensing, and potential escrow oversight creates a complex compliance landscape that requires state-specific counsel analysis.

## Section 3 — Lending / Finance Licensing

### Small Loan Lender Licensing (RSA 399-A)
- **License required**: Any person engaged in the business of making small loans, title loans, or payday loans to New Hampshire consumers must obtain a **Small Loan Lender license** from the Banking Department via NMLS.
- **"Small loan" definition** (RSA 399-A:1, XX): A loan that is:
  - $10,000 or less;
  - Has an APR of 10% or more;
  - Is for **personal, family, or household use** (consumer purpose).
- **"Payday loan"** (RSA 399-A:1, XIII): A short-term secured or unsecured loan, other than a title loan.
- **Who is a "lender"** (RSA 399-A:1, XII): Includes entities involved in any part of the loan process — lead generation, taking applications, brokering, funding, servicing, or holding servicing rights.
- **Key restriction**: Only one payday loan may be outstanding per consumer at any time; minimum 7-day term; maximum $500 principal.
- **Business-purpose loans**: Generally **NOT** covered by RSA 399-A if the loan is for business/commercial purposes rather than personal/family/household use.
- **Examinations**: Licensees examined at least every 24 months.

### Usury Cap (RSA 336:1)
- **10% per annum** is the maximum lawful rate of interest on any loan of $10,000 or less for personal, family, or household purposes, unless the lender is licensed under RSA 399-A or another applicable exemption statute.
- Loans exceeding $10,000 are generally not subject to this usury cap if the interest rate is agreed upon in writing.
- **Exemptions**: Banks, credit unions, licensed consumer/commercial finance lenders, and licensed small loan lenders are exempt from RSA 336:1.
- **Penalty**: Any lender charging more than the lawful rate forfeits the entire interest (RSA 336:2).

### Commercial/Business Financing
- **No specific state license** required for commercial/business-purpose lending in New Hampshire.
- **Usury**: No general usury limit when interest rate is agreed upon in writing for commercial loans. Banks, credit unions, and licensed consumer/commercial finance lenders are exempt from usury limits.
- **UCC Article 9** (RSA 382-A): Governs secured transactions; filing fee for UCC-1 financing statement is $48.
- **Loan broker licensing**: Not specifically required in New Hampshire for commercial loans, but if brokering consumer loans, may trigger small loan lender licensing requirements.

### Debt Adjuster Licensing (RSA 399-D)
- **License required** for any person engaged in "debt adjustment" — including debt management plans, debt settlement plans, and debt repayment plans.
- **"Debt adjustment" includes**: Providing advice/counseling to debtors, creating debt management contracts, negotiating with creditors, acting as agent of debtor to make payments to creditors, or serving as intermediary.
- **$25,000 surety bond** required.
- **Relevance to SmartContractor**: If SmartContractor intermediates between homeowners and contractors/insurers to obtain payment concessions or manage payment plans, this could trigger debt adjuster licensing requirements.

### Money Transmission (RSA 399-G)
- **License required** for any person engaged in the business of money transmission in New Hampshire.
- **Virtual currency exemption** (RSA 399-G:3, VII): Persons who engage solely in selling/issuing payment instruments or stored value in the form of convertible virtual currency, or who receive convertible virtual currency for transmission, are **exempt from money transmission licensing**.
- **However**, such exempt persons are **subject to RSA 358-A** (Consumer Protection Act).
- **"Convertible virtual currency"** defined in RSA 399-G:1.

## Section 4 — Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules

### Escrow Regulatory Framework in New Hampshire

- **No standalone escrow agent licensing statute** exists in New Hampshire comparable to states like California or Texas.
- **The New Hampshire Banking Department** has broad authority over escrow activities that are incidental to consumer lending, mortgage banking, or debt adjustment. Escrow services provided in connection with licensed activities may fall under Banking Department oversight.
- Escrow activities that are **ancillary to real estate transactions** are typically performed by title companies, attorneys, or real estate brokers operating under their respective professional licenses.

### Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Product — Risk Analysis

An "escrow-backed contractor advance" product — where SmartContractor or a partner holds insurance claim proceeds or homeowner funds in escrow and disburses them to contractors incrementally as work is completed — presents the following regulatory considerations:

| Risk Factor | Analysis | Mitigation |
|-------------|----------|------------|
| **Banking Dept oversight** | Holding third-party funds for disbursement may constitute activity requiring a banking or consumer credit license if done in connection with lending | Structure as attorney/trust account; use licensed title company or financial institution as escrow holder |
| **RSA 399-D (Debt Adjuster)** | Controlling disbursement of insurance proceeds to creditors/contractors on behalf of a homeowner may trigger debt adjuster licensing | Ensure escrow holder is independent; limit SmartContractor role to technology platform only |
| **RSA 399-A (Small Loan)** | If the advance is structured as a loan to the homeowner (consumer purpose, $10K or less, 10%+ APR), small loan licensing applies | Structure advances as commercial loans to contractors; keep homeowner out of borrower chain |
| **Fiduciary duty** | An escrow holder owes fiduciary duties to all parties; commingling or misappropriation carries civil and criminal liability | Maintain segregated accounts; engage licensed escrow/trust services; implement audit trails |
| **Consumer Protection (RSA 358-A)** | Treble damages for willful violations apply to escrow mismanagement, misleading disbursement timelines, or undisclosed fees | Full fee disclosure; clear escrow agreement terms; avoid misleading marketing |

### Escrow Best Practices for SmartContractor

1. **Use an independent licensed escrow agent** — engage a New Hampshire-licensed attorney, title company, or financial institution to serve as escrow holder rather than SmartContractor holding funds directly.
2. **Escrow agreement requirements**:
   - Written agreement signed by homeowner, contractor, and escrow holder
   - Clear description of the scope of work triggering disbursement
   - Itemized disbursement schedule tied to verified completion milestones
   - Process for dispute resolution and fund return
   - Full disclosure of all escrow fees
   - Maximum hold period and conditions for release
3. **Segregation of funds** — escrow funds must never be commingled with SmartContractor operating funds.
4. **Inspection/verification triggers** — disbursements should require third-party inspection or homeowner written approval of completed work phases.
5. **Mortgagee coordination** — if a mortgagee is named as a loss payee on the insurance policy, escrow disbursement must comply with the mortgagee's rights and any loss draft processing requirements.
6. **3-day rescission consideration** — while not statutorily required for escrow agreements, offering a 3-business-day cancellation period aligns with NH consumer protection norms and reduces regulatory risk.

### Escrow-Related Product Status
- **Status**: `BLOCKED_PENDING_REVIEW`
- No live escrow-holding or escrow-backed advance disbursement may proceed until:
  - Legal counsel confirms escrow structure does not trigger Banking Department licensing
  - Escrow holder arrangement with licensed NH entity is executed
  - RSA 399-D debt adjuster analysis is completed
  - RSA 358-A consumer protection disclosure language is approved

## Section 5 — Contractor Licensing & Financing

### Contractor Licensing
- **General contractors**: **No state-level license required** in New Hampshire. However, local municipalities (cities/towns) may have their own licensing or registration requirements. SmartContractor must verify compliance at the municipal level.
- **Specialty trades requiring state-level licenses**:
  - **Electricians**: Licensed by the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification Electricians' Board (multiple levels from apprentice to master).
  - **Plumbers**: Licensed by the NH Department of Safety (apprentice, journeyman, master).
  - **HVAC/Mechanical**: Licensed by the NH Mechanical Safety and Licensing Board.
  - **Asbestos abatement**: Licensed by the NH Department of Environmental Services.
  - **Lead abatement**: Licensed by the NH Department of Health and Human Services.
- **Business registration**: All businesses (including contractors) must register with the NH Secretary of State unless operating under the owner's personal name.

### Contractor Financing
- **Equipment financing and working capital loans** for contractors are generally unregulated at the state level if structured as business-purpose loans.
- **Factoring/invoice financing**: Not specifically regulated in New Hampshire.
- **Key risk**: If a contractor financing product is structured such that the homeowner (consumer) is the borrower for repair work on their primary residence, it may trigger **RSA 399-A** small loan licensing requirements and **RSA 336:1** 10% usury cap.

### Insurance Requirements
- Workers' compensation insurance required for employers with employees.
- General liability insurance commonly required by municipalities and for contracts; typical minimum $1 million.
- Surety bonds may be required for public works projects.

## Section 6 — Insurance Claim Advances & Assignment of Benefits

### Advance Payments on Claims
- **N.H. Admin. Code § Ins 1001.06**: "No insurer shall refuse to grant advance payments on a claim because the claimant, insured or authorized representative of either has retained an attorney for the purpose of facilitating recovery on his behalf."
- This rule **protects the right to advance payments** but does not create a mandatory obligation for insurers to provide them.
- **No specific statute found** requiring insurers to provide emergency advance payments or partial claim payments in New Hampshire.

### Additional Living Expenses (ALE) / Loss of Use
- ALE coverage is standard in most homeowners insurance policies in New Hampshire.
- ALE pays for temporary housing costs if the home is uninhabitable due to a covered loss.
- ALE only pays for **additional costs** beyond normal living expenses (e.g., extra rent, restaurant meals, laundry); it does not pay for the mortgage, regular grocery bills, etc.
- Some policies have dollar limits and/or time limitations on ALE.
- Homeowners should keep all receipts for additional costs.

### Claim Payments Generally
- After a loss, the insurance company assigns an adjuster to assess damage and estimate repair costs.
- The insurer issues claim payment checks ("loss drafts").
- If there is a mortgage, the check is typically made payable to **both the homeowner and the mortgagee**.
- The mortgagee/lender has rights to hold and disburse insurance proceeds based on the standard mortgage clause.

### Assignment of Benefits — Status: **PERMITTED (with caveats)**

New Hampshire does not have a comprehensive Assignment of Benefits (AOB) statute for property insurance claims. Instead, AOB is governed by:

1. **Common law** (contract principles)
2. **Insurance policy language**
3. **Case law**, specifically *Keene Auto Body, Inc. v. State Farm Mutual Auto Insurance Co.*, 293 A.3d 1146 (N.H. 2022)

#### *Keene Auto Body v. State Farm* (2022)
- The New Hampshire Supreme Court held that an insurance policy's **anti-assignment clause was ambiguous** and did not prohibit post-loss assignments.
- The policy stated: "No assignment of benefits or other transfer of rights is binding upon us unless approved by us."
- The court found this language did not clearly distinguish between **pre-loss** assignments (which increase insurer risk) and **post-loss** assignments (which do not).
- The court adopted the **"overwhelming majority" rule**: anti-assignment clauses generally do not prevent assignment of post-loss claims.
- **Crucial caveat**: The court explicitly stated it was **"expressing no opinion as to whether an unambiguous clause prohibiting assignment of post-loss claims would be enforceable."**
- Post-loss assignees (e.g., contractors) "stand in the shoes" of the insured — any defense available to the insurer against the insured is also available against the assignee.

#### Practical Implications for AOB
- **Post-loss AOB is likely enforceable in New Hampshire** under current case law, especially if the policy language is ambiguous.
- **If insurers revise policy language** to explicitly prohibit post-loss assignments, enforceability is uncertain.
- **AOB must be in writing** (standard contract requirement).
- **AOB is not required** for homeowners to get claims processed or repairs completed.

#### AOB Best Practices (if utilized)
- Written agreement signed by both parties
- Clear scope of work and compensation terms
- Itemized description of work, materials, labor, and fees
- Notice to insurer within a reasonable time
- Cannot impair rights of a mortgagee
- Cannot prohibit insured or mortgagee from communicating with insurer

#### No Fee Cap for Public Adjusters
- New Hampshire does **not** impose a fee cap on public adjuster compensation (unlike many states).
- However, the public adjuster contract must disclose the full fee/commission structure.

## Section 7 — Public Adjuster & Insurance Representation

### Licensing Required (RSA 402-D)
- **Public adjuster license required** to negotiate or settle insurance claims on behalf of insureds in New Hampshire.
- Licensed through the NH Insurance Department via NIPR.
- **$20,000 surety bond** required (RSA 402-D:11).
- **15 hours continuing education** every 2 years (RSA 402-D:12).
- **Biennial renewal** by October 1st; NH does not accept late renewals.

### Contract Requirements (RSA 402-D:13)
- Must be a **written contract** titled "Public Adjuster Contract"
- Must include:
  - Name, business name, phone, and address of public adjuster
  - Name and address of insured
  - Description of loss and location
  - Name of insurer and policy number
  - Full salary, fee, commission, or other consideration
  - Description of services
  - Signatures of both parties with dates
  - 12-point bold disclosure of 3-day rescission right

### Prohibited Conduct (RSA 402-D:17)
Public adjusters **CANNOT**:
- **Have any direct or indirect financial interest in any aspect of the claim** other than their disclosed fee
- **Have any interest in any construction firm, salvage firm, building appraisal firm, motor vehicle repair shop**, or any other firm performing work in conjunction with insured damages (this includes contractors)
- **Refer insured to any person with whom the PA has a financial interest** or from whom they receive compensation for referral
- **Enter into a contract where the insured would obtain full recovery without PA services**
- **Solicit an insured during the progress of a loss-producing occurrence**
- **Permit unlicensed employees to conduct licensed business**
- **Employ, be employed by, or partner with any licensed insurance producer, insurer, or employee of an insurer**
- **Require any interest in salvage** without written permission
- **Pay commissions or remuneration to unlicensed persons** for investigating/settling claims

### Rescission Rights
- Insured has **3 business days** to rescind the contract after signing.
- Rescission must be in writing.
- Any value given by insured must be returned within 15 business days.

### Penalties
- **Class A misdemeanor** for acting as a public adjuster without a license.
- Administrative fines up to $2,500 for misrepresentation or wrongful divulgence of information.
- Commissioner may order refund of moneys gained or restitution.

### Implications for SmartContractor
- **SmartContractor and its contractors MUST NOT engage in activities that constitute public adjusting** — including negotiating with insurance companies on behalf of homeowners, evaluating claims, or advising on coverage adequacy.
- **Any contractor that is also a public adjuster** must maintain strict separation between roles and comply with RSA 402-D:17 prohibitions.
- **SmartContractor must not share fees with public adjusters** or receive referrals from public adjusters with a financial interest.

## Section 8 — Token Collateral / Cryptocurrency

### New Hampshire Cryptocurrency Legal Framework

New Hampshire is one of the most **cryptocurrency-friendly** states in the U.S.:

1. **Virtual Currency Exemption from Money Transmission** (RSA 399-G:3, VII):
   - Persons who solely engage in selling/issuing payment instruments or stored value in convertible virtual currency, or who receive convertible virtual currency for transmission, are **exempt from money transmission licensing** in New Hampshire.
   - Such persons remain subject to the **Consumer Protection Act (RSA 358-A)**.

2. **State Crypto Reserve** (HB 302, 2025):
   - New Hampshire became the first U.S. state to authorize its state treasurer to invest public funds in cryptocurrency.
   - Allows up to 5% (some sources say 10%) of certain public funds to be allocated to eligible digital assets with market capitalization above $500 billion (currently only Bitcoin qualifies).
   - Assets must be held in U.S.-regulated custody.

3. **No specific token collateral statute**:
   - There is **no New Hampshire statute or regulation specifically addressing the use of cryptocurrency or digital assets as collateral** for lending purposes.
   - General UCC Article 9 (RSA 382-A) principles would apply to the creation and perfection of security interests in digital assets.
   - Whether cryptocurrency constitutes a "general intangible," "investment property," or another category of collateral under UCC Article 9 is an unresolved legal question that requires counsel analysis.

4. **Federal compliance still required**:
   - FinCEN registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) may still be required at the federal level for certain virtual currency activities.
   - AML/KYC obligations apply regardless of state exemption.

### Token Collateral Risk Assessment
- **Status**: `TOKEN_COLLATERAL_UNKNOWN_REQUIRES_COUNSEL_REVIEW`
- The permissive state regulatory environment does not eliminate legal uncertainty around:
  - Classification of cryptocurrency collateral under UCC Article 9
  - Creation and perfection of security interests in digital assets
  - Liquidation mechanics and compliance with state lending laws
  - Whether token collateral lock/liquidation constitutes money transmission at the federal level
  - Consumer protection implications of cryptocurrency collateral for consumer-purpose loans

## Section 9 — Mortgage / Loss Draft

### Standard Mortgage Clause
- Homeowners insurance policies typically include a **standard mortgage clause** naming the mortgagee as an additional insured/loss payee.
- The mortgagee's interest is protected regardless of policy violations by the insured (e.g., increase in hazard, change in ownership, foreclosure).

### Loss Draft Check Process
- When a mortgaged property suffers a covered loss, the insurance company issues claim checks payable to **both the homeowner and the mortgagee**.
- The mortgagee/lender holds and disburses insurance proceeds for repairs.
- **Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac guidelines** typically govern disbursement schedules:
  - Loans current: Initial disbursement up to $40,000 or 33% of proceeds; remainder based on inspection.
  - Loans delinquent: Funds held in escrow; disbursed in increments after inspection.

### Priority of Claims to Insurance Proceeds
- The mortgagee's claim to insurance proceeds is based on the **standard mortgage clause in the policy**, not merely the mortgage lien on the property.
- A contractor's mechanic's lien does **not** automatically take priority over the mortgagee's interest in insurance proceeds.
- **Mortgagee rights cannot be impaired** by an assignment of benefits without the mortgagee's consent.

### New Hampshire Specific Rules
- **RSA 417:4, XII(d)**: A creditor/lender of a loan secured by real property shall not require the borrower to keep the mortgaged property insured in a sum in excess of the value of the buildings on the real property.
- **RSA 417:4, XII(e)**: A creditor/lender cannot require an original insurance policy at closing; a binder showing the borrower as named insured and creditor as mortgagee is sufficient.

| Insurance Claim Risk | **MEDIUM** | Advance payments protected (Ins 1001.06). ALE coverage is standard. No specific claim advance financing statute. Loss draft process follows standard mortgagee procedures. Main risk is inadvertently engaging in claim adjustment without license. |
| AOB Risk | **MEDIUM** | Post-loss AOB permitted under *Keene Auto Body v. State Farm* (2022), but no comprehensive AOB statute. Insurers may revise policy language to prohibit post-loss assignments. AOB must be carefully drafted to avoid public adjuster territory and preserve mortgagee rights. |
| Public Adjuster Risk | **HIGH** | RSA 402-D has severe conflict-of-interest prohibitions. PAs cannot have interest in construction firms, cannot refer to affiliated businesses, cannot employ/be employed by insurers or producers. SmartContractor/contractors must avoid any activity that constitutes "investigating or settling claims" without a PA license. Class A misdemeanor for unlicensed PA activity. |
| Token Collateral Risk | **MEDIUM** | NH is crypto-friendly — virtual currency transmitters exempt from money transmission licensing (RSA 399-G:3,VII). No state prohibition on crypto collateral. HB 302 (2025) state crypto reserve signals policy support. UCC Article 9 treatment uncertain. Federal FinCEN/MSB obligations may still apply. Consumer protection exposure exists if token collateral used for consumer loans. |
| Consumer Protection Risk | **HIGH** | RSA 358-A provides for treble damages (up to 3x) plus attorney's fees for willful/knowing violations. Applies broadly to business practices including B2B transactions. Exemptions exist for insurance and banking activities regulated by those commissioners, but scope of exemption is uncertain for novel products. |
| Escrow Risk | **MEDIUM-HIGH** | No standalone escrow statute, but Banking Department may assert oversight. RSA 399-D debt adjuster risk if controlling disbursements. Fiduciary duties attach to escrow holding. Requires independent licensed escrow holder and counsel approval before any live operation. |
