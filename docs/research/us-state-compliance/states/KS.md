# Kansas (KS) — SmartContractor State Compliance

## 1. State Summary

Kansas presents a **MODERATE-TO-HIGH regulatory complexity** for SmartContractor products. The state has a specific Assignment of Benefits statute (K.S.A. 50-6143) governing post-loss assignment of insurance benefits to residential contractors, with detailed notice, cancellation, and formatting requirements. The Uniform Consumer Credit Code (UCCC, K.S.A. 16a-1-101 et seq.) imposes strict rate caps and licensing requirements on consumer lending. The Kansas Mortgage Business Act (K.S.A. 9-2201 et seq.) regulates mortgage-related financing. Digital assets are treated favorably under OSBC guidance — transmission of decentralized cryptocurrency alone does not require a money transmitter license, but any involvement of sovereign currency may trigger licensing. Public adjusters are licensed and restricted to first-party claims; pending legislation (HB 2461, introduced January 2026) would further prohibit public adjusters from negotiating residential and personal lines claims. Roofing contractors must register under the Kansas Roofing Registration Act. General contractors are not licensed at the state level but are subject to local requirements. All SmartContractor lending, token collateral, insurance claim advance, and repayment routing activities are **BLOCKED pending legal review**.

| Factor | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Regulatory Complexity** | Moderate-to-High |
| **General Contractor License** | Not required at state level (local only) |
| **Roofing Registration** | Required (Kansas Roofing Registration Act) |
| **Consumer Lending License** | Required — UCCC supervised loan license (OSBC) |
| **Money Transmitter License** | Required if sovereign currency involved |
| **Crypto Treatment** | Favorable — pure decentralized crypto transmission exempt from MTL |
| **AOB Status** | Allowed with significant restrictions (K.S.A. 50-6143) |
| **Public Adjuster License** | Required; limited to first-party claims |
| **Usury Cap** | 10% unless otherwise agreed (K.S.A. 16-201); 36%/21% tiered under UCCC |

## 3. Lending / Finance Licensing

### 3.1 Uniform Consumer Credit Code (UCCC) — K.S.A. 16a-1-101 et seq.

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Regulator** | Kansas Office of the State Bank Commissioner (OSBC) |
| **Scope** | Governs all consumer credit transactions made in Kansas, including loans, credit sales, and leases for personal, family, or household purposes |
| **Applicability** | Applies when: (a) written agreement received from Kansas consumer; OR (b) creditor induces Kansas resident via solicitation in Kansas (mail, telephone, internet, etc.) — K.S.A. 16a-1-201(1) |

**Rate Caps — Consumer Loans (Closed-End):**
| Tier | Rate | Citation |
|------|------|----------|
| Unpaid balance of $860 or less | 36% per annum | K.S.A. 16a-2-401(2) |
| Portion exceeding $860 | 21% per annum | K.S.A. 16a-2-401(2) |
| Open-end consumer loans | Rate agreed by parties (no statutory cap) | K.S.A. 16a-2-401(1) |
| Loans secured by second mortgage | 18% per annum | K.S.A. 16a-2-401(3) |

- **Prepaid Finance Charges:** Limited to 2% of amount financed or $100, whichever is less (non-real estate transactions). K.S.A. 16a-2-401(6)(b).

### 3.2 Supervised Loan Licensing — K.S.A. 16a-2-301

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **License Required** | Any person making consumer loans must obtain a supervised loan license from OSBC |
| **Surety Bond** | $100,000 minimum required |
| **Net Worth** | Minimum as determined by administrator, not to exceed $500,000 per applicant |
| **Exemptions** | Supervised financial organizations (banks, credit unions), FDIC in corporate capacity, attorneys forwarding contracts for collection |

### 3.3 Kansas Mortgage Business Act — K.S.A. 9-2201 et seq.

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **License Required** | Any person conducting "mortgage business" (taking residential mortgage loan applications, offering/negotiating terms for compensation) |
| **Loan Originator Registration** | Individual loan originators must register (K.S.A. 9-2203) |
| **Covered Transactions** | Loans primarily for personal, family, or household purposes secured by mortgage on residential real estate |
| **Rate Caps** | Code mortgage rate (generally 18% for transactions opting into UCCC) — K.S.A. 9-2228 |
| **Prepaid Finance Charges** | Up to 8% of amount financed; aggregate to mortgage company/related party not exceeding 5% |
| **Prohibited Acts** | K.S.A. 9-2212 — misrepresentation, fraudulent omission, deceptive practices |

### 3.4 General Usury — K.S.A. 16-201

Kansas imposes a **10% per annum usury cap** on loans unless otherwise agreed. The parties to any bond, bill, promissory note or other instrument of writing for the payment or forbearance of money may stipulate for interest at a rate not exceeding:
- **10% per annum** if no written contract rate is specified
- **15% per annum** maximum under K.S.A. 16-207 for certain written contracts
- The UCCC tiered rates (36%/21%) supersede for covered consumer transactions

### 3.5 Commercial/Business-Purpose Loans

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Exemption** | K.S.A. 16-207(a) — rate caps "shall not apply to a business or agricultural loan" |
| **Definition** | "A business or agricultural transaction" means a loan made primarily for purposes other than personal, family, or household purposes — K.S.A. 16-207(e)(5) |
| **Documentation Requirement** | Lenders must obtain clear documentation that loan proceeds are for business purposes to avoid UCCC application |

### 3.6 Loan Brokering / Credit Service Organizations

- Kansas regulates credit service organizations under K.S.A. 50-1116 et seq.
- Any person arranging loans for compensation may trigger licensing requirements.

## 4. Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules

### 4.1 Escrow Regulatory Framework in Kansas

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Primary Regulator** | Kansas Office of the State Bank Commissioner (OSBC) |
| **Authority** | OSBC may regulate escrow activities under its broad consumer protection and financial services oversight mandate |
| **Applicable Statutes** | UCCC (K.S.A. 16a-1-101 et seq.); Kansas Mortgage Business Act (K.S.A. 9-2201 et seq.); general consumer protection authority |

### 4.2 Escrow Agent Licensing / Registration

- Kansas does not have a standalone "escrow agent" license at the state level comparable to some states (e.g., California).
- **Title companies** and **attorneys** commonly perform escrow functions in Kansas real estate transactions.
- **Mortgage escrow accounts** are governed by the Kansas Mortgage Business Act when maintained by licensed mortgage businesses.
- Persons holding funds in escrow in connection with mortgage transactions generally must be licensed under the Kansas Mortgage Business Act or be exempt (e.g., banks, credit unions).

### 4.3 Escrow-Backed Advance Structure — SmartContractor Implications

An escrow-backed contractor advance involves holding insurance claim proceeds or homeowner payments in escrow and disbursing them to the contractor upon completion milestones. This structure implicates multiple Kansas regulatory frameworks:

| Risk Factor | Analysis |
|-------------|----------|
| **Money Transmission Risk** | Holding and disbursing funds on behalf of others may constitute money transmission under K.S.A. 9-508 if "receiving money or monetary value for transmission" is triggered. Two-party escrow arrangements may qualify for exemption; third-party involvement likely requires MTL. |
| **UCCC Applicability** | If the escrow advance is structured as a loan to a homeowner (consumer purpose), UCCC rate caps and supervised loan licensing apply. If structured as a business-purpose advance to the contractor, the K.S.A. 16-207 exemption may apply. |
| **Trust Account / Fiduciary Duties** | Escrow holders owe fiduciary duties to all parties. Commingling of escrow funds with operating funds is prohibited. Interest on escrow accounts generally belongs to the escrow beneficiary unless otherwise agreed. |
| **Mortgagee Rights** | K.S.A. 50-6143(b)(5)(A) protects mortgagee interests — any escrow disbursement must not impair the interest of a mortgagee listed on the declarations page. |

### 4.4 Escrow Requirements for AOB Transactions

When an Assignment of Benefits is in place under K.S.A. 50-6143:

- Insurance proceeds may be directed to the contractor as co-payee under a valid AOB.
- An escrow mechanism that intercepts or holds these funds must comply with the AOB timing and notice requirements.
- The 5-business-day AOB cancellation window (K.S.A. 50-6143(b)(4)) means **no escrow disbursement should occur within 5 business days** of AOB execution.
- The contractor must provide the AOB to the insurer within 3 business days (K.S.A. 50-6143(b)(3)); escrow documentation should confirm this.

### 4.5 Required Escrow Disclosures

| Disclosure | Requirement |
|------------|-------------|
| **Escrow Agreement** | Written escrow agreement specifying disbursement conditions, fees, and timelines |
| **AOB Cancellation Right** | Notice that AOB may be cancelled within 5 business days, affecting escrow disbursement |
| **Mortgagee Notice** | Disclosure if mortgagee endorsement or approval is required |
| **Fee Disclosure** | Any escrow fees must comply with UCCC limitations if consumer-purpose |
| **Non-Interest Bearing** | Escrow funds are typically non-interest bearing unless otherwise agreed in writing |

### 4.6 Escrow-Backed Advance Risk Assessment

| Risk Category | Level | Notes |
|---------------|-------|-------|
| Money Transmission | **HIGH** | Escrow disbursement involving third-party transmission likely triggers KMTA licensing |
| UCCC/Lending | **HIGH** | Consumer-purpose escrow advances subject to 36%/21% rate caps and supervised loan licensing |
| Mortgagee Interference | **MEDIUM** | Must not impair mortgagee interest per K.S.A. 50-6143(b)(5)(A) |
| Fiduciary/Breach | **MEDIUM** | Escrow holder owes fiduciary duties; commingling prohibited |
| AOB Compliance | **HIGH** | 5-day cancellation window and 3-day insurer notice must be tracked |

**STATUS: ESCROW_BACKED_ADVANCE_BLOCKED.** All escrow-backed contractor advance products require Kansas-counsel review and likely OSBC consultation before any pilot or live deployment.

## 5. Token Collateral / Digital Asset Risk

### 5.1 Kansas Money Transmitter Act — K.S.A. 9-508 et seq.

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Definition** | "Money transmission" means engaging in the business of receiving money or monetary value for transmission to a location within or outside the U.S. by wire, facsimile, electronic means, or any other means |
| **"Monetary value"** | A medium of exchange, whether or not redeemable in money |

### 5.2 OSBC Guidance on Virtual Currencies (May 18, 2021)

The OSBC issued formal guidance on the regulatory treatment of virtual currencies:

| Scenario | MTL Required? | Rationale |
|----------|---------------|-----------|
| Pure decentralized cryptocurrency transmission (no sovereign currency) | **NO** | "Cryptocurrencies as currently in existence are not considered 'money' or 'monetary value' by the OSBC" |
| Transmission with sovereign currency involvement | **MAY BE REQUIRED** | Sovereign currency involvement triggers KMTA analysis |
| Third-party exchange of crypto for sovereign currency | **YES** | "Generally considered money transmission" |
| Two-party currency exchange | **NO** | Two-party exchange by itself is NOT covered by KMTA |

### 5.3 Token Collateral Assessment for SmartContractor

| Activity | MTL Risk | Analysis |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Token Collateral Lock** | Low | Two-party collateral management (borrower + GCSC) without third-party transmission likely does NOT require MTL license |
| **Liquidation** | **HIGH** | Liquidation involving exchanging cryptocurrency for sovereign currency through a third-party exchange likely WOULD constitute money transmission |
| **Repayment in Cryptocurrency** | Low | Accepting cryptocurrency as repayment without third-party transmission likely does not require MTL licensing |

**STATUS: TOKEN_COLLATERAL_LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED.** While Kansas is relatively favorable to cryptocurrency activities, the specific application of token collateral lock, liquidation, and repayment routing in the context of lending requires state-specific legal analysis. The distinction between two-party and third-party transactions is critical.

## 6. Insurance Claim Advance / ClaimBridge Risk

### 6.1 Claim Settlement Timing Requirements

Kansas has adopted the NAIC Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Model Regulation (1981 edition) via K.A.R. 40-1-34:

| Requirement | Timeline |
|-------------|----------|
| Acknowledgment of claim | Within 10 days |
| Decision (acceptance/denial) | Within 15 days after receipt of proof of loss |
| Investigation completion | Within 30 days, or status updates every 45 days |
| Cash settlements | Insurer shall not settle for less than repair cost unless agreed by insured |

### 6.2 Additional Living Expenses (ALE)

- K.S.A. 40-2,125 addresses additional living expenses, requiring insurers to pay certain claims promptly.
- **K.S.A. 40-21-38** specifically addresses ALE and other covered items not subject to a recorded security interest, suggesting mortgagee rights do not extend to ALE payments.

### 6.3 Unfair Claim Settlement Practices — K.S.A. 40-2404(9)

The following are unfair claim settlement practices when committed flagrantly or with frequency indicating general business practice:
- (f) Not attempting in good faith to effectuate prompt, fair, and equitable settlements
- (g) Compelling insureds to institute litigation by offering substantially less than amounts ultimately recovered
- (n) Failing to promptly provide reasonable explanation for denial or compromise settlement

### 6.4 No Private Right of Action

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Key Case** | *Jahnke v. Blue Cross and Blue Shield of Kansas, Inc.*, 353 P.3d 455, 465 (Kan. App. 2015) |
| **Holding** | Violations of K.S.A. 40-2404 do NOT create a private right of action |
| **Enforcement** | Only the Insurance Commissioner may enforce |
| **Penalties** | Up to $1,000 per violation ($10,000 aggregate); up to $5,000 per violation ($50,000 aggregate in 6-month period) for willful violations — K.S.A. 40-2407 |

### 6.5 Loss Draft / Mortgagee Checks

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Mortgagee Interest** | K.S.A. 50-6143(b)(5)(A): AOB "shall not impair the interest of a mortgagee listed on the declarations page" |
| **Insurer Communication** | AOB cannot "prevent or inhibit an insurer from communicating with the named insured or mortgagee" — K.S.A. 50-6143(b)(5)(B) |
| **Standard Practice** | Loss draft checks issued jointly to insured and mortgagee when mortgage exists |

## 7. Assignment of Benefits

### 7.1 AOB Status: **ALLOWED WITH SIGNIFICANT RESTRICTIONS**

Kansas has a specific Assignment of Benefits statute for residential contractors: **K.S.A. 50-6143** (enacted 2019, effective July 1, 2019).

### 7.2 Key Requirements

**Permitted Scope:**
- Assignment is limited to **post-loss** assignment of rights/benefits to a residential contractor.
- May authorize residential contractor to be named as **co-payee** for payment of benefits — K.S.A. 50-6143(b)(1).
- Applies to property and casualty insurance policies insuring residential real estate (1-4 family dwellings).

**Mandatory Notice — 14-Point Capitalized Type:**
The assignment MUST include the following notice in **capitalized 14-point type:**

> "AN ASSIGNMENT OF RIGHTS OR BENEFITS IS VOLUNTARY. YOU ARE AGREEING TO ASSIGN CERTAIN RIGHTS THAT YOU HAVE UNDER YOUR INSURANCE POLICY. WITH AN ASSIGNMENT, THE RESIDENTIAL CONTRACTOR SHALL BE ENTITLED TO PURSUE ANY RIGHTS OR REMEDIES THAT YOU, THE INSURED POLICY HOLDER, HAVE UNDER YOUR INSURANCE POLICY. PLEASE READ AND UNDERSTAND THIS DOCUMENT BEFORE SIGNING.
>
> THE INSURER MAY ONLY PAY FOR THE COST TO REPAIR OR REPLACE DAMAGED PROPERTY CAUSED BY A COVERED PERIL, SUBJECT TO THE TERMS OF THE POLICY."

**Cancellation Window:**
- Named insured has the right to cancel the assignment **within five business days after execution** — K.S.A. 50-6143(b)(4).
- This is in addition to any other right to revoke.

**Notice to Insurer:**
- Residential contractor must provide a copy of the assignment to the insurer **within three business days** after the assignment is signed — K.S.A. 50-6143(b)(3).

**Prohibited Provisions:**
- Assignment shall NOT impair the interest of a mortgagee listed on the declarations page.
- Assignment shall NOT prevent or inhibit an insurer from communicating with the named insured or mortgagee.

**Voiding Conditions:**
- An assignment is **VOID** if the residential contractor:
  - Violates any provision of K.S.A. 50-6143; OR
  - Is not in compliance with the Kansas Roofing Registration Act — K.S.A. 50-6143(d).

**Enforcement:**
- Any violation of K.S.A. 50-6143 is a **deceptive act or practice under the Kansas Consumer Protection Act** — K.S.A. 50-6143(e).
- Penalties up to $10,000 per violation — K.S.A. 50-636.

### 7.3 AOB Risk Assessment: MODERATE

| Risk Factor | Level | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|
| Compliance complexity | Medium | 14-point font, 5-day cancellation, 3-day insurer notice create operational obligations |
| Voiding risk | **HIGH** | AOB void if roofing registration non-compliant or any statutory violation |
| Consumer protection exposure | **HIGH** | Violations are automatic KCPA violations; up to $10K per violation |
| Mortgagee interference | Low | Statute explicitly protects mortgagee interests |

## 8. Public Adjuster / Insurance Representation

### 8.1 Public Adjuster Licensing — K.S.A. 40-5501 et seq.

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **License Required** | No person shall act as a public adjuster unless licensed — K.S.A. 40-5503 |
| **Application Fee** | $100 — K.S.A. 40-5504 |
| **Examination** | Required (some exemptions) — K.S.A. 40-5504 |
| **Continuing Education** | 18 hours biennially, including 3 hours ethics |
| **Surety Bond** | "Reasonable amount" may be required — K.S.A. 40-5503 |
| **Record Retention** | Minimum five years after transaction |
| **Scope** | Limited to assisting insureds in **first-party claims only** — K.S.A. 40-5501 |

**Exemptions from Public Adjuster Licensing:**
- Attorneys
- Certain settlement negotiators
- Persons employed to obtain facts or provide technical assistance only
- Others as specified in K.S.A. 40-5503

### 8.2 Critical Pending Legislation: HB 2461 (Introduced January 2026)

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Summary** | Would **prohibit public adjusters from negotiating for or effectuating the settlement of any insurance claim relating to residential and personal lines of insurance** |
| **Impact** | Would significantly restrict public adjuster activity in the residential property market |
| **Status** | Introduced; referred to Committee on Insurance. Not yet enacted as of research date |

### 8.3 SmartContractor Implications

- SmartContractor and its contractor partners **MUST NOT** act as public adjusters or engage in activities requiring a public adjuster license.
- Negotiating with insurance companies on behalf of homeowners, interpreting policy coverage, or advising on claim values may constitute public adjusting.
- Contractors should limit their role to documenting damage, providing repair estimates, and performing repair work.
- **ANY claim negotiation or advocacy on behalf of homeowners should be referred to licensed public adjusters or attorneys.**

### 10.3 Final Risk Scores
