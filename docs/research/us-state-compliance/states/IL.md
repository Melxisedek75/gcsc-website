## Section 1 — Executive Summary & State Overview

Illinois presents a **complex and highly regulated environment** for SmartContractor deployment. The state enforces strict consumer lending laws with criminal penalties for unlicensed activity, imposes an absolute statutory prohibition on contractor involvement in insurance claims, maintains a heavily regulated public adjuster regime, and recently enacted comprehensive digital asset legislation creating a new registration obligation effective July 2027. All GCSC product lines face significant regulatory headwinds.

### Why Illinois Is Challenging for GCSC

- **Class 4 Felony for Unlicensed Consumer Lending**: The Consumer Installment Loan Act (205 ILCS 670) makes unlicensed consumer lending a felony and renders such loans null and void.
- **9% General Usury Cap**: Unless licensed under CILA or another exemption, consumer loans cannot exceed 9% APR (815 ILCS 205/4).
- **Contractor Insurance Claim Prohibition**: The Home Repair and Remodeling Act (815 ILCS 513/18) expressly bars contractors from filing claims or representing homeowners on insurance matters.
- **New Digital Asset Regulatory Regime**: The Digital Assets and Consumer Protection Act (Pub. Act 104-0428, signed Aug 2025) requires registration for "digital asset business activity" beginning July 1, 2027, with 1:1 reserve and custody obligations.
- **Strong Consumer Protection Framework**: The Consumer Fraud and Deceptive Business Practices Act (815 ILCS 505) provides broad private rights of action, attorney fee shifting, and enhanced penalties for elderly victims.

## Section 3 — Lending / Finance Licensing

### 3.1 Consumer Installment Loan Act (205 ILCS 670)

The CILA is the primary consumer lending statute in Illinois and represents the single greatest regulatory barrier to GCSC loan products.

**License Requirement**
- No person shall engage in the business of making loans and charge, contract for, or receive a greater APR than 9% without an IDFPR license (205 ILCS 670/1).
- **Class 4 Felony**: "Any person who engages in business as a Consumer Installment Loan lender without the license required by this Act shall be guilty of a Class 4 felony" (205 ILCS 670/20(a)).
- **Null and Void**: Unlicensed loans "shall be null and void and the person who made the loan shall have no right to collect, receive, or retain any principal, interest, or charges related to the loan" (205 ILCS 670/20(d)).

**License Parameters**
| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Application Fee | $300 |
| Annual Fee | $450 |
| Minimum Net Worth | $30,000 |
| Surety Bond | $25,000 |
| Rate Authority | Licensed rates above 9% general usury cap |
| Disclosure Requirements | Full TILA-style disclosure (amount financed, finance charge, APR, total of payments, payment schedule, security interest, prepayment terms) (205 ILCS 670/16) |

**Loan Brokering / Servicing**
- No separate loan broker license exists under Illinois law, but anyone arranging loans for compensation may be deemed engaged in the business of lending.
- **Key Risk**: Any GCSC arrangement that facilitates, brokers, or services loans to Illinois consumers likely requires CILA licensing unless structured through a licensed partner.

### 3.2 General Usury Cap (815 ILCS 205/4)

- **9% per annum** maximum on consumer loans unless licensed under CILA or another statutory exemption.
- Applies to any contract for the loan of money, goods, or things in action.

### 3.3 Payday Loan Reform Act (815 ILCS 122)

- Separate licensing regime for payday loans (short-term, small-dollar).
- Maximum loan: $500 per loan; $1,500 combined outstanding limit over any 30-day period.
- Licensed payday lenders may NOT obtain a CILA license except for title-secured loans (205 ILCS 670/1).
- **GCSC Relevance**: ClaimBridge-style advances could be characterized as payday loans if structured as short-term advances against imminent claim proceeds.

### 3.4 Commercial / Business-Purpose Loans

- The CILA applies to consumer-purpose loans. True commercial/business-purpose loans may fall outside the Act but require case-by-case analysis.
- **UNKNOWN REQUIRES COUNSEL**: Whether contractor working capital or equipment financing structured as commercial-purpose loans requires any IDFPR license.

### 3.5 Key Lending Risks for GCSC

| Risk | Severity | Statutory Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Unlicensed consumer lending | **CRITICAL** (Class 4 felony) | 205 ILCS 670/20(a) |
| Exceeding 9% usury cap | **CRITICAL** | 815 ILCS 205/4 |
| Loan nullity (unlicensed) | **CRITICAL** | 205 ILCS 670/20(d) |
| Arranging loans without license | **HIGH** | 205 ILCS 670/1 (implied) |
| Inadequate TILA-style disclosures | **HIGH** | 205 ILCS 670/16 |

## Section 4 — Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules

> **NEW SECTION** — This section addresses the specific regulatory framework for escrow arrangements that may underpin GCSC's proposed contractor advance / claim advance product mechanics in Illinois.

### 4.1 Escrow Regulatory Authority in Illinois

Escrow activities in Illinois are overseen by **IDFPR** through the Division of Financial Institutions. While Illinois does not have a standalone "escrow agent" licensing statute comparable to some states, escrow activity that is incidental to real estate transactions, lending, or money transmission falls under IDFPR's umbrella authority.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Primary Regulator** | IDFPR — Division of Financial Institutions |
| **Key Statute** | 205 ILCS 657 (Transmitters of Money Act) — escrow activities in connection with money transmission |
| **Escrow in Real Estate Transactions** | Governed by Illinois Real Estate License Act of 2000 (225 ILCS 454); only licensed managing brokers may hold escrow funds for real estate transactions |
| **Non-Real-Estate Escrow** | No standalone state escrow license; activities may be regulated as money transmission, trust activity, or lending depending on structure |

### 4.2 Escrow-Backed Advance Structures — Compliance Analysis

A GCSC product design in which **claim proceeds are directed into an escrow account** from which the contractor is paid as milestones are completed raises multiple Illinois regulatory questions:

**Scenario A: GCSC Holds Escrow Funds**
- If GCSC receives insurance claim proceeds on behalf of a homeowner and holds them for disbursement to a contractor, this activity may constitute:
  - **Money transmission** under 205 ILCS 657 if fiat currency is received and transmitted (though IDFPR guidance exempts pure virtual currency transmission).
  - **Digital asset business activity** under DACPA if tokenized escrow or digital assets are involved — registration required by July 1, 2027.
  - **Trust or fiduciary activity** potentially requiring IDFPR authorization.
- **Risk Level**: **HIGH** — Any entity holding third-party funds in Illinois requires clear statutory authority or licensing.

**Scenario B: Licensed Third-Party Escrow Agent Holds Funds**
- Using an IDFPR-authorized escrow agent or Illinois-licensed title company to hold claim proceeds significantly reduces GCSC regulatory exposure.
- GCSC would interact with the escrow arrangement through software/tracking only, without touching funds.
- **Risk Level**: **MEDIUM** — Lowest-risk pathway if GCSC does not hold, manage, or direct funds.

**Scenario C: Smart-Contract / Tokenized Escrow**
- If escrow functionality is implemented via smart contract (tokenized claim proceeds held in on-chain escrow with milestone-based release):
  - Likely constitutes "Digital Asset Business Activity" under DACPA (exchange, transfer, storage, or administration of digital assets on behalf of a customer).
  - DACPA **custody requirements** apply: 1:1 reserves, segregation of customer assets, prohibition on selling/transferring/pledging/lending customer digital assets except at customer direction (Pub. Act 104-0428).
  - **1:1 Reserve Requirement**: For every unit of digital asset held in escrow, the custodian must hold an equivalent unit — fractional reserve models are prohibited.
  - **Risk Level**: **HIGH** — DACPA registration, reserve, and custody obligations create significant compliance burden.

### 4.3 Milestone-Based Disbursement Rules

If an escrow-backed advance structure is implemented in Illinois (through a licensed escrow agent):

| Requirement | Source / Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Written escrow agreement** required | Illinois contract law; DACPA custody disclosures (eff. Jan 1, 2027) |
| **Consumer cancellation rights** must be preserved | 815 ILCS 513/18 (5-day / 13-day cancellation windows for insurance-paid contracts) |
| **No commingling** of escrow funds with operating funds | IDFPR Division of Financial Institutions guidance; DACPA segregation requirements |
| **Itemized disbursement schedule** tied to repair completion | Best practice under Home Repair and Remodeling Act (815 ILCS 513/15) |
| **Return of unused funds** to homeowner | 815 ILCS 513 consumer protection principles |
| **No deduction of GCSC fees** from escrow without clear authorization | 815 ILCS 505 (Consumer Fraud Act) |

### 4.4 Escrow-Backed Advance — Special Insurance-Paid Contract Rules

Under **815 ILCS 513/18**, contracts to be paid from insurance proceeds carry unique cancellation rights that directly impact escrow-backed advance structures:

> The consumer may cancel the contract prior to midnight on the earlier of:
> - The **5th business day** after receiving written notice from the insurer that the claim/contract is not a covered loss, OR
> - The **13th business day** after receipt of proof of loss by the insurer.

**Escrow Implication**: If claim proceeds have already been deposited into escrow and the homeowner exercises cancellation rights, the escrow must be capable of reversing disbursement and returning funds to the insurance carrier or homeowner as appropriate.

| Product Line | Escrow Structure Viability | Key Blocker |
|---|---|---|
| ClaimBridge (insurance claim advance) | **DEMO ONLY** | Contractor claim representation ban (815 ILCS 513/18); public adjuster advance prohibition (215 ILCS 5/1590); CILA licensing if advance = loan |

## Section 5 — Token Collateral / Digital Asset Regulation

### 5.1 Transmitters of Money Act (205 ILCS 657)

- **IDFPR Guidance (Pre-2025)**: Virtual currencies are NOT "money" under the TOMA. "Money" is defined as "a medium of exchange that is authorized or adopted by a domestic or foreign government as a part of its currency" (205 ILCS 657/5).
- **Result**: Transmission of solely digital currencies does NOT require a TOMA license.
- **Caution**: If a transaction involves exchanging digital assets for fiat currency or transmitting fiat currency, TOMA licensing may be triggered.

### 5.2 Digital Assets and Consumer Protection Act (DACPA) — Pub. Act 104-0428

Signed into law August 18, 2025, DACPA creates the most comprehensive digital asset regulatory framework in the Midwest.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Signed** | August 18, 2025 (SB 1797) |
| **Customer Protection Obligations** | Effective January 1, 2027 |
| **Registration Deadline** | July 1, 2027 |
| **Regulator** | IDFPR |

**Registration Requirement**
- Anyone engaging in "Digital Asset Business Activity" with Illinois residents must register with IDFPR.
- **"Digital Asset Business Activity"** includes: exchange, transfer, or storage of a Digital Asset as part of a business or on behalf of a customer; administration of Digital Assets; or other business activity involving Digital Assets as regulated by IDFPR.

**Custody Requirements (Critical for GCSC Collateral)**
- **1:1 Reserves**: Covered persons must maintain 1:1 reserves for each digital asset type.
- **Segregation**: Customer assets must be segregated from the custodian's own assets.
- **Prohibited Uses**: Selling, transferring, pledging, lending, or otherwise encumbering customer Digital Assets is prohibited **except at the customer's direction**.
- **Bankruptcy Protection**: Customer digital assets are NOT property of the custodian and are shielded from creditor claims.

**Exclusions from DACPA**
- Peer-to-peer exchanges, non-fungible tokens, certain DeFi activities.
- Personal/family/household use.
- Securities regulated by SEC; commodities regulated by CFTC.
- Merchants accepting digital assets for goods/services.

**Penalties**
| Violation | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Unregistered activity | Up to $100,000 per day |
| General violations | Up to $25,000 per day |
| Fraud / misrepresentation / negligence | Up to $75,000 per day |

### 5.3 Token Collateral Equipment Credit Status

| Status | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **TOKEN_COLLATERAL_BLOCKED** | Pending counsel review of DACPA applicability |
| **Key Question** | Whether token collateral lock/liquidation constitutes "Digital Asset Business Activity" |

## Section 6 — Insurance Claim Advance & Assignment of Benefits

### 6.1 Illinois Insurance Code — Claim Handling Requirements

| Requirement | Statutory Basis |
|---|---|
| **Unfair Claims Practices** | 215 ILCS 5/421-5/434 |
| **Timely Payment** | Insurers must affirm/deny within reasonable time; offer payment within 30 days of affirmation (50 Ill. Admin. Code 919.50) |
| **Interest on Late Claims** | 9% annual interest on claims not paid within 30 days of due proof of loss (215 ILCS 5/368a) |
| **Additional Living Expenses (ALE)** | Standard homeowners policies include ALE/loss of use coverage; generally payable on reimbursement basis |

### 6.2 Assignment of Benefits (AOB) — Property / Casualty

- **No Illinois statute expressly authorizing or prohibiting AOB for property/casualty insurance claims** was found.
- AOB is not prohibited per se for property insurance in Illinois.
- However, **contractor involvement in claims is heavily restricted** (see Section 8).

**AOB Status for GCSC: RESTRICTED**
- Contractors cannot file claims or represent homeowners on insurance claims (815 ILCS 513/18).
- Public adjusters are the only non-attorney professionals who can represent insureds on property claims, and they are heavily regulated.
- Any AOB arrangement that involves a GCSC entity or contractor negotiating with the insurer on behalf of the homeowner would likely violate 815 ILCS 513/18 and potentially constitute unauthorized public adjusting (215 ILCS 5/1515).

### 6.3 Health Insurance AOB (Not GCSC-Relevant but Illustrative)

- **215 ILCS 5/370a**: Assignment of accident and health insurance benefits is expressly permitted. If an insured assigns a claim to a health care professional or facility, payment shall be made directly to the assignee including any interest for late payment.
- This demonstrates Illinois knows how to expressly authorize AOB when it intends to — the absence of a property/casualty AOB statute, combined with the contractor ban, signals legislative intent to restrict claim intermediation.

### 6.4 Emergency Advance Payments

- No specific Illinois statute requires insurers to make emergency advance payments on property claims.
- Standard practice is that insurers may voluntarily issue advance payments, particularly for ALE.
- **UNKNOWN REQUIRES COUNSEL**: Whether Illinois law imposes any duty on insurers to advance funds for emergency repairs or ALE before full claim investigation.

### 6.5 Cancellation Rights for Insurance-Paid Contracts

Under **815 ILCS 513/18**, consumers who enter into contracts to be paid from insurance proceeds have special cancellation rights:

> The consumer may cancel prior to midnight on the earlier of:
> - The **5th business day** after receiving written notice from the insurer that the claim/contract is not a covered loss, OR
> - The **13th business day** after receipt of proof of loss by the insurer.

This provision directly impacts any GCSC product that ties contractor payment to insurance claim proceeds — the system must accommodate these statutory cancellation windows.

## Section 7 — Public Adjuster & Insurance Representation

### 7.1 Who May Negotiate with an Insurance Company on Behalf of a Homeowner

| Entity | Authority | Statutory Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Licensed public adjusters** | Yes — with IDOI license | 215 ILCS 5/Article XV-B |
| **Attorneys admitted in Illinois** | Yes — when acting in professional capacity | 215 ILCS 5/1515(d)(1) |
| **Insured / homeowner themselves** | Yes — always | Common law |
| **Contractors** | **NO — expressly prohibited** | 815 ILCS 513/18 |
| **GCSC / any non-licensed entity** | **NO** | 215 ILCS 5/1515(a) |

### 7.2 Public Adjuster Licensing Requirements (215 ILCS 5/1515-5/1615)

**License Required**
- No person shall act, advertise, solicit, or hold themselves out as a public adjuster without a license (215 ILCS 5/1515(a)).
- **All contracts entered into in violation are void and invalid** (215 ILCS 5/1515(e)).

**Exemptions**
- Attorneys; life/health claim negotiators; technical assistants (photographers, estimators, engineers); licensed health care providers preparing health claims; subrogation claim settlers (215 ILCS 5/1515(d)).

**Licensing Requirements**
| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Minimum Age | 18 |
| Examination | Public adjuster exam (Pearson VUE) |
| Residency | Illinois resident or designated home state |
| Surety Bond | $50,000 bond or irrevocable letter of credit (215 ILCS 5/1560) |
| License Fee | $250 (biennial) |
| Continuing Education | 24 hours per biennium (including 3 hours ethics) |
| Record Retention | 7 years minimum |

### 7.3 Standards of Conduct (215 ILCS 5/1590)

- Serve with objectivity and complete loyalty to insured's interests alone.
- **No solicitation** while loss is continuing, while fire department is engaged, or between 7:00 p.m. and 8:00 a.m.
- **No financial interest** in any aspect of the claim other than the contracted fee.
- **Cannot choose repair persons** for the insured.
- **Cannot advance money** except emergency services (215 ILCS 5/1590(a)(7)).
- **Cannot agree to settlement** without insured's knowledge and consent.
- **Cannot provide legal advice** or engage in unauthorized practice of law.
- **Cannot represent that their services are required** for the insured to submit a claim.

### 7.4 Fee Limitations

- **Non-residential property or catastrophic events**: Fee capped at 10% of insurance settlement unless approved in writing by IDOI Director (215 ILCS 5/1570(d)).
- **Residential / non-catastrophic**: No statutory cap found, but fees must be reasonable.

### 7.5 Critical Restriction: No Claim Advances

**215 ILCS 5/1590(a)(7)**: "A public adjuster shall not advance money or any valuable consideration, except emergency services to an insured pending adjustment of a claim."

This is a critical restriction for GCSC: **even licensed public adjusters cannot provide cash advances to insureds**. This directly blocks ClaimBridge-style claim advance products when public adjusters are involved.

### 7.6 Contractor / GCSC Must Not Act as Public Adjuster

- GCSC, its contractors, and any affiliated entities **MUST NOT** engage in activities that constitute public adjusting without proper licensing.
- Activities constituting public adjusting: negotiating with insurance companies on behalf of homeowners, advising homeowners on claim values, filing claims, challenging claim denials.
- **Violation Consequence**: Contracts are void and invalid; civil and criminal penalties may apply.

### 8.1 Contractor Licensing Structure

| Contractor Type | State License? | Local License? | Key Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| **General Contractors** | **NO** — not state-licensed | **YES** — municipal/county level | Local ordinances |

| **Plumbing Contractors** | **YES** — IDFPR | May also require local | 225 ILCS 320 |
| **Electrical Contractors** | **YES** — IDFPR | May also require local | 225 ILCS 327 |

### 8.2 Roofing Industry Licensing Act (225 ILCS 335)

**Two License Classes**
| Class | Scope | Bond Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Limited** | Residential roofing, 8 units or fewer | $10,000 surety bond |
| **Unlimited** | All types of roofing | $25,000 surety bond |

**Additional Requirements**: Exam, insurance, IDFPR application.

**Prohibition on Deductible Rebates**
- Roofing contractors are prohibited from advertising or promising to pay or rebate any insurance deductible.
- **First offense**: Class A misdemeanor (225 ILCS 335/9.8; 225 ILCS 335/10a).
- **Subsequent offense**: Class 4 felony.

### 8.3 Home Repair and Remodeling Act (815 ILCS 513)

Applies to home repair or remodeling work exceeding $1,000.

**Written Contract Requirements (815 ILCS 513/15)**
- Total cost with parts/materials itemized.
- Business name and address.
- Start and completion dates.
- Payment schedule.
- Termination provisions.

**Consumer Protections**
- **Consumer Rights Pamphlet** must be provided before contract signing (815 ILCS 513/20).
- **3-business-day right to cancel** for contracts signed at the homeowner's residence (815 ILCS 513/15).

**Insurance Requirements**
- Public liability and property damage insurance ($100,000/$300,000 bodily injury; $50,000 property damage) OR net worth of at least $1,000,000 (815 ILCS 513/25).

### 8.4 Contractor Prohibition on Insurance Claims (CRITICAL — 815 ILCS 513/18)

This is the single most important contractor-related statute for GCSC:

> **815 ILCS 513/18**: "A contractor offering home repair or remodeling services shall not call in or file a claim to an insurance carrier on the insured's behalf."

> **815 ILCS 513/18**: "A contractor offering home repair or remodeling services shall not represent, or offer or advertise to represent, on behalf of a homeowner on any insurance claim."

> **815 ILCS 513/18**: "A contractor offering home repair or remodeling services shall not advertise or promise to pay or rebate all or any portion of any insurance deductible as an inducement to the sale."

**Special Cancellation Rights (815 ILCS 513/18)**
- Consumer may cancel prior to midnight on the earlier of:
  - The 5th business day after receiving written notice from insurer that claim is not covered, OR
  - The 13th business day after receipt of proof of loss by the insurer.

**Violation Consequences**
- Any violation constitutes a violation of the **Consumer Fraud and Deceptive Business Practices Act** (815 ILCS 505).
- Civil penalties up to **$50,000 per violation**.
- Enforcement by Attorney General or State's Attorney.

## Section 9 — Mortgagee / Loss Draft & Escrow Notes

### 9.1 Mortgagee Protections

- Under standard homeowners policies, mortgagees are named as loss payees and have protected interests in insurance proceeds.
- **Illinois law**: A loss payee's recovery is limited to the outstanding mortgage balance at the time of payment, not the time of loss (federal court ruling under Illinois law, per Clausen analysis).

### 9.2 Loss Draft Check Procedures

- **No specific Illinois statute** governing loss draft check endorsement or escrow procedures was found.
- Standard industry practice (governed by mortgage contracts and federal guidelines — Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac): insurers issue joint-payee checks to homeowner and mortgagee for claims above certain thresholds.
- Mortgage servicers typically hold funds in escrow and disburse in stages as repairs are completed.

### 9.3 Escrow for Repair Disbursement

Mortgage servicers in Illinois follow standard industry practice for loss draft escrow:
- Initial inspection required before first disbursement.
- Funds released in increments (typically 1/3 at start, 1/3 at midpoint, 1/3 at completion).
- Final inspection and lien waiver required before final release.
- **GCSC Relevance**: If GCSC facilitates any part of this process, it must not hold, transfer, or control funds without appropriate licensing.

### 9.4 Insurance Cancellation — Mortgagee Notice

- **215 ILCS 5/143.17(d)**: All statutory and contractual restrictions requiring notice to mortgagee, lienholder, or other third party before cancellation apply where cancellation is effected under a premium finance agreement.

### 9.5 Key Risk for GCSC

| Risk | Severity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| GCSC holding or routing loss draft funds | **CRITICAL** | Likely constitutes money transmission or DACPA-regulated activity |

### 10.4 Final Risk Scores

| Risk Category | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Lending Risk** | **HIGH** | Unlicensed consumer lending is a Class 4 felony under CILA. Loans are null and void if made without a license. 9% APR cap applies unless licensed. Strict IDFPR oversight. |
| **Insurance Claim Risk** | **HIGH** | Contractors expressly prohibited from representing homeowners on claims (815 ILCS 513/18). Public adjusters cannot advance money. Complex overlap of contractor, adjuster, and lending laws. |
| **AOB Risk** | **HIGH** | While AOB is not per se prohibited for property claims, any arrangement where GCSC or a contractor negotiates with insurers risks violating the contractor claim representation ban and unauthorized public adjusting statutes. |
| **Public Adjuster Risk** | **HIGH** | Licensing required for any claim representation. $50,000 bond required. Strict conduct standards. Unlicensed activity voids contracts and may trigger criminal penalties. |
| **Token Collateral Risk** | **MEDIUM-HIGH** | DACPA creates comprehensive new regulatory regime effective 2025-2027. Registration required for digital asset business activity. Custody/segregation requirements may conflict with collateral lock/liquidation. Prior IDFPR guidance exempted pure virtual currency transmission from TOMA, but DACPA supersedes this framework. |
| **Escrow / Advance Risk** | **HIGH** | Escrow-backed advances require licensed escrow agent; GCSC cannot hold funds. 815 ILCS 513/18 cancellation windows must be preserved. Milestone verification without fund control is the only potentially viable pathway. |
| **Consumer Protection Risk** | **HIGH** | Illinois Consumer Fraud and Deceptive Business Practices Act (815 ILCS 505) provides broad private rights of action, attorney fee shifting, and punitive damages. Triple damages available for certain violations. Elderly victims (65+) subject to additional $10,000 per violation penalties. Home Repair and Remodeling Act violations automatically constitute Consumer Fraud Act violations. |
