**Key State Characteristics**: North Dakota has moderate-to-high regulatory complexity. The ND Insurance Department (insurance.nd.gov) regulates insurance, public adjusters, and residential contractor contracts. The ND Department of Financial Institutions (nd.gov/dfi) regulates money brokers, money transmission, and virtual currency. Contractor licensing is primarily local. The state is monopolistic for workers' compensation (WSI only). Post-loss assignment of benefits (AOB) is generally permitted to residential contractors under NDCC 26.1-39.2-04 with specific requirements. Public adjuster licensing is strictly enforced (NDCC 26.1-26.8). The 6% usury cap applies unless otherwise agreed.

## 2. Contractor / Home Improvement Rules

### Contractor Licensing
- Is a state general contractor license required? **Yes, for work over $4,000** — North Dakota Secretary of State administers contractor licensing under NDCC 43-07
- **License classes**:
  - Class A: Over $500,000 per job ($450 fee)
  - Class B: Up to $500,000 per job ($300 fee)
  - Class C: Up to $300,000 per job ($225 fee)
  - Class D: Up to $100,000 per job ($100 fee)
- **Threshold**: License required for any construction work valued over $4,000
- **Requirements**: Business registration, general liability insurance, WSI (workers' compensation) compliance
- **Renewal**: Annually by March 1
- **Penalties**: Class A misdemeanor for unlicensed contracting (NDCC 43-07-21)
- **Local licensing**: Many municipalities impose additional local licensing requirements beyond the state classification system
- **Source**: https://www.sos.nd.gov/business/licensing-registration/contractors

### Residential Contractor Contracts (NDCC 26.1-39.2)
Enacted 2019 (HB 1219) — applies to contractors performing roof system repair/replacement, exterior work, or cleanup services on residential real estate (1-4 families).

- **5-Day Cancellation Right**: Insured may cancel before midnight on the later of (a) 5th business day after entering contract, or (b) receiving notice from insurer that claim is not covered (NDCC 26.1-39.2-02)
- **Deductible Rebating Prohibited**: Residential contractors may NOT promise to rebate any portion of an insurance deductible as an inducement (NDCC 26.1-39.2-03)
- **Itemized Work Description Required**: Before commencing work, contractor must furnish insured AND insurer with itemized description of work, materials, labor, fees (NDCC 26.1-39.2-05)
- **Required Notice**: Every contract must include 14-point capitalized notice about deductible rebating prohibition (NDCC 26.1-39.2-06)
- **Violation**: Contract is VOID if contractor violates this chapter (NDCC 26.1-39.2-07)

### Workers' Compensation
- North Dakota is a **monopolistic state** — all employers must obtain coverage exclusively through Workforce Safety & Insurance (WSI)
- No private workers' compensation insurance available

## 3. Lending / Credit Rules

### Consumer / Homeowner Lending
- Is a consumer lending license required? **Yes** — under NDCC 13-04.1 (Money Brokers Act)
- **"Money Brokering" Definition**: "The act of arranging or providing loans or leases as a form of financing, or advertising or soliciting either in print, by letter, in person, or otherwise, the right to find lenders or provide loans or leases for persons or businesses desirous of obtaining funds for any purposes." (NDCC 13-04.1-01.1(5))
- **Scope**: Covers both consumer AND commercial lending; no dollar threshold (NDCC 13-04.1-02 FAQ)
- **APR Cap**: Finance charges may not exceed 36% per year (NDCC 13-04.1-09.2)
- **TILA Disclosures**: Required (NDCC 13-04.1-09.2)
- **Surety Bond**: $50,000 minimum (NDCC 13-04.1-03)
- **Net Worth**: $25,000 minimum (NDCC 13-04.1-04.2)
- **License Fees**: $400 initial application, $400 annual (NMLS)

### "Loan" Definition (HB 1127, effective August 1, 2025)
> "Loan" means a contract by which one delivers a sum of money to another and the latter agrees to return at a future time a sum equivalent to that which the person borrowed. This includes alternative financing products as identified by the commissioner through the issuance of an order. (NDCC 13-04.1-01.1(3))

### Exemptions from Money Brokers Act (NDCC 13-04.1-02.1)
- Banks, credit unions, savings and loan associations
- Insurance companies
- Residential mortgage lenders licensed under chapter 13-12
- Farm Credit Administration institutions
- Trust companies
- Licensed pawnbrokers
- Any other person or business regulated and licensed to lend money by ND
- Real estate brokers brokering loans for real estate sold
- Persons providing financing for their own property/inventory in normal course
- Certain nonprofit entities

### Usury (for non-regulated lenders)
- Maximum rate: 5.5% above average 6-month U.S. Treasury Bill rate (minimum 7%) (NDCC 47-14-09)
- Standard usury cap: 6% unless otherwise agreed
- Does NOT apply to lending institutions regulated by a state/federal agency
- Does NOT apply to loans over $35,000 or loans to corporations/LLCs

### Mortgage Lending
- Separate licensing required under NDCC 13-12 (NMLS)
- GCSC financing should NOT be structured as residential mortgage lending

### Payday/Deferred Presentment
- Separate license under NDCC 13-08
- Maximum fee: 20% of amount advanced
- Maximum outstanding: $600 per borrower
- Not applicable to GCSC products

## 4. Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules (NEW)

### Escrow Statutory Framework
- **Does North Dakota have a specific escrow-backed contractor advance statute?** No
- North Dakota has no statute specifically governing escrow-backed advances to contractors against future milestone or insurance claim payments
- The ND Department of Financial Institutions may regulate escrow activity depending on the nature of the escrow holder's activities
- Commercial lending and general contract law apply; UCC Article 9 governs secured transactions

### UCC Article 9 Applicability
- Advances secured by accounts, contract rights, or general intangibles fall under UCC Article 9 (NDCC Chapter 41-09)
- Perfection of security interest requires filing a UCC-1 financing statement with the North Dakota Secretary of State
- Priority determined by first-to-file or first-to-perfect rules
- The secured creditor exemption under NDCC 13-09.1-02(2)(g) references chapter 41-09 for enforcement of security interests in virtual currency

### Money Transmission Considerations
- NDCC 13-09.1 (Money Transmitters Act) regulates the receiving of money for transmission
- If an escrow-backed advance involves holding funds on behalf of others for disbursement upon completion of milestones, money transmission licensing may apply
- Escrow activities by attorneys and title companies are exempt from money transmission under NDCC 13-09.1-02(2)(c)

### Licensing Considerations for Escrow-Backed Advances
- **It is NOT CONFIRMED** whether an advance against future milestone payments or insurance claim proceeds constitutes "money brokering" under NDCC 13-04.1
- HB 1127 (eff. 8/1/2025) expanded the definition of "loan" to include "alternative financing products," potentially covering escrow-backed advances
- If structured as a direct loan, the Money Brokers Act applies with 36% APR cap
- If structured as a purchase of receivables (true sale), may fall outside lending licensure but requires legal analysis given HB 1127 expansion

### Key Implications for GCSC
- **Escrow-backed advance: UNKNOWN — legal review required**
- **STATUS**: Escrow-backed contractor advances against future insurance claim proceeds or contract milestones have NOT been confirmed as permissible without licensing in North Dakota
- Counsel must determine whether the advance structure constitutes:
  1. A loan requiring Money Brokers Act licensing
  2. Money transmission requiring licensing under NDCC 13-09.1
  3. A regulated escrow activity requiring DFI oversight
  4. A commercial factoring/purchase of receivables exempt from lending licensure
- **All escrow-backed advance operations: BLOCKED pending North Dakota-specific legal review**

## 5. Token Collateral / Digital Asset Risk

### Virtual Currency Business Activity Framework (NDCC 13-09.1)

North Dakota has a comprehensive regulatory framework for virtual currency business activity, housed within the Money Transmitters chapter.

- **License Required**: A person may not engage in "virtual-currency business activity" with or on behalf of another person unless licensed as a money transmitter or exempt (NDCC 13-09.1-46)
- **"Virtual Currency" Definition**: A digital representation of value that: (a) is used as a medium of exchange, unit of account, or store of value; and (b) is not money, whether or not denominated in money (NDCC 13-09.1-44(7))
- **"Virtual-Currency Business Activity" includes**: Exchange, transfer, and storage of virtual currency (NDCC 13-09.1-45)

### Key Exemptions Relevant to GCSC (NDCC 13-09.1-02(2))
- **(g) Secured Creditor**: "A secured creditor under chapter 41-09 or creditor with a judicial lien or lien arising by operation of law on collateral that is virtual currency, if the virtual-currency business activity of the creditor is limited to enforcement of the security interest in compliance with chapter 41-09 or lien in compliance with the law applicable to the lien"
- **(h) Virtual-Currency Control-Services Vendor**
- Other exemptions include personal/family use, $5,000 or less annual activity, attorneys/title companies providing escrow, securities/commodities intermediaries

### Property Interest Protections (NDCC 13-09.1-48)
- Virtual currency held for persons is NOT property of the licensee
- Not subject to claims of creditors of the licensee
- Deemed a permissible investment

### Disclosure Requirements (NDCC 13-09.1-47)
- Schedule of fees and charges
- Whether product/service is covered by insurance (FDIC/NCUA/SIPC)
- Irrevocability of transfers
- Liability for unauthorized transfers
- That virtual currency is not money

### Digital Asset Mining (2025 Legislation)
- ND enacted protections for digital asset mining in 2025 (HB 1513)
- Residential mining allowed if noise ordinances complied with
- Commercial mining businesses cannot be treated differently from other businesses in zoning

### Collateral / Liquidation Risk
- Accepting virtual currency as collateral for a loan may constitute "virtual-currency business activity" requiring money transmitter license
- Liquidation of token collateral likely requires money transmitter license unless secured creditor exemption applies
- Holding virtual currency on behalf of others as collateral may require money transmitter license
- Smart contract automation does not eliminate licensing requirements
- **All token collateral operations blocked pending money transmission analysis**

## 6. Insurance Claim Advance / ClaimBridge Risk

### Assignment of Benefits (AOB)
- Is AOB allowed, restricted, or prohibited? **REGULATED WITH SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS**
- North Dakota has specific legislation governing post-loss assignment of insurance benefits to residential contractors (NDCC 26.1-39.2-04, enacted 2019)
- A post-loss assignment may authorize a residential contractor to be named as a **copayee** for payment of benefits (NDCC 26.1-39.2-04(1))
- The assignment must be provided to the insurer within **5 business days** after execution (NDCC 26.1-39.2-04(2))

**Required Assignment Disclosures:**
- Must include statement that contractor made no assurances the loss will be fully covered
- Must include the following notice in **capitalized 14-point type** (NDCC 26.1-39.2-04(3)):

> "YOU ARE AGREEING TO ASSIGN CERTAIN RIGHTS YOU HAVE UNDER YOUR INSURANCE POLICY. THE ITEMIZED DESCRIPTION OF THE WORK TO BE DONE SHOWN IN THIS ASSIGNMENT FORM HAS NOT BEEN AGREED TO BY THE INSURER. PLEASE READ AND UNDERSTAND THIS DOCUMENT BEFORE SIGNING. THE INSURER MAY ONLY PAY FOR THE COST TO REPAIR OR REPLACE DAMAGED PROPERTY CAUSED BY A COVERED PERIL, SUBJECT TO THE TERMS OF THE POLICY."

**Assignment Limitations:**
- May not impair the interest of a mortgagee listed on the declarations page (NDCC 26.1-39.2-04(4))
- May not prevent or inhibit insurer from communicating with named insured or mortgagee (NDCC 26.1-39.2-04(5))
- Must include statement of insured's right to cancel (NDCC 26.1-39.2-04(6))

### Public Adjuster Restrictions
- **Licensing required** under NDCC 26.1-26.8 (enacted 2019)
- A "public adjuster" is a person who, for compensation, acts for or aids an insured in negotiating for or effecting the settlement of a **first-party claim** (NDCC 26.1-26.8-02(5))
- **License requirements**: Individual license through ND Insurance Department, $20,000 surety bond, examination, 24 hours biennial CE (3 hours ethics), background check
- **Critical prohibition**: A public adjuster may **NOT solicit or enter an agreement for the repair or replacement of damaged property** on which they have engaged to adjust or settle claims (NDCC 26.1-26.8-03)
- Willful unlicensed activity is a **Class C felony**
- GCSC and contractors must not negotiate insurance claims on behalf of homeowners

### Unfair Claim Settlement Practices (NDCC 26.1-04-03(9))
- Insurer must affirm or deny coverage within a "reasonable time" after proof of loss
- Must acknowledge communications with "reasonable promptness"
- Must adopt reasonable standards for prompt investigation
- Must attempt in good faith to effectuate prompt, fair, and equitable settlements where liability is clear
- Private right of action is unsettled in ND; pattern of practice required for regulatory action

### No Specific Insurance Claim Advance Statute
- North Dakota does not have a specific statute addressing "insurance claim advances" or "claim financing" as a distinct product
- Any claim advance product would likely be characterized as either:
  1. A loan (subject to Money Brokers Act, NDCC 13-04.1), or
  2. A purchase of receivables (which may now be characterized as a loan under HB 1127)
- **Claim financing BLOCKED pending comprehensive legal review of characterization and licensing requirements**

- Confirm money transmitter license before any virtual currency activity
- Confirm no claim negotiation is occurring
