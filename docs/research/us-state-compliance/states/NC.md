### Key Findings

- **Consumer lending** is regulated under the Consumer Finance Act (CFA), NCGS Chapter 53, Article 15, which requires licensure for non-depository lenders making consumer loans of 25,000 or less with APR exceeding the 16% usury limit. The CFA was significantly expanded effective October 1, 2023 to cover servicing as well as origination.
- **Money transmission** is regulated under the NC Money Transmitter Act (NCGS Chapter 53, Article 16B), which explicitly includes virtual currency transmission. A license is required for maintaining control of virtual currency on behalf of others.
- **Assignment of Benefits** for property insurance claims is **allowed at common law** in North Carolina (per *Alaimo Chiropractic v. Allstate*). Post-hurricane AOB reforms have been enacted. There is no comprehensive statutory framework governing property/casualty AOBs analogous to health insurance AOB rules, but reforms limit certain AOB practices.
- **Public adjusters** must be licensed under NCGS Chapter 58, Article 33A, carry a 20,000 surety bond, and serve with "objectivity and complete loyalty" to the client alone. Contractors and SmartContractor must not act as public adjusters.
- **General contractors** must be licensed for projects of 40,000 or more (NCGS Chapter 87, Article 1), with three license tiers based on project size.
- **Collection agencies** (including debt buyers) must be licensed by the NC Department of Insurance under NCGS Chapter 58, Article 70.
- North Carolina has a strong **Unfair and Deceptive Trade Practices Act** (NCGS 75-1.1) that provides private causes of action for consumers and applies broadly to commercial conduct.
- **Loss draft / mortgagee checks**: When a mortgage exists, insurance claim checks are typically made payable to both the homeowner and the mortgagee/lender, who controls disbursement through a loss draft process. NC mortgage servicers have duties to safeguard borrower funds under NCGS 53-244.110.

### Overall Assessment

All SmartContractor product features (token collateral equipment credit, ClaimBridge insurance claim advances, AOB facilitation, repayment routing) are **BLOCKED** pending legal review. North Carolina has explicit virtual currency money transmission licensing and comprehensive consumer lending regulation that would apply to many SmartContractor activities.

## 3. Lending / Finance Licensing Notes

### Consumer Finance Act (NCGS 53-164 to 53-191)

**Scope (as amended, effective October 1, 2023):**
- Applies to consumer loans of **25,000 or less** (increased from 15,000) with APR exceeding NC's statutory 16% usury limit (NCGS 24-1.1)
- Loans must be for consumer purpose, not secured by real estate
- Loans must be repayable in substantially equal consecutive monthly payments over at least 12 months but no more than 96 months (NCGS 53-176(a))
- **New as of October 1, 2023**: Also applies to servicers of such loans

**Interest Rate Limits for CFA Loans:**
- For loans not exceeding 10,000: 30% on first 4,000, 24% on next 4,000, 18% on remainder
- For loans exceeding 10,000: 18%
- Loans of 5,000+ to natural persons for personal/family/household purposes: APR cannot exceed 36% (NCGS 24-1.1(f))

**Licensing Requirement:**
- License required from NC Commissioner of Banks to engage in business of lending in amounts of 25,000 or less at rates exceeding the general usury limit
- Separate servicer license required as of October 1, 2023 for businesses receiving scheduled periodic payments from consumers on CFA loans
- Banks, trust companies, S&Ls, credit unions, pawnbrokers, industrial banks, and certain other entities are exempt (NCGS 53-191)

**Fees Allowed:**
- Processing fee of 25 for loans up to 2,500; 1% of cash advance for loans above 2,500, not to exceed 40 total (NCGS 53-176(b))
- Recording fees or non-filing insurance premium
- All other fees prohibited (NCGS 53-178)

**Key Restrictions:**
- Loan splitting prohibited
- Credit life, accident/health, unemployment, and credit property insurance permitted (NCGS 53-189)
- Substantially equal installments required
- Annual reporting required to Commissioner of Banks

### General Usury Law (NCGS Chapter 24)

- Legal rate of interest: **8%** (NCGS 24-1)
- Maximum contract rate for loans 25,000 or less: 16% or Treasury rate + 6%, whichever is greater (NCGS 24-1.1); has been 16% since 1984
- Loans over 25,000: any rate agreed upon in writing
- Home loans secured by first mortgage/deed of trust: 15% maximum (NCGS 24-1.1A)

### Commercial/Business-Purpose Loans

- The CFA applies to "consumer purpose" loans; business-purpose loans are generally outside CFA scope
- However, business-purpose loans may still be subject to general usury limits if 25,000 or less
- Business-purpose loans are exempt from federal TILA and RESPA but remain subject to NC UDTPA (75-1.1) and other state consumer protections
- **Note**: The determination of "consumer purpose" vs. "business purpose" is fact-specific. A loan to a contractor for working capital or equipment may qualify as business-purpose, but if the contractor is a sole proprietor or the loan is effectively for personal use, it could be deemed consumer-purpose.

### Loan Brokering / Servicing

- The CFA specifically covers loan servicing as of October 1, 2023
- Mortgage servicing is separately regulated under NCGS Chapter 53, Article 19 (N.C. Secure and Fair Enforcement Mortgage Licensing Act)
- Collection agency licensing required for third-party debt collection (NCGS Chapter 58, Article 70)

## 4. Consumer Protection, Escrow & Usury Enforcement (NEW)

### NC Commissioner of Banks Escrow Authority

The NC Commissioner of Banks possesses broad regulatory authority over escrow activities in the state:

- The Commissioner **may regulate escrow** arrangements involving financial services, particularly where such arrangements are incidental to lending or money transmission activities
- Mortgage servicers must handle escrow payments for insurance and taxes in compliance with NCGS 45-91
- Any SmartContractor product that holds or manages funds on behalf of consumers -- including insurance proceeds, repair deposits, or repayment reserves -- may trigger escrow regulation by the Commissioner of Banks
- Escrow activities that are not clearly exempt under the Mortgage Servicing Act or another statutory carve-out may require separate analysis and potential registration

### Unfair and Deceptive Trade Practices Act (UDTPA) -- NCGS 75-1.1

North Carolina's UDTPA is among the **broadest and most plaintiff-friendly** consumer protection statutes in the United States:

- Applies to any "unfair or deceptive act or practice in or affecting commerce"
- **No intent requirement**: A plaintiff need not prove the defendant intended to deceive
- **No pattern of conduct requirement**: A single incident can violate the Act
- **Private right of action** with treble (triple) damages and attorney fees available to prevailing plaintiffs
- "Unfair" conduct is defined broadly and includes practices that are unethical, oppressive, or injurious to consumers
- Applies to B2B transactions in many circumstances, not solely direct consumer transactions

**SmartContractor Implications:**
- All marketing claims, disclosures, and product terms must be reviewed for UDTPA compliance
- Interest rate calculations, fee structures, and collateral liquidation mechanisms must be transparent and accurately represented
- Any representation about insurance claim outcomes, timing, or amounts creates UDTPA exposure
- Smart contract terms that are opaque, misleading, or procedurally unfair may be challenged under 75-1.1

### 8% Usury Cap for Unlicensed Loans Under 25,000 (NCGS 24-1.1)

A critical trap for unlicensed lenders in North Carolina:

- The **general usury cap is 8%** per annum on loans where no contract rate is specified (NCGS 24-1)
- For written contract loans of 25,000 or less, the maximum contract rate is 16% (or Treasury rate + 6%, whichever is greater)
- **Loans under 25,000 made without a CFA license that charge more than 8% APR risk usury challenges** unless a valid written contract rate up to 16% is established
- Any loan to a consumer that exceeds the applicable usury limit may be void as to principal and interest, and the lender may forfeit all interest
- **Willful usury** (knowing violation) can result in criminal penalties and treble damages under related statutes

**SmartContractor Implications:**
- If SmartContractor makes or facilitates any consumer-purpose loan of 25,000 or less, the APR must not exceed applicable limits without a CFA license
- Interest rate calculations in smart contracts must account for all charges that could be deemed interest under NC law
- The 8%/16% usury framework applies regardless of whether the loan is fiat-denominated or token-denominated

### Post-Hurricane AOB Reforms

North Carolina has enacted reforms following hurricane events that affect assignment of benefits and insurance claim practices:

- While NC does not have a comprehensive AOB statute comparable to Florida's, **post-hurricane AOB reforms** have been implemented to address contractor abuse and consumer harm
- These reforms generally target: excessive AOB fee arrangements, contractors who inflate claims through AOB structures, and lack of transparency in AOB contracts
- SmartContractor must monitor ongoing legislative activity for additional AOB restrictions, particularly during and after hurricane seasons
- Any AOB facilitation product must account for the current reform environment and build in compliance safeguards against the practices targeted by reforms

### High Regulation Summary for Consumer Lending

North Carolina is a **high-regulation jurisdiction** for consumer lending:

| Factor | NC Treatment |
|--------|-------------|
| CFA License Required | Yes, for consumer loans 25K at >16% APR |
| Servicer License | Yes, as of October 2023 |
| Usury Limit | 8% legal; 16% contract max for 25K loans |
| Exemptions | Narrow; banks, CUs, certain industrial banks only |
| Fee Restrictions | Strict: limited processing fee, recording fees only |
| UDTPA Exposure | Very high: treble damages, attorney fees, no intent required |
| Criminal Penalties | Possible for willful usury and unlicensed lending |

## 5. Contractor Licensing & Financing Notes

### Contractor Licensing Requirements (NCGS Chapter 87, Article 1)

- **License required** for any person, firm, or corporation who contracts for construction projects of **40,000 or more** involving buildings, highways, public utilities, grading, or improvements
- Three classifications: Building contractor, Highway contractor, Public utilities contractor
- License limitations:
  - **Limited**: individual projects up to 750,000
  - **Intermediate**: individual projects up to 1,500,000
  - **Unlimited**: no dollar limit
- Must be at least 18 years of age, good moral character, evidence of financial responsibility
- Must identify a "qualifier" who has passed the Board examination
- License expires January 1 each year
- **No insurance requirement** for licensing itself, though individual projects may require it
- **No bonding requirement** for the license itself (unless using surety bond in lieu of financial requirements)

### Contractor Financing Implications

- Equipment financing and working capital loans for licensed contractors are typically structured as **business-purpose loans**
- Non-depository lenders should confirm the business-purpose exemption from the CFA
- Interest rate on business-purpose loans over 25,000 can be contractually agreed without CFA limits
- Loans 25,000 or less still subject to 16% usury limit unless structured as exempt transaction
- Contract-specific financing arrangements (e.g., material purchase financing) may fall under Retail Installment Sales Act (RISA) rather than CFA
- **SmartContractor equipment credit product** to contractors: if structured as a true business-purpose loan to a licensed entity, may fall outside CFA scope; if to an individual or sole proprietor, CFA may apply

## 6. Token Collateral / Crypto / Money Transmission Notes

### North Carolina Money Transmitter Act (NCGS 53-208.42 et seq.)

North Carolina is one of the few states with **explicit virtual currency regulation** integrated into its money transmission framework:

**Virtual Currency Definition (NCGS 53-208.42):**
- "Virtual currency" means a digital representation of value that can be digitally traded and functions as a medium of exchange, a unit of account, or a store of value but does not have legal tender status as recognized by the United States Government.

**Money Transmission Includes Virtual Currency (NCGS 53-208.42):**
- Money transmission includes "maintaining control of virtual currency on behalf of others."
- Virtual currency transmission is expressly within the scope of the MTA.

**Licensing Requirement (NCGS 53-208.43):**
- No person shall engage in the business of money transmission in NC without a license
- A person is considered engaged in money transmission if they solicit or advertise such services from a website accessible to NC citizens
- Activities authorized under the license include "Virtual Currency exchange or holding"

**Key Exemptions (NCGS 53-208.44):**
- Virtual currency **miners** are exempt
- **Blockchain 2.0 technologies** (smart contracts, smart property, colored coins) are NOT regulated by the MTA if they do not involve virtual currency as a medium of exchange
- **Multi-signature software providers** are exempt (cannot authorize transaction alone)
- **Non-custodial/non-hosted wallet providers** are exempt
- **Payee agents**: A person appointed by a payee to collect and process payments as the bona fide agent of the payee, for transactions conducted in whole or in part in virtual currency, are exempt
- **Software companies** implementing blockchain services are exempt

**Special Requirements for Virtual Currency Licensees:**
- Virtual currency counts as permissible investment (PI) only to the extent it covers outstanding transmission obligations received in like-kind virtual currency (NCCOB FAQ)
- Licensee must have established procedures for examiners to verify accuracy of reported virtual currency holdings
- NCCOB may request verification of aggregate virtual currency transmission obligations and holdings at any time
- **Additional insurance/surety bond coverage** may be required to address "cybersecurity risks inherent in the applicant's business model" (NCGS 53-208.47)
- Virtual currency kiosks/ATMs require MTA license

**Permissible Investments and Virtual Currency:**
- Virtual currency is allowed as PI only to the extent it covers outstanding transmission obligations received in like-kind virtual currency
- Must be maintained in an account located in the United States
- Virtual currency held without corresponding customer liability is treated as intangible asset and not sufficient capital

### Implications for SmartContractor Token Collateral

| Aspect | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **Token collateral lock** (holding crypto as collateral) | Likely constitutes "maintaining control of virtual currency on behalf of others" -- MTA license required |
| **Token liquidation** (selling collateral to recover loan) | May constitute virtual currency transmission -- MTA license required |
| **Smart contract-only operations** (no custody) | If SmartContractor uses non-custodial smart contracts where users retain private keys, may fall outside MTA scope |
| **Multi-sig arrangements** | If SmartContractor holds only one key in a multi-sig setup, may qualify for exemption |
| **Business-purpose vs. consumer** | NC MTA limits scope largely to transmissions for "personal, family, or household purposes" -- pure B2B may be exempt |

**CRITICAL DETERMINATION**: Whether SmartContractor's token collateral mechanism requires an NC Money Transmitter license depends heavily on the technical architecture:
- If SmartContractor takes custody of tokens (holds private keys or equivalent control): **MTA license likely required**
- If SmartContractor uses non-custodial smart contracts (users retain control): **may fall under exemption for Blockchain 2.0 / non-custodial wallets**
- If any repayment routing involves virtual currency transmission on behalf of consumers: **MTA license likely required**

**STATUS: TOKEN_COLLATERAL_UNKNOWN_REQUIRES_COUNSEL_REVIEW** -- The applicability of the MTA to SmartContractor's specific token collateral model depends on technical architecture decisions that must be reviewed by NC-counsel familiar with both the MTA and the specific smart contract design.

## 7. Insurance Claims & ClaimBridge Notes

### Property Insurance Claim Payment Process in NC

**Prompt Payment:**
- NCGS 58-3-225 requires insurers to pay or deny health benefit claims within 30 days of receipt; property/casualty claims have general 60-day payment expectation per NC DOI guidance
- Interest accrues on late claim payments
- Insurer must provide status reports to insured if claim unpaid after 60 days

**Additional Living Expenses (ALE):**
- ALE coverage is standard in most NC homeowners policies
- Triggered when covered loss makes residence "not fit to live in"
- Covers necessary increase in living expenses to maintain normal standard of living
- ALE is **reimbursement-based** -- covers expenses actually incurred, not advance payments
- Typical limits: time-limited (12-24 months) or dollar-limited depending on policy
- ALE does NOT cover normal living expenses (mortgage, property taxes)
- ALE payments require receipts and documentation

**Emergency Advance Payments:**
- Some insurers offer advance payments against the total settlement as a goodwill gesture
- These are advances against the final claim amount, not separate payments
- Advances typically require proof of loss and are deducted from final settlement
- No statutory right to an advance payment; insurer discretion
- NFIP/FEMA advance payments (for flood): up to 5,000 pre-inspection, up to 50% of contractor estimate post-inspection (per Hurricane Matthew guidance)

**Claim Proceeds and Mortgages:**
- When a mortgage exists, claim checks are typically payable to both the insured and the mortgagee
- Mortgagee/lender has rights under the deed of trust to control disbursement
- Loss draft process: lender holds funds in escrow, releases in draws as repairs progress
- If loan is delinquent, servicer may refuse to release funds or apply to loan balance
- NC mortgage servicers must act with reasonable skill, care, and diligence (NCGS 53-244.110)

### Standard Loss Draft Process in NC

When a mortgaged property suffers insured damage:

1. **Insurance claim check** is typically made payable to:
   - The insured homeowner(s)
   - The mortgagee/lender (listed as "ISAOA/ATIMA" -- Its Successors and/or Assigns, As Their Interest May Appear)

2. **All parties must endorse the check** before funds can be accessed

3. **Mortgagee controls disbursement** through loss draft process:
   - Lender cashes check after confirming ISAOA/ATIMA verbiage
   - Funds held in escrow/repair account
   - Released in draws as repairs are completed
   - Inspection typically required before final release

4. **Monitored claims** (typically over 40,000 or delinquent loans):
   - Stricter oversight
   - Multiple inspections
   - Documentation requirements (contractor W-9, license, insurance, estimates)
   - Funds released in staged payments tied to completion

5. **Delinquent loan status**:
   - Servicer may apply insurance proceeds to loan balance instead of repairs
   - Loss draft department may refuse to release funds
   - Foreclosure process may supersede repair needs

### Relevant NC Statutes

- **NCGS 53-244.110** (Mortgage servicer duties): Requires licensed servicers to safeguard and account for money handled for the borrower; act with reasonable skill, care, and diligence; communicate in good faith about delinquency/default
- **NCGS 45-91** (Escrow handling on home loans): Addresses how servicers must handle escrow payments for insurance and taxes
- **NCGS 45-102** (Pre-foreclosure notice for home loans): Requires 45-day written notice before filing foreclosure on primary residence

### Implications for ClaimBridge

Any product offering insurance claim advances, ALE financing, or claim proceeds-based repayment must consider:
- Insurance claim proceeds are uncertain and subject to coverage disputes
- Mortgagee involvement creates additional complexity for direct payment
- ALE is reimbursement-based, not an automatic entitlement
- Advances from insurers are discretionary and deducted from final settlement
- **SmartContractor CANNOT** guarantee claim proceeds will be available to repay any advance
- SmartContractor cannot bypass mortgagee's security interest in insurance proceeds
- Any repayment mechanism relying on direct receipt of insurance proceeds must account for mortgagee involvement
- Mortgagee may refuse to endorse check until repairs are documented
- If loan is delinquent, insurance proceeds may never reach the homeowner or contractor
- **Repayment routing from insurance proceeds is highly uncertain when a mortgage exists**

## 8. Assignment of Benefits (AOB) Notes

### AOB Status in North Carolina: ALLOWED AT COMMON LAW; NO SPECIFIC PROPERTY AOB STATUTE

**Legal Basis:**
- North Carolina courts recognize the validity of insurance policy benefit assignments under general contract law principles (*Alaimo Chiropractic v. Allstate*, 2007)
- An assignment is "a transfer of property or some other right from one person to another, which confers a complete and present right in the subject matter to the assignee"
- Insurance policy benefits are contractual rights belonging to the insured and are assignable
- The leading case: *Alaimo Chiropractic v. Allstate* -- court held assignment of proceeds valid and obligated insurer to acknowledge assignee's rights

**Health Insurance AOB (NCGS 58-3-225):**
- Health benefit plans are subject to specific AOB rules under the prompt pay statute
- Definition of "claimant" includes "a health care provider or facility that is responsible or permitted under contract with the insurer or by valid assignment of benefits"
- This statute is specific to **health insurance** and does not govern property/casualty claims

**Property/Casualty AOB:**
- **No specific statute** governing assignment of property insurance benefits was found
- General contract law principles apply
- AOB is a contract between the homeowner and the contractor/assignee
- Insurer's duty to recognize valid assignment once notice is provided
- Assignment can transfer the right to receive payment directly from the insurer

**Post-Hurricane AOB Reforms:**
- North Carolina has enacted **post-hurricane AOB reforms** to address contractor abuse following storm events
- These reforms limit certain AOB practices, particularly around fee structures and claim inflation
- Unlike Florida, which enacted extensive AOB reform, NC's reforms are more targeted but still significant
- The NC General Assembly continues to evaluate additional AOB restrictions
- General contract law defenses (fraud, duress, unconscionability) may apply to invalidate abusive AOBs

**Required Elements for Valid AOB (General Contract Law):**
- Clear intent to assign
- Identification of the rights assigned
- Notice to the obligor (insurer) preferred but not always required for validity
- Consideration
- No statutory font-size, cancellation window, or form requirements for property AOBs

**Risks and Considerations:**
- AOB transfers the homeowner's right to payment to the contractor
- Homeowner may lose control over the claim negotiation process
- Contractor may accept lower settlement than homeowner could obtain independently
- If contractor and insurer dispute scope/value, homeowner may be entangled
- AOB does NOT transfer the duty to perform repairs -- contractor still must perform
- AOB + contractor acting as claim negotiator = potential public adjuster licensing issue

### SmartContractor AOB Implications

SmartContractor facilitating AOB arrangements between homeowners and contractors raises:
1. **Contractual validity** -- general contract law applies
2. **Public adjuster concern** -- if SmartContractor or contractor assists with claim negotiation, this may require public adjuster licensing
3. **Insurance regulation** -- NC DOI may view facilitating AOBs as an insurance-related activity
4. **Consumer protection** -- UDTPA (75-1.1) could apply if AOB process is deceptive or unfair
5. **Post-hurricane reform compliance** -- AOB facilitation must comply with post-hurricane restrictions on fee arrangements and claim inflation practices

**STATUS: AOB_ALLOWED_BUT_REGULATED -- No specific statute prohibits property insurance AOBs in NC, but common law contract principles apply, post-hurricane reforms limit certain practices, and significant public adjuster/licensing risks exist.**

## 9. Public Adjuster & Insurance Representation Notes

### Licensing Requirements (NCGS Chapter 58, Article 33A)

**Who Must Be Licensed:**
- Any person who acts or holds themselves out as a public adjuster in NC (NCGS 58-33A-10)
- Business entities acting as public adjusters must also obtain a license
- **Exclusions**: Attorneys-at-law representing clients in the course of their practice; insurance company employees; independent adjusters representing insurers

**License Requirements:**
- Individual license application
- **20,000 surety bond** (NCGS 58-33A-50)
- Complete record of each transaction maintained (NCGS 58-33A-75)
- Serve with "objectivity and complete loyalty the interest of his or her client alone" (NCGS 58-33A-80)
- License renewal required; remains effective unless revoked, terminated, or suspended

**What Public Adjusters Can Do:**
- Investigate, negotiate, and settle claims on behalf of insureds
- Advise and assist the insured in presenting claims
- Prepare estimates and inventories
- Obtain repairs/replacement cost estimates
- Advise on rights and obligations under the policy

**Restrictions:**
- Cannot have any direct or indirect financial interest in the outcome of a claim other than the agreed compensation (NCGS 58-33A-80)
- Compensation typically limited to a percentage of the claim settlement
- Cannot act as both contractor and public adjuster on the same claim (ethics concern)
- Must serve "the interest of his or her client alone" -- conflicts of interest prohibited

### Critical Distinction: Contractor vs. Public Adjuster

A contractor who:
- **Repairs damage** = performing normal contractor services (license under Ch. 87)
- **Negotiates with insurance company on scope/value of claim** = may be acting as public adjuster (requires license under Ch. 58, Art. 33A)
- **Prepares damage estimates for insurance purposes** = potentially public adjuster activity if done to negotiate claim

**NAPIA (National Association of Public Insurance Adjusters) position**: Contractors should not act as public adjusters. NAPIA prohibits its members from being contractors who do this type of work.

### SmartContractor Implications

SmartContractor and its contractor network **MUST NOT**:
- Negotiate with insurance companies on behalf of homeowners
- Prepare claim estimates for the purpose of insurance negotiation unless licensed as public adjusters
- Hold themselves out as able to "maximize" or "handle" insurance claims
- Accept compensation based on insurance claim outcomes (contingency fees)

SmartContractor **CAN**:
- Provide factual documentation of work performed
- Provide repair estimates for work to be done
- Facilitate communication between homeowner and insurer (without negotiating)
- Educate homeowners about the claim process generally

**ANY CLAIM NEGOTIATION ACTIVITY MUST BE REFERRED TO A LICENSED PUBLIC ADJUSTER OR THE HOMEOWNER'S ATTORNEY.**

### Smart Contract Specific Risks in NC

1. **Money Transmitter Act**: Smart contracts that hold virtual currency on behalf of users may trigger MTA licensing. Non-custodial designs where users retain private key control may qualify for the Blockchain 2.0 / software exemption.

2. **Consumer Finance Act**: If smart contract-facilitated loans are 25,000 or less to consumers at >16% APR, CFA license required. Interest rate calculation must comply with NC tiered rate structure. Unlicensed loans under 25,000 risk violating the 8% usury cap.

3. **UDTPA (75-1.1)**: Smart contract terms that are deceptive, unfair, or unconscionable may violate NC's broad consumer protection statute. Treble damages and attorney fees available to prevailing plaintiffs. No intent or pattern requirement.

4. **Collection Agency Act**: Automated repayment collection through smart contracts should be reviewed to ensure it does not constitute unlicensed collection agency activity.

5. **NC Commissioner of Banks Escrow Authority**: Smart contracts that hold consumer funds in escrow-like arrangements -- including insurance proceeds, repair deposits, or repayment reserves -- may be subject to the Commissioner's escrow regulation authority.
