# Idaho (ID) State Compliance

## 1. State Summary

Idaho presents a **mixed regulatory environment** for SmartContractor products. The state generally permits post-loss Assignment of Benefits (AOB) for property insurance claims, does not have specific AOB restrictions (a 2021 bill to regulate AOB failed), classifies digital assets as intangible personal property, and requires contractor registration rather than a traditional general contractor license. Public adjusters are separately licensed. Consumer lending is regulated under the Idaho Credit Code (UCCC-based), with business-purpose loans generally exempt. Token and cryptocurrency activities fall under the Idaho Money Transmitters Act (Title 26, Chapter 31). Escrow activities are regulated by the Idaho Department of Finance. All SmartContractor live product features remain **BLOCKED** pending legal review.

**Key Idaho Characteristics:**
- **AOB Status**: Generally permitted for post-loss property claims; no specific Idaho statute restricting AOB
- **SB 1057 (2021)**: "Insured Homeowner Protection Act" FAILED in Senate (11-24); would have restricted AOB
- **Digital Assets**: Classified as intangible personal property under Idaho Digital Asset Act (2022); Idaho has NOT adopted UCC Article 12 (2022 CER amendments)
- **Public Adjusters**: Licensed under Title 41, Chapter 58; $20,000 bond required; contractors cannot act as public adjusters without separate license
- **Consumer Lending**: Idaho Credit Code applies; regulated lender license required for consumer loans; business-purpose loans generally exempt; 12% usury cap applies to loans under $1,000 unless licensed
- **Escrow**: Regulated through the Idaho Department of Finance; escrow agents handling contractor advances must hold appropriate licensure
- **Money Transmission**: Idaho Code Title 26, Chapter 31; virtual currency exchanges considered money transmitters
- **Crypto Stance**: Idaho has been relatively crypto-neutral, recognizing digital assets as property without expansive restrictive frameworks

## 3. Lending / Finance Licensing Notes

### Idaho Credit Code (Title 28, Chapters 41-49)
- Idaho has adopted the Uniform Consumer Credit Code (UCCC)
- **Regulated Lender License** required for making consumer loans (Idaho Code 28-46-301 et seq.)
- Licensed by the Idaho Department of Finance
- **Application requirements**: $30,000 minimum liquid assets; sample forms; history of lending; registration with Idaho Secretary of State
- License renewed annually
- **Business/commercial/agricultural purpose loans** are generally **exempt** from the Idaho Credit Code
- Loans secured by first mortgage/deed of trust on real property are considered business purpose and exempt

### Key Exemptions (Idaho Code 28-41-202, 28-41-204)
- Transactions for business, commercial, or agricultural purposes
- Organizations (non-natural persons) borrowing
- Loans secured by first mortgage on real property
- Credit extended to government entities

### Usury and Rate Limits
- **12% usury cap** for loans under $1,000 unless the lender holds a regulated lender license (Idaho Code 28-22-104)
- For licensed regulated lenders, various rate structures are permitted under the Idaho Credit Code
- Unlicensed payday lending capped at $15 per $100 borrowed (Idaho Code 47-33-102)
- 60% APR ceiling referenced for certain consumer credit (Idaho Code 28-31-402)
- Violation of the 12% usury cap on unlicensed small loans may result in forfeiture of all interest and potential criminal penalties

### Implications for SmartContractor
- Equipment credit/loans to **business entities** (contractors, restoration companies) may be exempt from ICC if structured as business-purpose loans
- Consumer-purpose loans to **homeowners** would require a regulated lender license or partnership with a licensed lender
- Any advance under $1,000 to an individual consumer must comply with the 12% usury cap unless SmartContractor or its partner holds a regulated lender license
- **Commercial/business purpose exemption is critical for SmartContractor contractor lending products**
- Partnering with a licensed Idaho lender is recommended for any consumer-facing advance products

## 4. Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules

### Idaho Escrow Regulation Overview
- Escrow activities in Idaho are regulated by the **Idaho Department of Finance**
- **Escrow Agent Licensing**: Persons engaged in the business of handling escrows must hold an escrow agent license under Idaho Code Title 26, Chapter 29
- Exemptions from escrow licensing may apply to certain financial institutions and title companies operating under other regulatory frameworks

### Escrow Agent Licensing Requirements
- Application filed with the Idaho Department of Finance
- Minimum net worth and surety bond requirements apply
- Fidelity bond and errors-and-omissions coverage required
- Annual reporting and compliance obligations
- Examinations by the Department of Finance may occur

### Escrow-Backed Contractor Advances
- If SmartContractor uses an escrow structure to hold contractor advance funds, the escrow arrangement must comply with Idaho escrow law
- **Escrow agents holding advance funds** for distribution to contractors must be properly licensed or exempt
- **Key compliance points for escrow-backed advances:**
  1. The escrow holder must be a licensed escrow agent or a statutorily exempt entity (e.g., chartered bank, title insurer)
  2. Escrow instructions must clearly define disbursement conditions tied to verifiable completion milestones
  3. All funds must be maintained in a segregated, non-commingled escrow account
  4. Escrow fees must be disclosed to all parties
  5. Record retention requirements apply under Department of Finance rules

### Disbursement Conditions and Milestone Verification
- Escrow disbursement triggers should be tied to:
  - Lien releases from subcontractors and material suppliers
  - Inspection sign-offs by licensed inspectors where required
  - Homeowner acknowledgment of satisfactory work completion
  - Compliance with Idaho contractor registration requirements
- SmartContractor should not unilaterally control disbursement; a neutral escrow agent or tripartite agreement is recommended

### Prohibited Practices in Escrow-Backed Contractor Transactions
- Commingling escrow funds with operating funds
- Disbursing funds before securing required lien releases
- Failing to maintain fidelity bond or E&O coverage at statutory minimums
- Acting as an escrow agent without a license (unless exempt)
- Delaying disbursement beyond contractually agreed milestones without cause

### SmartContractor Escrow Implications
| Factor | Status |
|--------|--------|
| Escrow agent license required | Yes, for non-exempt entities |
| Bank exemption available | Yes, if a chartered depository institution holds funds |
| Segregated account requirement | Yes |
| Milestone-based disbursement | Recommended best practice |
| Lien release verification | Required before final disbursement |
| Criminal penalty for unlicensed escrow | Possible under Idaho Code |

**ESCROW_REQUIRES_LICENSED_AGENT_OR_BANK_PARTNER** - SmartContractor must either partner with a licensed Idaho escrow agent, a chartered bank, or a title company exempt from escrow licensing to hold contractor advance funds in escrow. An unlicensed escrow arrangement would violate Idaho law.

## 5. Contractor Licensing & Financing Notes

### Idaho Contractor Registration
- Idaho requires **registration** (not a traditional "license") for general contractors with the Idaho Contractors Board (part of DOPL)
- **Who must register**: Anyone performing construction work in Idaho; exemption for projects under $2,000
- **Requirements**: $50 application fee; general liability insurance ($300,000 minimum); workers' compensation insurance (if employees); photo ID
- **Renewal**: Biennial, $100
- Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC contractors are licensed through the **Idaho Division of Building Safety** and must employ licensed tradespeople

### Contractor Financing
- No specific Idaho statute prohibiting financing to contractors
- Business-purpose loans to contractors likely exempt from Idaho Credit Code
- Equipment financing and working capital advances to contractors should be structured as commercial transactions
- **12% usury cap reminder**: Any contractor advance that could be recharacterized as a consumer loan under $1,000 is subject to the usury cap unless made by a licensed regulated lender

### Disclosure Requirements
- Contractors must disclose registration status
- Insurance certificates must be maintained
- No specific financing disclosure requirements found for contractor-to-homeowner transactions under Idaho law
- However, business-purpose loans should include clear documentation that the loan is for commercial purposes

### Lien Law Considerations
- Idaho's mechanics' lien law (Title 45, Chapter 5) allows contractors, subcontractors, and material suppliers to file liens against real property for unpaid work
- Preliminary notice requirements apply for subcontractors and suppliers
- Lien waivers are standard practice in contractor financing and escrow disbursement workflows
- SmartContractor should ensure lien releases are obtained before final disbursement of escrowed funds

## 6. Token Collateral / Crypto Notes

### Idaho Digital Asset Act (HB 583, signed March 2022)
- Amends Title 28 of Idaho Code
- **Digital assets classified as intangible personal property** (Idaho Code 28-5304)
- Digital securities and virtual currencies covered
- Does NOT include CBDCs in the definition

### Idaho Money Transmitters Act (Title 26, Chapter 31)
- **Virtual currency exchanges** fall under money transmission requiring a license
- "Money transmission" includes sale/issuance of payment instruments or receiving money for transmission
- Minimum net worth: $50,000 ($25,000 per additional location, max $250,000)
- Bond: $10,000 base + $5,000 per location (max $500,000)
- **No-action letters issued** for: selling own inventory of virtual currency; crypto ATMs selling own inventory; exchanges with no fiat currency involvement
- Idaho has been relatively **crypto-neutral**, neither aggressively restricting nor broadly embracing cryptocurrency activities beyond standard money transmission frameworks

### UCC Treatment
- Idaho has **NOT adopted the 2022 UCC Article 12 amendments** (Controllable Electronic Records) as of research date
- Idaho instead enacted its own **Digital Asset Act** (non-UCC approach)
- Digital assets treated as **general intangibles** under pre-2022 UCC Article 9
- Perfection of security interest in digital assets by **filing UCC-1 financing statement** with Idaho Secretary of State
- **Perfection by "control" (UCC Article 12 method) NOT available in Idaho** as of research date

### SmartContractor Token Collateral Assessment
| Factor | Status |
|--------|--------|
| Digital asset as property | Recognized as intangible personal property |
| Token collateral lock | Technically possible via smart contract |
| UCC perfection | By filing only (no control perfection) |
| Liquidation of collateral | Smart contract enforcement uncertain; may require judicial process |
| Money transmitter license | May be required if SmartContractor handles fiat conversion |

**TOKEN_COLLATERAL_UNKNOWN_REQUIRES_COUNSEL_REVIEW** - Idaho recognizes digital assets as property and they can serve as collateral under UCC Article 9 (general intangibles), but the lack of UCC Article 12 adoption means perfection by control is not statutorily recognized. Smart contract-based collateral lock and liquidation face legal uncertainty.

## 7. Insurance Claim Advance & Loss Draft Notes

### Idaho Insurance Claim Handling
- Idaho's **Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act** (Idaho Code 41-1329) governs insurer conduct
- No specific statutory deadline for insurers to pay property/casualty claims
- Requires: prompt acknowledgment; reasonable investigation; attempt in good faith to effectuate prompt, fair settlements
- Attorney fees available if insurer fails to pay amount "justly due" within 30 days of proof of loss (Idaho Code 41-1839)

### Additional Living Expenses (ALE)
- Standard homeowners policies include ALE coverage in Idaho
- ALE covers: temporary housing, additional food costs, laundry, pet boarding, transportation
- ALE does NOT cover: mortgage, normal utilities, regular expenses
- Documentation required for reimbursement

### Emergency Advance Payments
- No specific Idaho statute requiring emergency advance payments for property claims
- Some insurers voluntarily offer advance payments
- Claim proceeds subject to mortgagee/loss draft procedures if property is mortgaged

### Loss Draft / Mortgagee Check Procedures
- When property is mortgaged, insurance claim checks made jointly to homeowner and mortgagee
- Mortgage servicer manages disbursement of funds for repairs
- Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac guidelines apply for conventional loans

| Loan Status | Threshold | Treatment |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| Current (<31 days delinquent) | <=$40,000 | Initial disbursement up to greater of $40K, 33%, or excess over UPB+interest+advances |
| Current | >$40,000 | Multiple disbursements with inspections |
| 31+ days delinquent | <=$5,000 | Single payment |
| 31+ days delinquent | >$5,000 | 25% initial, then 25% increments per inspection |

### Idaho-Specific Mortgagee Issues
- Idaho follows standard mortgage industry practices
- No Idaho-specific loss draft statute found
- Mortgage holder has right to be named as loss payee on policy
- Claim proceeds may be used to pay off mortgage in total loss scenario (with written request)
- SmartContractor advances repaid from insurance proceeds face the risk of mortgagee-controlled disbursement delays

## 8. Assignment of Benefits Notes

### AOB Status: **GENERALLY PERMITTED** (No Idaho Statute Restricting AOB for Property Claims)

**Critical Finding**: Idaho does **NOT** have a statute specifically restricting or regulating Assignment of Benefits for property insurance claims.

### SB 1057 - Insured Homeowner Protection Act (2021)
- Would have created comprehensive AOB restrictions for Idaho
- **FAILED in Idaho Senate**: 11 Yea, 24 Nay (March 17, 2021)
- Bill provisions would have included:
  - Written AOB agreement requirements
  - 14-day rescission window
  - Itemized per-unit cost estimates
  - Notice to insurer within 3 business days
  - 18-point boldface required notices
  - Prohibition on contractor negotiating claim settlement
- **Status: DEFEATED - NOT LAW**

### Current Idaho AOB Law
- Idaho Code 41-3417: Subscriber's contracts for **dental care** must permit assignment of dental benefits to non-participating licensees
- No general AOB prohibition or restriction for property/casualty insurance
- Post-loss assignments generally permitted under common law
- Policy anti-assignment clauses generally **not enforceable** for post-loss assignments

### SmartContractor AOB Implications
- Assignment of post-loss insurance benefits to contractors is **generally permitted** in Idaho
- No specific rescission window, notice requirements, or font-size rules required by Idaho statute
- **However**, contractors receiving AOBs **CANNOT** negotiate claims with insurers without public adjuster license
- AOB does not transfer right to negotiate/settle claim unless assignee is licensed public adjuster or attorney
- SmartContractor should require clear AOB documentation that preserves the homeowner's right to cancel and does not imply claims negotiation authority

## 9. Public Adjuster / UPPA Notes

### Idaho Public Adjuster Licensing (Title 41, Chapter 58)
- **License required** to act as public adjuster in Idaho
- Licensed by Idaho Department of Insurance
- **$20,000 surety bond** or irrevocable letter of credit required
- Must pass Idaho public adjuster exam
- 24 hours continuing education every 2 years (3 hours ethics)
- **Exams administered by Pearson Vue**

### Definition of "Public Adjuster" (Idaho Code 41-5802)
Any person who, **for compensation**, on behalf of the insured:
- Advertises or solicits business as a public adjuster
- Directly or indirectly solicits business, investigates or adjusts losses
- Advises an insured about first-party claims for losses or damages

### What Contractors CAN Do (Without PA License)
1. Offer repair/reconstruction services
2. Give opinion on whether damage is covered
3. Prepare estimate and scope of work
4. Discuss estimate with customer
5. Recommend policyholder file a claim
6. Be present when insurer's adjuster inspects
7. Answer questions about estimates

### What Contractors CANNOT Do (Without PA License)
1. **Negotiate the claim with insurance company on customer's behalf**
2. **Investigate, appraise, evaluate, or assist customer in adjusting a claim**
3. **Prepare the insurance claim for customer**
4. **Review insurance policy or advise on coverage**
5. Advertise as "claim specialists" or ability to "deal with insurance companies"

### Unauthorized Practice of Public Adjusting (UPPA)
- Acting as public adjuster without license constitutes UPPA
- **Idaho has NOT enacted specific UPPA criminal statute** but DOI can take enforcement action
- NAIC Model Act language available for Idaho reference
- Insurers may refuse to negotiate with unlicensed adjusters

### SmartContractor Implication
- **SmartContractor and its contractor users MUST NOT negotiate claims with insurers on behalf of homeowners**
- SmartContractor can facilitate communication but cannot act as representative
- Recommendation: Require homeowner to retain licensed public adjuster or attorney for claim negotiation
- Platform should include guardrails preventing contractors from offering claims negotiation services
