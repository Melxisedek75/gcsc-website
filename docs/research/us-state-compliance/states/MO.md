## 1. State Summary

Missouri presents a MODERATE-TO-HIGH regulatory complexity environment for SmartContractor products. Key findings:

- **Consumer lending** is regulated by the Missouri Division of Finance with a dual-track system: small loans ($500 or less) under RSMo 408.500 and consumer installment loans ($500+) under RSMo 367.100-367.215. A 9% usury cap applies to consumer loans under $5,000 unless the lender is licensed under Missouri law (RSMo 408.030). Interest rates above $5,000 are contractual with no general usury cap for licensed lenders.
- **Commercial financing** is now regulated under Missouri's Commercial Financing Disclosure Law (Section 427.300, SB 1359), effective February 28, 2025, requiring disclosures for providers making more than five commercial financing transactions per year.
- **Contractor licensing** is decentralized — Missouri has NO statewide general contractor license requirement. Licensing is at the local/city level (Kansas City, St. Louis, etc.). Specialty trades (electrical, plumbing, HVAC, elevators) require state-level licensing.
- **Escrow-backed contractor advances** fall under the regulatory purview of the Missouri Division of Finance. Escrow agents may require licensure, and escrow arrangements for contractor advances must comply with Missouri consumer protection and lending laws.
- **Assignment of Benefits** under RSMo 376.427 applies specifically to HEALTH CARE SERVICES (medical, dental, pharmaceutical, etc.). It does NOT clearly extend to property repair contractors. However, AOB is generally permitted post-loss as a private contractual matter between the homeowner and contractor, though enforceability against insurers is uncertain.
- **Public adjusters** must be licensed under RSMo Chapter 325, carry a $10,000 bond, and are prohibited from performing restoration/repair work on properties they represent. Contractors and SmartContractor personnel MUST NOT negotiate with insurance companies on behalf of homeowners without a public adjuster license.
- **Money Transmitter Law (RSMo 361.700 et seq.)** regulates the transmission of money and may apply to certain SmartContractor payment flows, including escrow disbursement and digital asset liquidation.
- **Token/cryptocurrency collateral** has NO clear regulatory framework in Missouri. HB 2672 (2022) proposed exemptions for virtual currency from money transmitter laws but did not pass. Token collateral for lending purposes falls into an unregulated gray area requiring counsel review.
- **Consumer protection** is robust under the Missouri Merchandising Practices Act (RSMo Chapter 407), which prohibits deceptive, fraudulent, or unfair business practices and provides for Attorney General enforcement and private rights of action.

**Bottom Line:** Both SmartContractor product lines (Token Collateral Equipment Credit and ClaimBridge) face significant legal uncertainty and should be marked as BLOCKED pending legal review. The AOB statute's limitation to health care services, the public adjuster licensing requirement, the absence of clear crypto lending rules, and escrow licensing uncertainty create material compliance risks.

## 3. Lending & Finance Licensing

### 3.1 Consumer Lending Licenses

**Small Loans ($500 or less) — RSMo 408.500:**
- License required from Director of Division of Finance for lenders other than banks, trust companies, credit unions, savings banks, and savings and loan companies
- Annual license fee: $600 per location
- Maximum fees: 75% of loan amount (among highest in the U.S.)
- Loan terms: 14-31 days
- No rollovers permitted; maximum 6 renewals with 5% principal reduction each time
- Veritec database tracking required
- TILA/Reg Z disclosures required
- Posting of maximum APR required in lobby (14-point bold type)
- Borrower cancellation right: may cancel without cost by returning full principal by close of next business day

**Consumer Installment Loans ($500+) — RSMo 367.100-367.215:**
- Certificate of registration required from Division of Finance
- No upward limit on loan amount
- Annual audit by CPA required (367.205)
- Surety bond or $100,000 letter of credit may substitute for audit
- Records: cash journal, general ledger, individual ledger, alphabetical index required
- Interest: contract rate as parties may agree (408.100)
- Permissible charges and fees defined in 408.140
- Posting of maximum rates and fees required
- Examination authority: Director may examine and investigate complaints

**Usury Cap — RSMo 408.030:**
- A 9% usury cap applies to consumer loans under $5,000 unless the lender holds a valid Missouri lending license
- Licensed lenders are exempt from the 9% cap and may charge contractual rates
- Loans of $5,000 or more are not subject to the usury cap regardless of licensing status
- Violation of the usury cap renders the loan usurious and may result in forfeiture of interest and penalties

### 3.2 Commercial Lending

**Commercial Financing Disclosure Law — Section 427.300, SB 1359:**
- Effective: February 28, 2025 (or 6 months after Division of Finance promulgates rules)
- Applies to: Providers consummating MORE than 5 commercial financing transactions in Missouri in any 12-month period
- Covers: Commercial loans (secured/unsecured), accounts receivable purchase transactions, commercial open-end credit plans
- **Required disclosures**: Total funds provided, funds disbursed, total repayment amount, total dollar cost, payment terms/frequency/amounts, prepayment costs/discounts
- **No APR disclosure required**
- **Broker registration** required with Division of Finance + $10,000 surety bond
- Penalties: $500 per incident / $20,000 aggregate for initial violations; doubled after AG notice

**Exemptions from Commercial Financing Disclosure Law:**
- Depository institutions and affiliates/subsidiaries
- Service corporations to depository institutions
- Farm Credit Act regulated lenders
- Real estate-secured transactions
- Leases
- Purchase money obligations
- Licensed money transmitters
- Transactions exceeding $500,000
- Providers with 5 or fewer transactions in 12 months
- Captive finance companies
- Motor vehicle dealer transactions over $50,000

### 3.3 Money Transmitter Law — RSMo 361.700 et seq.

- License required to engage in money transmission in Missouri
- Security (surety bond) required in an amount determined by the Director of Finance
- "Money transmission" means engaging in the business of selling or issuing payment instruments, stored value, or receiving money or monetary value for transmission to a location within or outside the United States
- Virtual currency/cryptocurrency is NOT explicitly exempted from money transmitter licensing
- Escrow activities that involve the receipt and transmission of funds may trigger money transmitter licensing requirements
- Penalties for unlicensed money transmission include civil fines up to $1,000 per day and potential criminal liability

### 3.4 Key Licensing Implications for SmartContractor

| Scenario | License Required |
|----------|-----------------|
| Consumer loans to homeowners for repairs | YES — Consumer lending license (367.100-367.215 or 408.500) |
| Business-purpose loans to contractors (>5/yr, ≤$500K) | Commercial financing disclosure + possible broker registration |
| Equipment financing to contractors | May qualify as purchase money obligation (exempt) |
| Escrow-backed contractor advances | Escrow licensure + possible money transmitter license |
| Token collateral lock/liquidation | Money transmitter analysis REQUIRED |
| Loan brokering with compensation | Broker registration required |

- If SmartContractor partners with a bank: bank exemption may apply but marketplace lending arrangements are explicitly covered
- Loan brokering without registration is prohibited if compensation is received

## 4. Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules

### 4.1 Escrow Regulatory Framework

The Missouri Division of Finance has authority to regulate escrow activities. Key considerations:

**Escrow Agent Licensure:**
- Missouri does not have a standalone escrow agent statute, but escrow activities involving the holding of funds for others may be subject to Division of Finance oversight
- Title companies, banks, and credit unions may act as escrow agents by virtue of their existing licenses
- Non-bank entities holding funds in escrow for contractor advances may require a money transmitter license (RSMo 361.700 et seq.) or other applicable license
- The Division of Finance may examine escrow activities conducted by licensed entities and require compliance with consumer protection laws

**Escrow Requirements for Contractor Advances:**
- Escrow agreements must be in writing and specify the conditions for disbursement
- Funds held in escrow for contractor advances must be maintained in a segregated account
- Escrow agents owe fiduciary duties to all parties to the escrow
- Disbursement must occur only upon verified completion of contracted work or other agreed milestones
- Escrow agents must provide regular accounting of funds held

### 4.2 Escrow-Backed Advance Structure

SmartContractor's escrow-backed contractor advance product involves three parties:
1. **Homeowner** — receives repair services; funds are the source of repayment
2. **Contractor** — performs restoration work; receives advance from escrow
3. **Escrow Agent** — holds and disburses funds per the escrow agreement

**Regulatory Treatment:**
- Escrow-backed advances to contractors secured by insurance claim proceeds may be treated as commercial financing transactions subject to Section 427.300 (SB 1359)
- If the advance is effectively a loan to the homeowner (with repayment from insurance proceeds), consumer lending licensing applies
- The escrow agent must not commingle escrow funds with operating funds
- Interest earned on escrow funds belongs to the parties as specified in the escrow agreement

### 4.3 Escrow Disbursement Conditions

| Milestone | Disbursement Trigger |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Advance funding | Upon execution of escrow agreement + contractor authorization |
| First draw (materials) | Upon proof of materials purchase/order |
| Progress draws | Upon inspector/third-party verification of completed work |
| Final disbursement | Upon homeowner sign-off + certificate of completion |
| Return of surplus | To homeowner or funding source per escrow terms |

### 4.4 Compliance Requirements

**For the Escrow Agent:**
- Maintain fidelity bond and errors & omissions insurance
- Provide written escrow instructions signed by all parties
- Maintain records for minimum 5 years
- Comply with anti-money laundering (AML) requirements
- Report suspicious activity as required by federal law

**For SmartContractor:**
- If SmartContractor acts as escrow agent: money transmitter or escrow licensure required
- If SmartContractor partners with a licensed escrow agent: contractual due diligence required
- All escrow fees must be disclosed pursuant to Missouri Merchandising Practices Act
- Escrow terms must comply with consumer loan disclosures if the advance is a consumer loan

### 4.5 Risk Factors & Blocking Conditions

| Risk | Status | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| Escrow agent unlicensed | HIGH | Verify escrow partner holds required licenses |
| Consumer loan licensing | HIGH | Obtain consumer lending license if advance is to homeowner |
| Money transmitter | HIGH | Confirm escrow activity does not trigger MT licensing |
| Commingling of funds | HIGH | Segregated accounts required; audit trail mandatory |
| MMPA disclosure violations | MEDIUM-HIGH | Full fee and term disclosure required |
| Mortgagee priority | MEDIUM | Conduct title/lien search before funding |

**Recommended Status:** BLOCKED pending — (1) Escrow partner licensing verification, (2) Money transmitter analysis for escrow activity, (3) Consumer lending license pathway confirmed if applicable, (4) Written escrow agreement reviewed by Missouri counsel.

## 5. Contractor Licensing & Commercial Financing

### 5.1 Contractor Licensing

- **No state-level general contractor license** required in Missouri
- All businesses must register with Missouri Secretary of State
- Licensing is at local/city level: Kansas City, St. Louis, Springfield, Columbia, Independence, Joplin, etc.
- Specialty trades require STATE licensing: electricians, plumbers, mechanical/HVAC, elevator contractors
- Local requirements typically include: general liability insurance, workers' compensation, surety bonds (amount varies by jurisdiction)

**Verification Requirements:**
- SmartContractor must verify contractor holds valid local license before funding
- For specialty trades (electrical, plumbing, HVAC): verify state license through Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance
- Confirm general liability insurance is active and adequate
- Confirm workers' compensation coverage (or valid exemption)

### 5.2 Contractor Financing Implications

- Business-purpose loans to contractors are treated as commercial financing
- Missouri's Commercial Financing Disclosure Law applies to contractor working capital loans if >5 transactions/year and $500,000 or less
- Equipment financing may be treated as purchase money obligations (exempt from disclosure law if incurred as part of price of collateral)
- Real estate-secured loans are exempt from the commercial disclosure law
- Contractor licensing status should be verified at local level

### 5.3 Disclosure Requirements for Contractor Financing

- Written statement of business purpose recommended to establish commercial purpose
- Commercial disclosure requirements: total funds provided, total repayment, total cost, payment schedule, prepayment terms
- No APR disclosure required under Missouri law
- If financing is a consumer loan (homeowner is borrower): full TILA/Reg Z disclosures + Missouri consumer loan disclosures required

### 5.4 Escrow-Integrated Contractor Advance

When SmartContractor provides an advance to a contractor through an escrow arrangement:
- The advance is typically a commercial financing transaction (contractor is borrower)
- If repayment comes from insurance proceeds assigned by homeowner, the homeowner's assignment must be documented
- The contractor cannot simultaneously act as a public adjuster (RSMo 325.055)
- The escrow agent must disburse funds only upon verified completion of work milestones

## 7. Insurance Claims & Assignment of Benefits

### 7.1 Missouri Insurance Claim Framework

**Valued Policy Statute — RSMo 379.140:**
- Applies to total losses from covered perils
- Insurer must pay policy limit for total loss
- Exclusions: unscheduled personal property, detached structures, builder's risk, mortgage insurance, replacement cost coverage, losses covered by multiple policies

**Partial Loss Statute — RSMo 379.150:**
- Applies to fire losses
- CARRIER has option to repair property OR issue payment (changed in 2021; previously insured's option)
- If carrier elects to repair, this does NOT create a general contractor relationship between carrier and insured

**Additional Living Expenses (ALE):**
- ALE is a CONTRACTUAL coverage, not a statutory mandate in Missouri
- Policy language controls extent of ALE coverage
- Typically covers: hotel, restaurant meals, storage, transportation above normal living expenses
- Limits: policy-specific (time limits, dollar limits, or both)
- No Missouri statute found mandating emergency advance payments for ALE
- Advances are at insurer discretion per policy terms

**Prompt Pay Law (2024 update):**
- Payment from insurance carrier must be made to doctor or patient within 45 days of receiving claim
- Applies to health insurance; property insurance prompt pay rules unclear

**Mortgagee/Loss Draft Checks:**
- No specific Missouri statute found mandating joint check issuance to mortgagee
- Standard industry practice: mortgagee named on loss draft when mortgage exists
- Mortgagee may hold funds in escrow and disburse as repairs progress
- Force-placed insurance regulated under RSMo 379.1850 (effective August 2023)
- Lender-placed insurance restrictions include: premiums must be based on replacement cost, notice requirements, no compensation from insurers to lenders

### 7.2 Assignment of Benefits

**AOB Status: RESTRICTED TO HEALTH CARE SERVICES — NOT CLEARLY APPLICABLE TO CONTRACTORS**

**RSMo 376.427 — Assignment of Benefits:**

Missouri's AOB statute applies specifically to:
- "Health care services including medical, surgical, dental, pediatric, pharmaceutical, chiropractic, licensed ambulance service, and optometric services"
- "Health care providers"

**Key Provisions:**
1. "Upon receipt of an assignment of benefits made by the insured to a provider, the insurer shall issue the instrument of payment for a claim for payment for health care services in the name of the provider."
2. "All claims shall be paid within 30 days of the receipt by the insurer of all documents reasonably needed to determine the claim."
3. "Nothing in this section shall preclude an insurer from voluntarily issuing an instrument of payment in the single name of the provider."
4. Exception: Does not apply to insurers that directly contract with certain members of a class of providers (i.e., in-network vs. out-of-network)

**Critical Limitation:**
- The statute uses the term "provider" defined in the context of HEALTH CARE SERVICES
- Property repair contractors are NOT listed as covered providers
- There is NO general AOB statute for property/casualty insurance in Missouri
- The AHIP 50-State Survey (2019) confirms Missouri's AOB applies to health care services only

**Post-Loss AOB Practice:**
- Despite the statutory limitation, AOB is generally permitted post-loss as a private contractual matter
- A homeowner MAY execute a private assignment of claim proceeds to a contractor or SmartContractor
- However, this is a CONTRACTUAL assignment, not a statutory AOB — insurer is not obligated to honor it
- Private assignment of insurance proceeds may be subject to: (1) mortgagee consent, (2) insurer policy terms, (3) anti-assignment clauses in insurance policies

**Dental-Specific Updates:**
- 2022 amendment: Extended AOB to pre-paid dental plans
- 2024 amendment: Extended prompt pay to 45 days

### 7.3 Implications for SmartContractor

- No statutory right to claim advance payments in Missouri
- Any claim advance product would be contractual between SmartContractor and homeowner
- If SmartContractor advances funds to homeowner based on anticipated insurance proceeds, this is effectively a LOAN requiring appropriate lending license
- ALE payments are at insurer discretion; SmartContractor cannot compel insurer to advance ALE funds
- A contractor CANNOT rely on RSMo 376.427 to compel an insurer to issue a joint check or assign claim proceeds
- Attempting to negotiate claim payments from insurers on behalf of homeowners without a public adjuster license would violate RSMo 325.015

**Recommended Status:** AOB_NOT_STATUTORILY_APPLICABLE_TO_CONTRACTORS — Private contractual assignment may be possible but requires counsel review.

## 8. Public Adjuster & Insurance Representation

### 8.1 Public Adjuster Licensing — RSMo Chapter 325

**License Required (325.015):**
- "No person, partnership, association or corporation shall, directly or indirectly, act as a public adjuster or public adjuster solicitor without first securing a license"
- License issued by Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance

**Definition:**
- Public adjuster: "Any person, partnership, association or corporation engaging in the adjustment or settlement of claims for losses or damages arising out of policies of fire or allied lines of insurances"
- Exclusions: Attorneys at law acting in professional capacity; life/health insurance claim negotiators

**Requirements:**
- Application + fee
- Examination through Pearson VUE
- $10,000 surety bond
- Good character/financial responsibility
- Continuing education: 24 hours biennially

**Fee Caps:**
- Generally may not exceed 20% of insurance claim payment
- Higher limits may apply in declared emergencies

**Contract Requirements:**
- Must be in writing
- Must state time, date, and place of execution
- Must describe services, compensation terms
- Cancellation right: until midnight of the THIRD BUSINESS DAY after signing (325.050)
- Cancellation notice must be disclosed in each contract

**Prohibitions (325.055):**
- "A licensed public adjuster shall not perform restoration or repair work on property they represent"
- Cannot have direct/indirect financial interest in claim (other than fee)
- Cannot acquire interest in salvage
- Cannot refer insured to persons with whom they have financial interest
- Cannot choose repair contractors for the insured

### 8.2 Key Implications for SmartContractor

| Activity | License Required? | Penalty for Violation |
|----------|------------------|----------------------|
| Negotiating with insurer on homeowner's behalf | YES — Public adjuster license | Unlicensed practice; fines; potential criminal liability |
| Adjusting or settling fire/allied lines claims | YES — Public adjuster license | Violation of RSMo 325.015 |
| Performing repair work AND representing insured | PROHIBITED | License revocation; penalties |
| Referring insured to affiliated contractor | PROHIBITED | License revocation; penalties |
| Providing claim documentation assistance | NO — if no negotiation | Permitted as administrative service |
| Educating homeowner on claim process | NO — if no advice on settlement | Permitted as informational service |

- A contractor who also negotiates with the insurance company on behalf of the homeowner is acting as an UNLICENSED PUBLIC ADJUSTER
- SmartContractor or its contractor partners MUST NOT negotiate claim settlements with insurers
- If SmartContractor wants to represent homeowners in claim negotiations, it must obtain a public adjuster license
- A licensed public adjuster CANNOT also be the repair contractor
- These restrictions apply equally in escrow-backed advance arrangements

## 9. Consumer Protection, Mortgage & Disclosures

### 9.1 Missouri Merchandising Practices Act — RSMo Chapter 407

The Missouri Merchandising Practices Act (MMPA) is one of the strongest consumer protection statutes in the United States:

**Prohibited Practices:**
- Deception, fraud, false pretense, false promise, misrepresentation, unfair practice
- Concealment, suppression, or omission of any material fact
- Advertising or representing goods/services with intent not to sell as advertised
- Disparaging goods/services of another by false/misleading representation

**Enforcement:**
- Missouri Attorney General has civil enforcement authority
- Private right of action available to consumers
- Damages can include actual damages, attorney fees, and costs
- Willful violations may result in enhanced damages

**Implications for SmartContractor:**
- ALL product disclosures must be accurate, complete, and not misleading
- Fee structures must be transparently disclosed
- Marketing materials must not misrepresent product terms or regulatory status
- Escrow fees, financing costs, and contractor advance terms must be disclosed in writing
- SmartContractor must not represent itself as licensed where it is not
- The MMPA applies to both consumer and commercial transactions in many contexts

### 9.2 Mortgage & Loss Draft Rules

**Missouri Mortgagee/Loss Draft Rules:**
- No specific Missouri statute found mandating mortgagee involvement in property insurance claim payments
- When a mortgagee is named on the policy, insurers typically issue loss draft checks jointly to the insured and mortgagee
- Mortgagee may hold funds in escrow and disburse as repairs progress
- For claims over $20,000-$40,000, mortgagees typically use monitored disbursement
- For total losses, appraisal requirements and multi-phase disbursement

**Force-Placed Insurance — RSMo 379.1850 (effective August 28, 2023):**
- Regulates lender-placed insurance on mortgaged homes
- Does not apply to business/commercial/agricultural credit
- Premiums must be based on replacement cost value
- Lender must mail certificate of insurance to mortgagor's last known address
- No compensation from insurers to lenders for force-placed insurance

**Implications for SmartContractor:**
- If SmartContractor advances funds to homeowner and a mortgage exists, the mortgagee may have priority claim to insurance proceeds
- SmartContractor's claim to insurance proceeds may be subordinate to mortgagee
- Loss draft checks may be jointly payable to homeowner and mortgagee, complicating repayment routing
- SmartContractor should conduct title/lien search before advancing funds

Violations can result in license suspension/revocation and civil penalties up to $1,000/day. |
| Insurance Claim Risk | HIGH | No clear statutory right to claim advances or ALE payments. Valued policy statute (379.140) only applies to total losses. Partial loss statute (379.150) gives repair/payment option to carrier, not insured. Prompt pay applies to health insurance; property insurance unclear. Claim advances would likely be treated as loans requiring license. |
| AOB Risk | HIGH | Missouri AOB statute (376.427) explicitly limits covered providers to health care services (medical, dental, pharmaceutical, etc.). Contractors are NOT covered. AOB is generally permitted post-loss as a contractual matter, but no statutory enforcement against insurers. Private contractual assignment is possible but untested and subject to mortgagee rights and anti-assignment clauses. |
| Public Adjuster Risk | HIGH | Strict licensing required under RSMo Chapter 325. Unlicensed adjusting is a violation. Licensed adjusters prohibited from performing repair work. Fee cap of 20%. 3-day cancellation right. SmartContractor and contractors must not negotiate with insurers. Separate public adjuster license would require $10,000 bond, examination, and ongoing compliance. |
| Token Collateral Risk | HIGH | No regulatory framework exists. HB 2672 (2022) would have provided exemptions but did not pass. Money transmitter law may apply to collateral lock/liquidation. UCC perfection in digital assets uncertain. Securities law may apply depending on token characteristics. Federal regulations (SEC, CFTC, FinCEN) also apply. |
| Escrow Risk | MEDIUM-HIGH | Missouri Division of Finance may regulate escrow activities. Escrow agents may require money transmitter or other licensing. Escrow-backed advances may trigger consumer or commercial lending laws. Segregated accounts, fiduciary duties, and disclosure requirements apply. |
| Consumer Protection Risk | MEDIUM-HIGH | Missouri Merchandising Practices Act (RSMo Chapter 407) is broadly enforced. Prohibits deception, fraud, misrepresentation, unfair practices. Attorney General has enforcement authority. Private right of action available. Damages can include attorney fees. Any SmartContractor product must ensure full transparency and avoid any misleading representations. Escrow fees and financing costs must be disclosed. |

1. All smart contract functions creating legal obligations must be BLOCKED

3. Audit records may be stored as blockchain hashes for record retention
4. If and when legal clearance is obtained, implement Missouri-specific disclosure flows
5. Consider integration with Missouri Division of Finance reporting requirements
6. Public adjuster licensing check should be built into contractor onboarding
7. Escrow milestone verification should require third-party inspector sign-off
