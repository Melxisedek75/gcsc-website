## 1. State Summary

Utah presents a **mixed regulatory environment** for SmartContractor products. The state is notably **crypto-friendly** — among the most blockchain-friendly states in the U.S. — with blockchain tokens explicitly exempt from the Money Transmitter Act (UT Code §7-25-102, as amended by SB 213/2019), the state treasurer authorized to invest in digital assets and engage in staking and lending under HB 230 (2025), and cryptocurrency classified as property under the Revised Uniform Unclaimed Property Act. This creates a potentially favorable environment for token-collateralized products, subject to federal alignment and counsel review.

However, Utah maintains a **structured insurance regulatory framework** with specific rules governing claim payments, public adjusters, and post-loss assignments to contractors. Key regulatory layers include:

- **Utah Insurance Department** (insurance.utah.gov) — Primary regulator for insurance claims, adjuster licensing, and claim settlement practices.
- **Utah Department of Financial Institutions (DFI)** — Oversees consumer lending under the Utah Consumer Credit Code, commercial financing registration, and escrow-related activities.
- **Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing (DOPL)** — Licenses and regulates residential and commercial contractors.
- **Utah Consumer Credit Code (Title 70C)** — Governs consumer lending with NMLS notification requirements.
- **Money Transmitter Act (Title 7, Chapter 25)** — Utah's money transmission law, with a critical exemption for blockchain tokens.
- **"Insured Homeowners Protection Act" (UT Code §13-50-301)** — Strict requirements for post-loss assignments to residential contractors, including written contracts, 5-day cancellation rights, and 14-point bold notice.
- **Public Adjuster Licensing (§31A-26-201)** — Required to negotiate or settle claims on behalf of insureds.

**SmartContractor Status:** All live lending, token collateral, claim advance, assignment-of-benefits (AOB), and repayment routing operations are **BLOCKED** pending Utah-specific legal review.

## 3. Lending / Finance Licensing Notes

### 3.1 Utah Consumer Credit Code (UCCC) — Title 70C

**Notification Requirement (§70C-8-202):**
- Entities engaging in consumer credit transactions must file a **Consumer Credit Notification (CCN)** through NMLS.
- As of 2024, all notifications must be managed through NMLS (transition complete August 26, 2024).
- Annual notification due: **December 31 of each year**.
- Fee: $100 per year (§70C-8-203).

**Exemptions (§70C-1-202(2)):**
- Extensions of credit primarily for **business, commercial, or agricultural purposes**.
- Closed-end credit secured by first lien on a dwelling.
- Transactions where the amount financed exceeds **$73,400** (as of January 1, 2026, adjusted for inflation).
- Depository institutions and wholly-owned subsidiaries.

**Key Implication for SmartContractor:** Business-purpose loans to contractors may fall outside the UCCC if structured as commercial credit. Consumer-purpose loans to homeowners would likely trigger notification requirements.

### 3.2 Commercial Financing Registration and Disclosure Act (SB 183, eff. Jan 1, 2023)

**Registration:**
- A "provider" of commercial financing must register with the Utah DFI and NMLS.
- Applies to providers consummating **more than 5 commercial financing transactions** in Utah per calendar year.
- Registration must be renewed annually.

**Required Disclosures (before consummation):**
- Total amount of funding provided.
- Total amount disbursed (if different).
- Total amount the business must pay.
- Total dollar cost of the financing.
- Manner, frequency, and amount of each payment.
- Prepayment policy.
- Any amounts paid to brokers.

**Exemptions:**
- Transactions over $1,000,000.
- Transactions of $50,000+ to motor vehicle dealers.
- Depository institutions and subsidiaries.
- Licensed money transmitters.
- Purchase-money obligations (UCC-defined).
- Captive financing arrangements.
- Real-estate-secured transactions.
- Providers doing ≤5 transactions per year.

### 3.3 Utah Money Transmitter Act — Title 7, Chapter 25

**Standard Requirements:**
- Net worth: minimum **$1,000,000**.
- Surety bond: minimum **$50,000**.
- License fee: $300.

**Critical Blockchain Exemption (§7-25-102, as amended by SB 213, 2019):**
- "Blockchain tokens" are **explicitly exempted** from the definition of money transmission.
- A person who facilitates the creation, exchange, or sale of certain blockchain technology-related products is exempt.
- A payment instrument does not include blockchain tokens.

**Implication:** Token collateral activities that involve only blockchain tokens (not fiat currency transmission) may be exempt from MTA licensing. However, any fiat currency movement or third-party payment services would require additional analysis.

## 4. Escrow / Custody / Fiduciary Requirements

### 4.1 Escrow Regulation in Utah

The **Utah Department of Financial Institutions (DFI)** has authority to regulate escrow activities in the state. Entities holding funds in trust or escrow — particularly in connection with real property transactions, construction contracts, or insurance claim proceeds — may be subject to escrow licensing or registration requirements.

**Relevant considerations for SmartContractor:**
- If the platform holds, manages, or disburses funds on behalf of contractors, homeowners, or insurers in a fiduciary capacity, escrow licensing analysis is required.
- Utah escrow agents are generally regulated under Title 7, Chapter 20 (Escrow Agents), requiring a license from the DFI unless an exemption applies.
- **Exemptions** may include: attorneys performing escrow services in connection with legal representation; title insurers; licensed real estate brokers under limited circumstances; and depository institutions.

### 4.2 Claim Proceeds Escrow

Insurance claim proceeds — particularly those involving mortgagee/loss payee interests — often flow through escrow arrangements:

- When a mortgagee is listed on the policy declarations page, claim payments are typically made jointly to the insured and the mortgagee (§13-50-301(6)).
- The mortgagee may place funds in a restricted escrow account and release them based on repair progress.
- Any entity that receives, holds, or disburses insurance claim proceeds as an intermediary should evaluate whether this constitutes escrow activity under Utah law.

### 4.3 Construction Project Escrow

Residential construction contracts in Utah may involve escrow arrangements for:
- Deposits or advance payments from homeowners.
- Progress payments held pending inspection or completion milestones.
- Retainage held by property owners.

These arrangements may trigger escrow licensing if SmartContractor or an affiliated entity holds funds in trust for third parties.

### 4.4 Fiduciary Obligations

Under Utah law, any entity that exercises discretionary authority or control over client funds assumes fiduciary duties including:
- Duty of loyalty.
- Duty of care.
- Duty to account.
- Duty to segregate client funds from operational funds.

**SmartContractor Implications:**

| Activity | Potential Escrow Trigger? | Notes |
|----------|--------------------------|-------|
| Holding token collateral in smart contract | UNKNOWN | Digital asset custody may not fit traditional escrow definitions; counsel review required |
| Receiving insurance claim proceeds for disbursement to contractor | POSSIBLE | If acting as intermediary holding funds pending conditions, escrow license may apply |
| Managing loss draft/repair escrow on behalf of mortgagee | LIKELY | Traditional escrow activity; license likely required |
| Facilitating direct lender-borrower transactions (no fund holding) | UNLIKELY | If funds flow directly between parties, escrow licensing may not apply |
| Holding homeowner deposits for construction work | POSSIBLE | Deposit-taking in trust may trigger escrow or money transmission analysis |

**CRITICAL: ESCROW_STATUS = REQUIRES_COUNSEL_REVIEW**

Any arrangement where SmartContractor, a subsidiary, or a smart contract holds, manages, or routes funds on behalf of third parties must be reviewed by Utah-licensed counsel to determine whether escrow licensing, money transmitter licensing, or exemptions apply.

## 5. Contractor Licensing & Verification

### 5.1 Regulatory Authority: DOPL

The **Utah Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing (DOPL)**, within the Utah Department of Commerce, licenses and regulates contractors in the state.

**Website:** https://commerce.utah.gov/dopl/

### 5.2 License Types (Relevant to SmartContractor)

| Code | Classification | Scope |
|------|---------------|-------|
| R100 | Residential and Small Commercial Contractor | Residential and small commercial projects |
| B100 | General Building Contractor | General building construction |
| R101 | Residential and Small Commercial Non-Structural Remodeling and Repair | Projects under $50,000 |
| S220 | Carpentry | Structural and finish carpentry |
| S230 | Siding | Siding installation and repair |
| S270 | Drywall | Drywall installation and repair |
| S280 | Roofing | Roofing installation and repair |

### 5.3 Licensing Requirements

- **Qualifying Individual:** Must pass the Utah Contractor Business-Law Examination plus a trade-specific exam.
- **Ownership/Employment:** Qualifier must be a 20% owner OR a W-2 employee in a management position.
- **General Liability Insurance:** $100,000 per occurrence / $300,000 aggregate.
- **Workers' Compensation:** Required if the contractor has employees.
- **License Renewal:** Odd-numbered years, due by November 30.
- **Continuing Education:** 6 hours required prior to renewal.

### 5.4 Fees

- Initial: $50 per qualifier + $175 per classification.
- Renewal: $128 plus applicable fees.

### 5.5 Key Point

Utah does **NOT** have a single statewide general contractor license. Licensing is handled through DOPL with specific trade classifications. A contractor must hold the appropriate classification for the work performed. SmartContractor should verify the specific classification of any contractor on the platform before offering financing or facilitating claim payments.

### 5.6 Commercial Financing to Contractors

- Commercial loans to licensed contractors for business purposes likely fall outside UCCC notification if the loan amount exceeds $73,400 or if structured as commercial credit.
- If the provider consummates ≤5 commercial financing transactions per year in Utah, the provider may be exempt from the Commercial Financing Registration and Disclosure Act.
- If >5 transactions, registration with DFI/NMLS and pre-transaction disclosures are required.

## 6. Token Collateral / Digital Asset Notes

### 6.1 Utah: A Crypto-Friendly Jurisdiction

Utah is one of the most blockchain-friendly states in the United States. The state has enacted a series of statutes that create a favorable — though not fully certain — environment for token-collateralized lending and digital asset activities.

### 6.2 Key Legislation

**SB 213 (2019) — Blockchain Technology Act:**
- Defines blockchain, blockchain tokens, and cryptocurrency under Utah law.
- **Exempts blockchain token facilitation from the Money Transmitter Act** (§7-25-102).
- Blockchain tokens are **NOT** "payment instruments" under the MTA.
- A person who facilitates the creation, exchange, or sale of blockchain tokens is explicitly exempt from money transmission licensing.

**HB 230 (2025) — Blockchain and Digital Innovation Amendments (signed March 2025):**
- Authorizes the **state treasurer to invest public funds in qualifying digital assets**.
- Defines "qualifying digital assets" (minimum $500 billion market cap for non-stablecoins).
- Authorizes the state treasurer to engage in **staking and lending** of digital assets under specified conditions.
- Creates a framework for stablecoin recognition (fully backed, redeemable at par).
- Prohibits local governments from discriminatory zoning against digital asset mining.

**HB 357 (2024) — Utah DAO Act:**
- Grants legal personality to DAOs as "Utah LLDs" (Limited Liability Decentralized Organizations).
- Provides limited liability protections for DAO members.
- Establishes that no implicit fiduciary duties exist unless explicitly stated in the DAO's governing documents.

**Revised Uniform Unclaimed Property Act:**
- Virtual currency is explicitly classified as **"property"**.
- Defined as a digital representation of value that does not have legal tender status.

### 6.3 Token Collateral Assessment

| Factor | Status |
|--------|--------|
| State money transmitter license for token custody | NOT REQUIRED (exempt per §7-25-102) |
| State lending license for crypto-collateralized loans | UNKNOWN — no explicit statute |
| Token classification | Property (not money/currency) |
| Smart contract enforceability | UNKNOWN — no explicit statute |
| Liquidation of token collateral | UNKNOWN — no explicit restriction at state level |

**CRITICAL: TOKEN_COLLATERAL_LENDING_STATUS = UNKNOWN_REQUIRES_COUNSEL_REVIEW**

While Utah exempts blockchain tokens from money transmission and classifies them as property, there is **no explicit state-level authorization for private token-collateralized lending**. The state's crypto-friendly posture suggests a potentially favorable environment, but:

- Federal securities law alignment is required.
- UCC Article 12 (controlling digital assets) compliance is needed.
- Consumer lending laws may apply depending on borrower type.
- Staking/lending provisions in HB 230 apply only to the state treasurer, not private entities.

## 7. Insurance Claim Advance & AOB Notes

### 7.1 Utah Claim Handling Requirements (Property & Casualty)

**Sources:** Utah Code Title 31A Ch 26 Pt 3; Admin Code R590-190

**Key Timelines:**

| Action | Deadline | Statute |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Acknowledge claim | 15 days of notice | 31A-26-303 / R590-190 |
| Provide claim forms | 15 days of notice | 31A-26-303 |
| Decision (accept/deny) | 30 days of proof of loss | 31A-26-303 |
| Extension notification | Within initial 30-day period | 31A-26-303 |
| Follow-up letters (if extended) | Every 45 days | 31A-26-303 |
| Payment (if liability clear) | "Prompt" — generally within 30 days of proof of loss | 31A-26-303(h) |

**Unfair Claim Settlement Practices (§31A-26-303):**
- Not attempting in good faith to effectuate prompt, fair, equitable settlement = unfair practice.
- Failing to promptly provide reasonable explanation for denial = unfair practice.
- Compelling insureds to litigate by offering substantially less than amounts recovered = unfair practice.
- **NO PRIVATE RIGHT OF ACTION** — enforcement by the Insurance Commissioner only.

**Additional Living Expenses (ALE):**
- ALE coverage is standard in homeowners policies.
- Insurer must pay for the "shortest time required to repair or replace."
- Utah statute does not mandate specific ALE advance timelines beyond "prompt" settlement.

**Claim Advances:**
- Utah does not have a specific statute mandating emergency claim advances.
- Insurers generally issue initial payments as an "advance against total settlement."
- Failure to pay promptly when liability is clear constitutes an unfair claim settlement practice.

**Key Implication for SmartContractor ClaimBridge:** Utah does not have a specific statute authorizing third-party claim advances. Any entity providing advances against insurance claim proceeds would need to ensure compliance with lending laws and avoid acting as an insurance adjuster.

### 7.2 Assignment of Benefits — "Insured Homeowners Protection Act" (UT Code §13-50-301)

Utah has **specific legislation** governing post-loss assignments to residential contractors, enacted as Part 3 of the Residential Construction Contracts Act.

**Full Statute Name:** "Post-loss assignment of rights or benefits to a residential contractor"

**Key Requirements (§13-50-301):**

1. **Valid Only if in Writing:** A post-loss assignment of rights or benefits to a residential contractor under a property and casualty insurance policy must be:
   - In writing.
   - Executed by all named insureds.
   - Contain specific provisions.

2. **Required Contents:**
   - Itemized description of work to be performed.
   - Itemized description of materials, labor, and fees.
   - Total itemized amount to be paid.
   - Statement that the contractor has made no assurances that the claimed loss will be fully covered.

3. **Required Notice (in 14-point bold type):**
   > "YOU ARE AGREEING TO GIVE UP CERTAIN RIGHTS YOU HAVE UNDER YOUR INSURANCE POLICY. PLEASE READ AND UNDERSTAND THIS DOCUMENT BEFORE SIGNING."

4. **Cancellation Window:**
   - **5 business days** from the later of: (a) date of execution, or (b) date the insured receives a copy.
   - Must cancel in writing.
   - If cancelled, the contractor has 10 business days to return any payments or deposits.

5. **Copy to Insurer:**
   - The executed assignment must be provided to the insurer within **5 business days**.

6. **Mortgagee Protection:**
   - The assignment shall not impair the interest of a mortgagee listed on the declarations page.

7. **Communication Rights:**
   - The assignment shall not prevent the insurer from communicating with the named insured or mortgagee.

8. **Void if Violations:**
   - A post-loss assignment is void if the residential contractor violates certain provisions of the Act.

### 7.3 Prohibited Acts — §13-50-302

A residential contractor may **NOT**:
- Rebate or offer to rebate any portion of an insurance deductible.
- Advertise or promise to pay or rebate any part of an insurance deductible.
- Pay or offer to pay any part of an insurance deductible as an inducement.

### 7.4 AOB Status for SmartContractor

| Element | Status |
|---------|--------|
| AOB permitted for contractors? | YES — with strict statutory requirements |
| Written contract required? | YES |
| Cancellation period? | 5 business days |
| Itemized estimate required? | YES |
| Copy to insurer required? | YES — within 5 business days |
| Fee cap? | Not in §13-50-301 (contractual) |
| Deductible rebate prohibition? | YES — strict prohibition |

**Implication for SmartContractor:** Any assignment of claim proceeds to a contractor or to SmartContractor must comply with the Insured Homeowners Protection Act requirements, including written form, itemized scope, 14-point bold notice, and 5-day cancellation window.

## 8. Public Adjuster / Insurance Representation Notes

### 8.1 Licensing Required (§31A-26-201)

- **Public adjuster license required** to negotiate or settle claims on behalf of insureds.
- License classes: Property & Casualty, Accident & Health, Workers' Compensation, Crop.
- Exam required for residents; reciprocity available for non-residents.
- Fingerprints and background check required.
- Continuing education: 24 hours every 2 years (including 3 hours ethics).

### 8.2 Public Adjuster Contract Requirements (§31A-26-401)

- **Written contract required** — a public adjuster cannot act without one.
- Contract must be filed with the Insurance Department.
- Subject to **recision for 10 days** after signing.
- Signed copy must be provided to the insured at the time of signing.
- Contract may not contain terms limiting or nullifying any law.

### 8.3 Public Adjuster Compensation (§31A-26-402)

- **NO STATUTORY FEE CAP** in Utah.
- Compensation types permitted: hourly fee, flat fee, percentage of claim payment.
- If flat fee: contract must state type of initial expenses with dollar estimates.
- If percentage: contract must specify the percentage.
- **CANNOT require, demand, or accept a fee before claim settlement.**
- Must provide itemized invoice of expenses.

### 8.4 Who May Negotiate with the Insurance Company

| Role | License Required? | Notes |
|------|-------------------|-------|
| Public Adjuster | YES — §31A-26-201 | Represents insureds only; cannot represent insurers |
| Independent Adjuster | YES — §31A-26-201 | Represents insurers |
| Company Adjuster | NO | Employed by the insurer |
| Attorney | NO (if in attorney-client relationship) | Exempt from adjuster licensing |
| Insurance Producer | NO (limited) | Only as to claims adjusted on request of insurer |
| Contractor | UNKNOWN | Contractor negotiating own payment may not be adjusting; **COUNSEL REVIEW NEEDED** |

### 8.5 CRITICAL FOR SMARTCONTRACTOR

**SmartContractor, its contractors, and any representatives MUST NOT engage in activities that constitute "insurance adjusting" without a license.**

"Insurance adjusting" means: directing the investigation, negotiation, or settlement of a claim under an insurance policy, on behalf of an insurer, policyholder, or claimant.

**Activities that likely REQUIRE a public adjuster license:**
- Negotiating claim value with the insurance company on behalf of the homeowner.
- Directing investigation of a claim on behalf of the homeowner.
- Settling a claim on behalf of the homeowner.

**Activities that may NOT require a license (secondary source analysis — not legal advice):**
- Performing repair work and billing the insurance company as a co-payee.
- Providing an estimate for repair work.
- Communicating with the insurer about scope of repairs (if not "directing" the claim).

**Status: PUBLIC_ADJUSTER_RISK = HIGH** — Any SmartContractor representative or contractor negotiating claims on behalf of homeowners without a public adjuster license would violate §31A-26-201.

## 9. Mortgage / Loss Draft / Repayment Routing Notes

### 9.1 Standard Utah Practice

- When a property has a mortgage, the mortgagee (lender) is listed as a loss payee on the insurance policy.
- Insurance claim checks are typically made payable to **both the insured and the mortgagee**.
- Both endorsements are required before funds can be accessed.

### 9.2 Mortgagee/Lender Procedures (Industry Standard)

| Claim Amount | Typical Handling |
|-------------|------------------|
| ≤$20,000–$40,000 | Lender endorses check and returns to borrower |
| >$40,000 | Funds placed in restricted escrow; released based on repair progress |
| Defaulted loan | All funds placed in escrow regardless of amount |

**Utah does not have a specific statute governing loss draft procedures** — these are governed by:
- Loan documents and mortgage agreements.
- Insurance policy mortgagee clause.
- Federal mortgage servicing rules (if applicable).

### 9.3 Mortgagee Protection Under §13-50-301

Post-loss assignments "shall not impair the interest of a mortgagee listed on the declarations page." Any SmartContractor AOB or repayment routing arrangement must preserve the mortgagee's security interest and comply with the mortgagee's loss draft procedures.

### 9.4 Implication for Repayment Routing

Any assignment of claim proceeds must account for mortgagee interests. If SmartContractor routes repayment from insurance proceeds, the platform must:
- Verify whether a mortgagee exists on the policy.
- Coordinate with the mortgagee's loss draft department.
- Ensure the mortgagee's endorsement or escrow requirements are satisfied.
- Document that the assignment does not impair the mortgagee's interest per §13-50-301(6).
