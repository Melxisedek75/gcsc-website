# GCSC State Compliance Packet: Pennsylvania (PA)

> **REGULATORY CLASSIFICATION: HIGH REGULATION STATE**
>
> Pennsylvania presents a **moderate-to-high regulatory complexity** environment for GCSC products. The state maintains a well-established consumer lending framework through the Consumer Discount Company Act (CDCA), the Loan Interest and Protection Law (LIPL), and the Mortgage Licensing Act. Home improvement contractors are regulated at the state level through HICPA. Post-loss assignment of insurance benefits is generally permitted under Pennsylvania common law. Public adjusters are licensed and regulated by the Pennsylvania Insurance Department, with strict prohibitions on dual roles. **All GCSC live product operations in Pennsylvania are BLOCKED pending comprehensive legal review.**
>
> **CRITICAL LEGISLATIVE UPDATE:** Act 7 of 2025 (Senate Bill 202), signed by Governor Shapiro on June 27, 2025, explicitly brings virtual currency transmission under the Money Transmitter Act (MTA), effective **August 26, 2025**. This fundamentally alters the token collateral landscape in Pennsylvania.

## 3. Lending / Finance Licensing

### Consumer Discount Company Act (CDCA) - 7 P.S. SS 6201 et seq.

The CDCA is the **primary statute governing consumer lending** in Pennsylvania.

- **Licensure Required**: Any person "in the business of negotiating or making loans or advances of money or credit" where the lender charges interest exceeding the general usury rate of 6% (for loans of $25,000 or less)
- **Licensed CDCA Rate Structure**: Licensed CDCA lenders may charge up to approximately **24% APR** on loans of $25,000 or less under the tiered rate structure (7 P.S. S 6213.A, S 6213.E)
- **Usury Cap (Unlicensed)**: The 6% usury rate applies to unlicensed lenders making loans under $50,000 (41 P.S. SS 201, 202)
- **Criminal Usury**: Charging interest exceeding **25%** may constitute criminal usury

### Loan Interest and Protection Law (LIPL)

- General usury cap of **6% per annum** for loans under $50,000 made by unlicensed lenders
- Exceeding 25% interest carries criminal penalties; borrower is not required to pay amounts over the legal rate
- Civil penalties of **$1,000 per loan** ($3,000 for borrowers 60+) may apply for usury violations
- Residential mortgages are subject to Act 6, which sets a floating ceiling rate based on long-term government bond yields plus 2.50%

### Mortgage Licensing Act (MLA) - 7 Pa.C.S. Chapter 61

- As of August 29, 2025, HB 1103 amendments take effect, allowing licensed mortgage lenders to offer "discount points"
- Requires licensing for first and secondary mortgage loans
- Administered by DoBS through NMLS
- License types: Mortgage Lender, Mortgage Broker, Mortgage Loan Originator, Consumer Discount Company

### Small Loan Act - 73 Pa.C.S. SS 8301 et seq.

- Governs payday-type loans
- Finance charges limited to $15 per $100 borrowed
- Maximum loan amount: $5,000 (typically $500 for payday-type loans)
- Only one payday loan outstanding at a time per borrower
- 48-hour cooling-off period required between loans
- No rollovers or renewals permitted

### Commercial/Business-Purpose Loans

- Pure commercial loans (business purpose) are generally exempt from CDCA requirements if not made to consumers
- Equipment financing may fall under the bona fide sale exemption of 7 P.S. S 6217 if structured as a true lease or conditional sale
- Partnering with a CDCA-licensed entity would be required for consumer lending activities
- Loan brokering/soliciting without a license may violate CDCA S 6203.A

### CRITICAL RISK: True Lender Doctrine

> Pennsylvania **aggressively pursues** "true lender" theories (see *Commonwealth v. Think Finance, Inc.*).
>
> - Arranging loans through bank partners does **not** automatically shield non-bank entities from state usury and licensing laws
> - The Third Circuit has upheld Pennsylvania's ability to apply state usury laws to non-bank entities even when a bank is the nominal originator

## 4. Money Transmission, Escrow & Virtual Currency Licensing

> **NEW SECTION - This consolidated section addresses PA's escrow authority, money transmitter requirements, and the landmark Act 7 of 2025 virtual currency provisions.**

### 4.1 Escrow Regulation

**Regulator**: Pennsylvania Department of Banking and Securities (DoBS)

- Pennsylvania does not have a standalone **escrow agent licensing statute** of general application
- The PA Department of Banking and Securities **may regulate escrow activities** when conducted in connection with licensed financial services (e.g., mortgage lending, money transmission)
- Escrow activities in connection with real estate transactions are typically performed by:
  - Licensed attorneys
  - Title companies licensed under Pennsylvania's insurance framework
  - Mortgage servicers operating under MLA licensure
- **Mortgage servicers** generally hold claim proceeds in escrow and disburse as repairs are completed
- **Implication for GCSC**: Any escrow or custodial holding of funds in connection with consumer transactions should be evaluated for potential licensing triggers under CDCA or MTA

### 4.2 Money Transmitter Act (MTA) - 7 P.S. SS 6101 et seq.

**Traditional Money Transmission Requirements:**

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Surety Bond | $1,000,000 required |
| Application | Through NMLS |
| Annual Reporting | Required |
| Agent Registration | Required |
| AML Compliance | Required |
| Ongoing Obligations | Significant compliance requirements |

**Prohibited Activity**: "No person shall engage in the business of money transmission without first having obtained a license from the department." (7 P.S. S 6102)

### 4.3 CRITICAL: Act 7 of 2025 (SB 202) - Virtual Currency Under MTA

> **Effective Date: August 26, 2025**

On June 27, 2025, Governor Josh Shapiro signed **Act 7 of 2025**, which amends Pennsylvania's Money Transmitter Act to **explicitly include virtual currency transmission**. This is the first Pennsylvania statute specifically regulating virtual currency.

**Key Provisions:**

| Provision | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Virtual Currency Definition** | "A digital representation of value that functions as a medium of exchange, unit of account, or store of value" (excludes most loyalty rewards and in-game tokens) |
| **Licensing Requirement** | "No person shall engage in the business of transmitting virtual currency by means of a transmittal instrument for a fee or other consideration with or on behalf of an individual without first having obtained a license from the department." (7 P.S. S 6102(a)(2)) |
| **Self-Hosted Wallet Exemption** | Individuals who custody their own digital assets are exempt; those using self-hosted wallets to transmit virtual currency **on behalf of others for compensation** are subject to licensure |
| **B2B Transmission Exemption** | Business-to-business transmissions of virtual currency in connection with commercial contracts are exempt from the MTA |
| **No Grace Period** | There will be **no grace period** when Act 7 amendments become effective on August 26, 2025 |
| **Prior VCSOP Rescinded** | The Department's Virtual Currency Statement of Policy (effective October 15, 2024) will be rescinded concurrently |

### 4.4 Token Collateral Implications for GCSC

| Activity | Regulatory Treatment Under Act 7 |
|---|---|
| Locking customer tokens as collateral | Likely constitutes a regulated activity requiring MTA licensure |
| Token transfer, custody, or management for compensation | MTA licensure likely required |
| B2B exemption applicability | **Does NOT apply** to consumer-facing token collateral products |
| Self-custody arrangements | May require careful legal structuring |
| Liquidation of token collateral | Virtual currency transmission; MTA licensure required |

**STATUS: `TOKEN_COLLATERAL_BLOCKED_PENDING_COUNSEL_REVIEW`**

> The interaction between token collateral lending and the new Act 7 amendments has not been tested and requires formal legal analysis. No live token collateral operations should proceed without Pennsylvania-specific legal counsel approval and likely MTA licensure.

### 4.5 Prior Regulatory Position (Pre-Act 7)

| Period | DoBS Position |
|---|---|
| Pre-April 2024 | Virtual currency was NOT "money" under the MTA |
| April-October 2024 | DoBS issued Virtual Currency Statement of Policy reversing position |
| October 15, 2024 | VCSOP effective - "money" interpreted to include virtual currency |
| **August 26, 2025** | **Act 7 codifies licensing requirement in statute** |

## 5. Contractor / HICPA Requirements

### Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act (HICPA)

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Statute** | HICPA, effective July 1, 2009 |
| **Administered by** | PA Office of Attorney General, Bureau of Consumer Protection |
| **Registration Required** | All contractors doing >$5,000 annually in home improvement work |
| **Exemptions** | Small contractors (<$5,000/year), large retailers (net worth >$50M), new home builders, commercial-only contractors |
| **Registration Fee** | $50, valid for 2 years |
| **Insurance Required** | Minimum $50,000 personal injury liability and $50,000 property damage coverage |

### HICPA Contract Requirements

All home improvement contracts **must**:

- Be in writing, legible, and signed by both parties
- Include contractor's HIC registration number
- Contain approximate start and completion dates
- Include description of work, materials, and specifications
- State total sales price, with down payment and special order materials listed separately
- Include all subcontractors' names and addresses
- Include toll-free number of Bureau of Consumer Protection
- Include **Notice of Right of Rescission (3 business days)**
- A copy must be provided to homeowner at execution

### Payment Restrictions

| Contract Value | Deposit Limit |
|---|---|
| Over $1,000 | Cannot exceed 1/3 of total contract price (including special order materials) |
| Over $5,000 | Cannot exceed 1/3 of total contract price |

- Contractor may **NOT** accept a deposit before contract is signed
- "Time and materials" contracts allowed since 2014 amendment, but must include **10% cap** above initial estimate
- Any cost beyond initial estimate + 10% requires **written change order signed by homeowner**

### Prohibited Contract Clauses (Voidable by Homeowner)

- Hold harmless clauses
- Confession of judgment provisions
- Jury trial waivers
- Waivers of state/local health, life, safety, and building codes
- Any clause waiving homeowner's rights under HICPA

### Penalties

- Civil penalties of $1,000 or more for failure to register
- **Treble damages and attorney's fees** available to prevailing homeowners under HICPA
- Home improvement fraud is a **criminal offense**

### Key Implications for GCSC

- Any contractor financing product must ensure contractors are properly HIC-registered
- Financing arrangements that function as consumer loans may trigger CDCA licensing requirements
- Down payment restrictions may affect advance payment structures
- Contracts must comply with all HICPA requirements to be enforceable

## 6. Insurance Claim Advances & AOB

### General Insurance Claim Rules in Pennsylvania

**Applicable Law**: Pennsylvania Statutes Title 40 P.S. (Insurance Code), Pennsylvania Code Title 31 (Insurance Department regulations)

### Claim Payment Timing

- Insurers must acknowledge claims promptly
- Unfair claim settlement practices are defined at 40 P.S. S 1171.5(a)(1)
- Insurers are prohibited from: refusing to pay claims without reasonable basis, failing to affirm or deny coverage within reasonable time, failing to implement standards for prompt investigation

### Additional Living Expenses (ALE)

- ALE is standard coverage in homeowners policies, typically 20-30% of dwelling coverage
- ALE covers the difference between normal living expenses and temporary expenses due to displacement
- ALE is typically a separate insurance check from repair/replacement payments
- Receipts are generally required for reimbursement
- No specific Pennsylvania statute requiring emergency advance payments for ALE, but insurers must handle claims promptly and cannot engage in unfair claim practices

### Emergency Advance Payments

- Pennsylvania does not have a specific statute mandating emergency advance payments for homeowner claims
- Insurers' duty of good faith and fair dealing under 42 Pa. C.S. S 8371 (bad faith statute) may support arguments for timely advance payments
- The Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act (40 P.S. SS 1171.1-1171.15) provides regulatory framework for claim handling

### Claim Financing / Factoring

- No specific Pennsylvania statute addressing third-party financing of insurance claims
- Assignment of claim proceeds (post-loss) is generally permitted under Pennsylvania law
- Any entity providing advances against insurance claims should carefully evaluate whether such activity constitutes lending under the CDCA

### Assignment of Benefits (AOB) Status: **PERMITTED (POST-LOSS)**

Pennsylvania follows the **majority rule** regarding assignment of insurance benefits:

| Timing | Enforceability |
|---|---|
| **Pre-loss assignments** | Anti-assignment clauses are **enforceable** - insurer consent required |
| **Post-loss assignments** | Anti-assignment clauses are generally **UNENFORCEABLE** - insured may assign vested claim |

**Key Case Law:**

- *Seneca Ins. Co. v. Lexington & Concord Search & Abstract, LLC*, 484 F.Supp.2d 374 (E.D. Pa. 2007): Post-loss assignment of a money claim does not increase insurer risk
- *Williams v. American Security Insurance Company*, E.D. Pa. 2017: Federal courts upheld post-loss assignments of first-party property claims to contractors

### AOB Limitations

- An assignment cannot materially increase the insurer's risk or burden
- The assignment must be of a claim that has vested (post-loss)
- Assignment of bad faith claims is more restricted (see *Feingold v. Palmer & Barr*, 831 F. App'x 608 (3d Cir. 2020))
- An assignment does not give the assignee greater rights than the assignor had

### CRITICAL RISK: Lending Characterization

> Providing advances to homeowners against expected insurance proceeds, with repayment from those proceeds, may be **characterized as a loan requiring CDCA licensure** if interest/fees exceed 6%.

### GCSC Implications

- Post-loss assignment of insurance claim proceeds to a contractor or financing entity is likely valid in Pennsylvania
- The assignment should be in writing and clearly identify the claim and proceeds being assigned
- The insurer must be notified of the assignment
- **CRITICAL**: If GCSC or a contractor assists with claims processing under an AOB, care must be taken to avoid engaging in public adjuster activities without a license

## 7. Public Adjuster Rules

### Licensing Requirements

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Statute** | 63 P.S. SS 1601-1608 (Public Adjuster Act) |
| **Regulations** | 31 Pa. Code Chapter 115 |
| **Licensing Authority** | Pennsylvania Insurance Department |
| **License Required** | **Yes** - No person may act as a public adjuster without a license |

### Contract Requirements (31 Pa. Code S 115.2)

- Contract must be titled "Public Adjuster Contract"
- Must include name, address, phone number of adjuster
- Must specify consideration as percentage of payments received or maximum dollar amount
- Must include **Notice of Right to Cancel** (cancellation within 4 calendar days of execution)
- Must be **approved by the Insurance Department** before use
- Fee cannot be collected from first check only - must be percentage of each check
- Cannot require insured to authorize insurer to issue checks only in adjuster's name

### Dual Role Restrictions (CRITICAL for GCSC)

> A public adjuster who is also a contractor **can only perform ONE function at a time**.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Separate Contracts** | Must have **two separate contracts**: one approved public adjuster contract AND a separate service contract for repair work |
| **Timing Restriction** | A public adjuster who also wants to work as a contractor **cannot begin repairs until the insurance company has made a decision on the claim** |
| **Contractor Recommendation** | A public adjuster may recommend a contractor, but the insured can choose their own |
| **Ownership Interest** | Cannot enter into repair contract through a company in which adjuster has direct/beneficial interest until payment received (63 P.S. S 1605(d)) |
| **Compensation Disclosure** | Cannot receive compensation from any repair/restoration company unless disclosed in public adjuster contract and agreed to by insured |

### Duties (31 Pa. Code S 115.9)

- Notify insurer within **2 business days** of contract execution
- Communicate all settlement offers to insured in writing within **5 business days**
- Reply to insurer communications in writing within **5 business days**

### Prohibited Conduct (63 P.S. S 1606)

- Soliciting contracts door-to-door within **7 days of a catastrophic event**
- Charging excessive fees
- Delaying, deterring, or interfering with insured's right to contact insurer directly

### Contractors Acting as Public Adjusters

| Activity | Permitted? |
|---|---|
| Acting as public adjuster without separate license | **NO** |
| Advertising claims processing assistance | **NO** |
| Interpreting insurance policies | **NO** |
| Negotiating with insurance companies | **NO** |
| Obtaining post-loss assignment of benefits | **YES** - different legal framework |
| Documenting damage for homeowner | **YES** |
| Providing repair estimates to homeowner | **YES** |

### GCSC Implications

- **GCSC and its contractor partners MUST NOT engage in activities that constitute public adjusting without a license**
- Prohibited activities include: negotiating with insurers on behalf of homeowners, interpreting policy language, advising on claim strategy, preparing claim estimates for submission to insurers
- Contractors should limit their role to: performing repair work, documenting damage, providing repair estimates to the homeowner
- Any claim advocacy services should be referred to **licensed public adjusters** or **attorneys**

## 8. Mortgage / Loss Draft

### Loss Draft / Claim Check Rules

**Standard Practice**: When a mortgagee is listed on a homeowner's insurance policy, insurance claim checks are typically made payable jointly to the homeowner and the mortgage servicer.

### Mortgage Servicer Requirements

| Scenario | Disbursement Rule |
|---|---|
| Current loans (or <31 days delinquent) | Initial disbursement up to greater of $40,000, 33% of proceeds, or amount exceeding UPB + accrued interest + advances |
| Delinquent loans (31+ days) | Different disbursement schedules apply |
| Claims >$40,000 | Typically require monitored disbursement with inspections |

### Mortgagee Clause

Insurance policies include a mortgagee clause (ISAOA/ATIMA - "Its Successors and/or Assigns As Their Interest May Appear") ensuring the mortgage holder is named on claim checks.

### Pennsylvania-Specific Rules

- Pennsylvania does **not** have a specific statute governing loss draft disbursement procedures
- The mortgage agreement and investor/servicer guidelines (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA) govern loss draft handling
- Mortgage servicers generally hold claim proceeds in escrow and disburse as repairs are completed

### Assignment of Claim Proceeds with Mortgages

| Issue | Detail |
|---|---|
| Mortgagee consent | May be required when mortgage exists |
| Mortgagee interest | Mortgagee's interest in property gives them claim to insurance proceeds |
| AOB effectiveness | Any AOB or assignment must account for mortgagee's interest |
| Practical reality | Mortgage servicers will not honor an assignment that bypasses their security interest |

### GCSC Implications

- If a homeowner assigns claim proceeds to GCSC or a contractor, but the mortgagee is also a payee on the check, the assignment may not be effective as to the mortgagee's interest
- GCSC should **verify whether a mortgage exists** before relying on an assignment of claim proceeds
- Coordination with mortgage servicers' loss draft departments will likely be required
- The mortgage servicer's disbursement schedule (often 3-6 installments for large claims) may conflict with repayment timing expectations
