## Section 1: State Overview & Risk Classification

### 1.1 Jurisdictional Summary

New Jersey presents a **HIGHLY REGULATED** environment for SmartContractor products. The state maintains overlapping regulatory frameworks across multiple agencies, with some of the strictest consumer protection, contractor oversight, and anti-fraud regimes in the United States. Any entity offering financing, claim advances, or contractor-backed payment products in New Jersey must navigate a complex web of licensing, bonding, disclosure, and conduct requirements.

**Core Characteristics:**

| Attribute | Classification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Overall Regulatory Intensity | **SEVERE** |
| SmartContractor Product Status | **BLOCKED — Legal Review Required** |
| Primary Regulator (Financial) | NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI) |
| Primary Regulator (Contractors) | NJ Division of Consumer Affairs (DCA) |
| Consumer Protection Standard | Treble damages + attorney fees mandatory |
| AOB Regulation | **Extremely Strict (Post-2018 Reforms)** |
| Licensing Barrier Height | Very High (net worth, audits, fingerprinting, bonds) |

### 1.2 Key Blocking Factors

1. **Consumer Finance Licensing Act (NJSA 17:11C):** Requires $100,000 net worth, $100,000 liquid assets, audited financials with unqualified opinion, and fingerprinting for all officers/directors. Unlicensed consumer lending is a direct violation.

2. **Home Repair Financing Act (NJSA 17:16C-62 et seq.):** Any contractor who executes a financed home repair contract must hold a separate DOBI home repair contractor license. Salespersons must also be individually licensed and may only affiliate with one contractor at a time.

3. **Assignment of Benefits Reforms (P.L. 2018, c. 149):** New Jersey enacted comprehensive post-2018 reforms restricting AOB arrangements. These reforms impose mandatory notices, 3-day cancellation rights, strict font and formatting requirements, and significant contractor accountability measures. Violations can result in contract rescission, treble damages, and loss of licensure.

4. **Consumer Fraud Act (NJSA 56:8-1 et seq.):** Among the strictest in the nation. Provides mandatory treble damages, attorney fees, and costs for any "ascertainable loss" caused by unlawful practices. Technical contract violations — including font-size failures, missing registration numbers, or omitted cancellation notices — independently trigger liability.

5. **Public Adjuster Licensing (NJSA 17:22B):** Contractors are **absolutely prohibited** from negotiating insurance claims on behalf of homeowners. Only licensed public adjusters may represent insureds for compensation. Violations carry criminal penalties.

6. **Usury Ceiling (NJSA 31:1-1):** 30% cap for loans under $50,000. Violations void the interest obligation and may trigger criminal usury charges.

### 1.3 Bottom Line Assessment

**All SmartContractor product lines are BLOCKED for live operation in New Jersey.** The state's strict consumer fraud regime, overlapping licensing requirements, unresolved digital asset collateral questions, and severely restricted AOB framework create material legal risk that cannot be mitigated without extensive review by New Jersey-licensed counsel. Demo-only and hash-reference audit records are permissible for product development purposes.

## Section 3: Contractor Licensing & Registration (Dual System)

### 3.1 Layer 1: Division of Consumer Affairs Registration

**ALL** home improvement contractors operating in New Jersey must register annually with the Division of Consumer Affairs under the Contractors' Registration Act (NJSA 56:8-136 et seq.).

**Registration Requirements:**

| Requirement | Specification |
|-------------|-------------|
| Annual Registration Fee | $110 |
| Registration Number Display | Must prominently display "NJHIC#" on all advertising, contracts, vehicles |
| Commercial General Liability | Minimum $500,000 |
| Workers' Compensation | Required if contractor has employees |
| Written Contracts | Mandatory for all jobs exceeding $500 |
| Contract Contents | Registration number, insurance certificate copy, total price, start/end dates, materials description, warranties, security interest disclosure |
| Right to Cancel | 3-day cancellation right required in all contracts; must appear in **at least 10-point bold type** |
| Violation Penalty | Fourth-degree crime **plus** Consumer Fraud Act liability (treble damages + attorney fees) |

**Critical Contract Compliance Notes:**
- NJ courts enforce contract technical requirements **strictly**. Missing registration numbers, incorrect font sizes, or omitted cancellation notices independently create liability regardless of substantive performance.
- The 10-point bold type requirement for the 3-day cancellation notice is mandatory and routinely scrutinized in enforcement actions.
- Contracts must specify whether the contractor will obtain necessary permits.

### 3.2 Layer 2: DOBI Home Repair Contractor License (Financed Contracts)

Any contractor who executes a **financed** home repair contract must hold a separate Home Repair Contractor License from DOBI under the Home Repair Financing Act (NJSA 17:16C-62 et seq.).

**Triggering Activities:**
- Offering, arranging, or executing home repair contracts where payment is financed through a third party
- Any extension of credit by the contractor beyond 90 days
- Assignment of the contract to a finance company

**Exemptions:**
- Cash installment payments over 90 days or less
- Credit card payments (direct)
- Contracts paid in full at time of service

**Salesperson Licensing:**
- Any salesperson who secures a financed contract must hold an individual Home Repair Salesperson License
- Salesperson may only be affiliated with **ONE** licensed home repair contractor at any time
- No dual-affiliation permitted

### 3.3 Prohibited Home Repair Contract Provisions (NJSA 17:16C-64)

No financed home repair contract in New Jersey may contain:

- **Acceleration clauses** triggered by holder insecurity or subjective judgment
- **Power of attorney** granted to the contractor or financing entity
- **Confession of judgment** provisions
- **Assignment of wages, salary, or commissions** as security
- **Provisions relieving the contractor from liability** for defective work or materials
- **Waiver of the homeowner's right to cancel** or any other statutory protection

**Implication for SmartContractor:** Any SmartContractor financing or claim-advance documentation must be reviewed to ensure none of these prohibited provisions appear. Smart contract-based acceleration or liquidation triggers may be particularly suspect under NJ's strict prohibitions.

### 3.4 2024–2026 Contractor Reforms (P.L. 2023, c. 237)

## Section 4: Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules

### 4.1 Escrow Regulatory Framework in New Jersey

The **New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI)** regulates escrow activities in the state. While New Jersey does not maintain a standalone "escrow agent" licensing statute comparable to some other jurisdictions, escrow services related to real estate transactions fall under DOBI's comprehensive financial services oversight. Entities holding funds in escrow for construction, repair, or restoration purposes are subject to significant regulatory scrutiny.

**Key Regulatory Touchpoints:**

| Authority | Scope |
|-----------|-------|
| DOBI — Banking Division | Oversight of escrow-holding financial institutions, mortgage servicers |
| DOBI — Insurance Division | Insurance proceeds escrow (loss draft administration) |
| NJ Real Estate Commission | Escrow for real estate transactions (N.J.S.A. 45:15-1 et seq.) |
| NJ Consumer Fraud Act | Escrow-related misrepresentations, failure to disburse |

### 4.2 Escrow-Backed Advance Mechanics

An **escrow-backed contractor advance** is a financing arrangement in which a third party (the advance provider) deposits funds into an escrow account controlled by a licensed escrow agent, with disbursement to the contractor tied to verified completion of specified work milestones. The homeowner's insurance claim proceeds are directed into the same escrow account to repay the advance.

**Typical Escrow-Backed Advance Flow:**

1. **Advance Agreement:** Homeowner, contractor, and advance provider execute an escrow-backed advance agreement
2. **Escrow Establishment:** Funds are deposited with a DOBI-regulated escrow holder
3. **Milestone-Based Disbursement:** Contractor submits proof of completion; escrow agent verifies and releases funds incrementally
4. **Insurance Proceeds Routing:** Homeowner directs insurer to deposit claim payments into the escrow account
5. **Repayment:** Escrow agent applies insurance proceeds first to repay the advance (principal + approved charges), then disburses any surplus to the homeowner
6. **Final Accounting:** Full reconciliation and accounting provided to all parties

### 4.3 Legal Requirements for Escrow-Backed Advances in New Jersey

#### 4.3.1 Escrow Holder Qualifications

An entity serving as escrow holder for contractor advances in New Jersey should ideally be:
- A licensed bank, trust company, or savings association chartered under NJ or federal law
- A title insurance company or agent authorized to conduct business in New Jersey
- A licensed attorney maintaining funds in an IOLTA account
- A DOBI-licensed entity with demonstrated escrow administration capability

**SmartContractor Note:** If SmartContractor or any affiliated entity intends to serve as escrow holder, NJ counsel must confirm whether separate escrow licensure or banking charter authority is required. **Do not assume escrow activities can be performed without specific authorization.**

#### 4.3.2 Escrow Agreement Requirements

The escrow agreement for contractor advances in New Jersey must contain, at minimum:

| Required Element | Detail |
|-----------------|--------|
| **Identities of All Parties** | Homeowner (beneficial owner), contractor (performing party), advance provider (secured party), escrow agent (fiduciary) |
| **Deposit and Funding Terms** | Exact amount of advance, source of funds, timing of deposit |
| **Disbursement Schedule** | Itemized draw schedule tied to verified completion milestones |
| **Verification Standards** | Who inspects, what documentation required, dispute resolution mechanism |
| **Insurance Proceeds Handling** | Direction of claim payments, application priority, surplus disposition |
| **Interest Treatment** | How interest on escrowed funds is handled (if any) |
| **Fees and Costs** | All fees charged by escrow agent, fully itemized |
| **Default and Termination** | Conditions for stopping disbursement, curing defaults, terminating escrow |
| **Recordkeeping** | Escrow agent's obligation to maintain records and provide statements |
| **Governing Law** | New Jersey law; venue in appropriate NJ court |

#### 4.3.3 Milestone Verification & Inspection Requirements

New Jersey's strict contractor fraud prevention framework requires that escrow disbursements be tied to **verified completion**, not mere contractor certification. Best practices include:

- **Independent third-party inspection** at each milestone
- **Photographic documentation** of completed work
- **Permit inspection sign-offs** where municipal permits are required
- **Homeowner acknowledgment** of satisfactory completion (with right to object)
- **10% holdback** until final inspection and certificate of completion/occupancy (if applicable)

#### 4.3.4 Consumer Protection Requirements

Escrow-backed advances are subject to the full scope of NJ consumer protection law:

- **NJ Consumer Fraud Act (NJSA 56:8-1 et seq.):** Any material misrepresentation about the escrow arrangement, failure to disburse properly, or undisclosed fees triggers treble damages, attorney fees, and costs.
- **3-Day Right to Cancel:** If the escrow-backed advance is structured as a home improvement contract or financing arrangement exceeding $500, the homeowner retains a 3-business-day right to cancel.
- **Written Contract:** All terms must be in writing, in plain language, with all required disclosures.
- **Registration Number:** If the contractor is a party to the escrow agreement, their NJHIC# must appear prominently.
- **Home Repair Financing Act:** If the advance is structured as financed home repair, the contractor must hold DOBI home repair contractor license.

### 4.4 Escrow & Insurance Proceeds — Loss Draft Coordination

When insurance proceeds are involved (the standard SmartContractor ClaimBridge scenario), escrow administration intersects with loss draft processing:

**Joint Payee Requirements:**
- Insurance checks for structural damage are typically made jointly payable to the homeowner **and** the mortgagee/loss payee
- The mortgage holder's endorsement is required before funds can be deposited into escrow
- Some mortgage servicers maintain their own escrow accounts and may resist third-party escrow arrangements

**Escrow-Mortgagee Coordination:**

| Scenario | Handling |
|----------|----------|
| Mortgagee releases full check | Deposit into SmartContractor-managed escrow; follow milestone disbursement schedule |
| Mortgagee holds funds in own escrow | Coordinate with mortgagee's loss draft department; advance provider takes subordinate position or obtains mortgagee acknowledgment |
| Mortgagee inspects before release | Advance provider funds initial milestones; mortgagee-funded escrow repays advance as mortgagee releases funds |
| No mortgage (property free and clear) | Cleanest scenario — homeowner directs insurer to pay escrow directly; milestone disbursement proceeds unencumbered |

### 4.5 Prohibited Practices in Escrow-Backed Advances

The following practices **must not** appear in any SmartContractor escrow-backed advance program in New Jersey:

1. **Advance Without Escrow:** Direct payment to contractor without independent escrow control
2. **Contractor-Controlled Escrow:** Contractor serving as escrow holder for their own advance (clear conflict of interest)
3. **Undisclosed Fee Stacking:** Hiding escrow fees, inspection fees, or processing charges in the advance amount
4. **Lien Waiver Absence:** Disbursing funds without obtaining conditional/partial lien waivers from contractor and subcontractors
5. **Unverified Milestone Payments:** Releasing funds on contractor's word alone without independent verification
6. **Failure to Account:** Not providing detailed monthly or transaction-level escrow statements to all parties
7. **Commingling:** Mixing escrow funds with operating funds (escrow funds are trust funds, not property of the holder)

### 4.6 Escrow Agent Liability Exposure

Under New Jersey law, an escrow agent owes a **fiduciary duty** to all parties to the escrow. Breach of this duty can result in:

- **Professional negligence claims** for improper disbursement
- **Consumer Fraud Act liability** (treble damages + attorney fees)
- **Conversion claims** for unauthorized distribution of funds
- **Disciplinary action** by DOBI if the escrow agent is a regulated entity
- **Criminal liability** in cases of intentional misappropriation

**SmartContractor Risk Mitigation:**
- Use established, licensed escrow agents (banks, title companies, licensed attorneys)
- Maintain clear, written escrow agreements with all required elements
- Implement multi-party authorization for disbursement
- Maintain separate escrow accounts for each transaction (no commingling)
- Provide regular, transparent accounting to all parties
- Obtain E&O insurance covering escrow administration

## Section 5: Consumer Finance & Lending Licensing

### 5.1 Consumer Lender License (NJSA 17:11C)

**License Required:** Any person engaging in the business of making loans of $50,000 or less for personal, family, or household purposes, payable in installments, must obtain a Consumer Lender License from DOBI.

| Requirement | Specification |
|-------------|---------------|
| Net Worth Minimum | $100,000 (demonstrated on audited financials) |
| Liquid Assets Minimum | $100,000 (not cumulative with net worth) |
| Audited Financials | Required with unqualified opinion (even for newly organized entities) |
| Fingerprinting | Required for all officers, directors, members, partners, substantial stockholders, sole proprietors |
| Application Fee | $700 non-refundable; $1,000 if seeking both consumer lender and sales finance licenses |
| In-State Office | Not required |
| License Term | 2 years (July 1 odd year to June 30 odd year) |
| Processing Time | ~60 days from acceptance of complete application |
| Core Statute | NJSA 17:11C-3: "No person shall engage in business as a consumer lender or sales finance company without first obtaining a license" |

**Exclusions:**
- Residential first or second mortgage loans (separate licensing regime)
- Loans exceeding $50,000 for personal, family, or household purposes
- Commercial/business-purpose loans (may fall outside NJSA 17:11C but see Section 5.3)

### 5.2 Sales Finance Company License

Required for entities that:
- Acquire retail installment contracts from retail sellers
- Solicit the purchase of retail installment contracts
- Aid retail sellers in the sale or assignment of retail installment contracts

**Key Distinction:** Sales finance companies may submit **unaudited** financials (vs. audited required for consumer lenders). Governing statutes include NJSA 17:11C-1 et seq. and the NJ Retail Installment Sales Act of 1960 (NJSA 17:16C-1 et seq.).

### 5.3 Commercial/Business-Purpose Loan Characterization

NJSA 17:11C focuses on consumer-purpose loans ($50,000 or less for personal/family/household use). Business-purpose loans may fall outside the licensing requirement. **However:**

- If a loan to a contractor is structured as a retail installment contract, Sales Finance Company License may apply
- If a loan involves a home repair contract, the Home Repair Financing Act (NJSA 17:16C-62 et seq.) applies independently
- NJ courts examine the **substance** of the transaction, not just its label
- Loans to contractors that indirectly finance residential repairs may be recharacterized as consumer-purpose

### 5.4 Usury and Interest Rate Restrictions (NJSA 31:1-1)

| Loan Amount | Maximum Legal Rate | Violation Consequence |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Under $50,000 | 30% per annum | Interest void; criminal usury possible; treble damages under CFA |
| $50,000 and above | No statutory cap | Subject to general unconscionability standards |

**Critical:** New Jersey's criminal usury statute (NJSA 2C:21-19) imposes criminal penalties for willful violation of the usury ceiling. Any SmartContractor financing must include rigorous rate compliance checks.

### 5.5 Loan Brokering

New Jersey does not appear to maintain a separate "loan broker" license distinct from the consumer lender license. Activities that involve arranging or brokering loans to consumers likely require the arranger to hold a Consumer Lender License. ## Section 6: Assignment of Benefits (AOB) — CRITICAL SECTION

### 6.1 Overview: Why New Jersey AOB Is Different

New Jersey has some of the **strictest AOB laws in the United States**, particularly following the 2018 reforms (P.L. 2018, c. 149). While the state follows the common law "post-loss assignment" doctrine (permitting assignment of claim proceeds after a loss occurs despite policy anti-assignment clauses), the legislature enacted comprehensive statutory reforms to combat contractor abuse of AOB arrangements.

**The Critical Distinction:** Pre-loss assignments of insurance policies require insurer consent; post-loss assignments of the right to claim proceeds do not. However, even post-loss assignments are now heavily regulated by statute.

### 6.2 Common Law Foundation: Flint Frozen Foods Doctrine

New Jersey courts established in *Flint Frozen Foods v. Firemen's Insurance Co.* that:

- Standard property insurance policies contain anti-assignment clauses: "Assignment of this policy will not be valid unless we give our written consent"
- These clauses are **valid and enforceable for PRE-loss assignments** of the policy itself
- These clauses are **NOT enforceable for POST-loss assignments** of claim proceeds
- "Once a loss occurs, an insured's claim under a policy may be assigned without the insurer's consent"
- Post-loss assignment is of a "chose in action" (a right to payment), not of the insurance policy itself

This common law foundation means that, absent statutory reform, homeowners could freely assign their claim proceeds to contractors or other third parties after a covered loss.

### 6.3 Statutory Reforms: P.L. 2018, c. 149

In response to widespread contractor abuse — particularly following major storm events — New Jersey enacted comprehensive AOB reforms. While the precise codified location in NJ's complex statutory framework requires direct NJ counsel verification, the reforms are understood to include:

#### 6.3.1 Mandatory Pre-Assignment Notices

Before executing any assignment of benefits, the homeowner must receive:

| Notice Element | Requirement |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Right to Refuse** | Clear statement that the homeowner is NOT required to sign an AOB to obtain repairs |
| **Rights Being Transferred** | Explanation that the assignee will have all rights to the insurance claim, including the exclusive right to communicate with the insurer, file claims, and sue the insurer |
| **Loss of Control** | Statement that the homeowner will no longer have control over the claim process once the AOB is signed |
| **Financial Exposure** | Disclosure that the homeowner may be responsible for costs exceeding the insurance payment |
| **Cancellation Right** | Notice of the 3-day cancellation right (see 6.3.2) |

#### 6.3.2 Three-Day Cancellation Right

- Homeowners have **3 business days** to cancel any assignment of benefits without penalty
- Cancellation must be effectuated by written notice to the assignee
- If cancelled, the assignee must return any payments received and cease all claim activity
- The cancellation notice must be provided as a detachable form with the AOB document

#### 6.3.3 Strict Font and Formatting Requirements

New Jersey's AOB reforms impose specific formatting requirements:

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Notice text | Minimum font size requirements (typically 12-point or larger for key disclosures) |
| Bold/highlighting | Critical warnings must be prominent |
| Language | Plain language; no legal jargon that obscures meaning |
| Signature blocks | Separate signature for acknowledgment of each major disclosure |
| Detachable cancellation form | Must be physically separable from the main document |

**Implication:** Any SmartContractor AOB document that fails these technical requirements is voidable and may trigger Consumer Fraud Act liability.

#### 6.3.4 Scope Limitation

The AOB reforms are understood to restrict or regulate:
- Assignment of **property insurance claim proceeds** (the primary SmartContractor concern)
- Post-storm emergency repair scenarios where contractor abuse was most prevalent
- Assignment of both the right to payment AND the right to pursue the claim (including litigation)

### 6.4 AOB Prohibited Practices

Under New Jersey's comprehensive framework, the following AOB-related practices are prohibited or severely restricted:

1. **Conditioning Repairs on AOB Execution:** Contractors may not refuse to perform work unless the homeowner signs an AOB
2. **Concealing AOB Nature:** The document must clearly be labeled as an "Assignment of Benefits" and not disguised as a repair contract or authorization
3. **Waiving Cancellation Rights:** Any purported waiver of the 3-day cancellation right is void
4. **Failing to Provide Copy:** The homeowner must receive a fully executed copy of the AOB immediately upon signing
5. **Unauthorized Claim Filing:** The assignee must not file claims or supplements without homeowner notification
6. **Excessive Charges:** Charges by the assignee that exceed customary and reasonable rates may be challenged

### 6.5 Contractor-AOB Interaction: The Public Adjuster Problem

A critical intersection in New Jersey: contractors who receive AOBs **cannot** then negotiate the insurance claim unless they are separately licensed as public adjusters. This creates a significant operational tension:

| Activity | Permitted Actor |
|----------|----------------|
| Execute home repair contract | Licensed home improvement contractor |
| Receive AOB of claim proceeds | Any assignee (with proper disclosures) |
| Negotiate claim amount with insurer | **Licensed public adjuster ONLY** |
| File claim supplements | **Licensed public adjuster ONLY** (if for compensation) |
| Litigate against insurer | Attorney or licensed public adjuster |

A contractor who receives an AOB and then negotiates the claim amount — or hires an unlicensed person to do so — violates the Public Adjusters' Licensing Act.

### 6.6 Health Insurance AOB (Reference Only)

For completeness, New Jersey maintains separate AOB regimes for health insurance:

- **NJ Surprise Bill Act (P.L. 2018, c. 32):** Mandatory assignment of benefits for emergency/urgent out-of-network services
- **NJSA 39:6A-4 (PIP):** Personal Injury Protection benefits assignable only to service benefit providers
- **N.J.A.C. 11:3-4.9:** Administrative regulations governing PIP AOB

These health insurance AOB frameworks are **not directly applicable** to property insurance claim advances but demonstrate New Jersey's comprehensive approach to AOB regulation.

## Section 7: Public Adjuster & Insurance Representation Rules

### 7.1 Licensing Requirements

Public adjusters in New Jersey are strictly licensed by DOBI under the Public Adjusters' Licensing Act (NJSA 17:22B-1 et seq.).

| Requirement | Specification |
|-------------|---------------|
| Initial License Fee | $300 (first year) |
| Renewal Fee | $70 (reduced rate) |
| Surety Bond | $10,000 required |
| Examination | Must pass state licensing exam within 12 months before application |
| Fingerprinting | Live Scan electronic fingerprinting required |
| Photographs | Two passport-size photos |
| License Term | Biennial (2 years) |
| Continuing Education | 15 hours per renewal period |
| Temporary Sublicense | Permitted during catastrophic loss (90 days, renewable once) |

### 7.2 Conduct Restrictions

Licensed public adjusters in New Jersey must comply with strict conduct rules:

| Restriction | Statutory Basis |
|-------------|----------------|
| **No solicitation 6pm–8am within 24 hours of loss** | NJSA 17:22B-13 |
| **Identification badge required** | Must wear visible ID badge when performing services |
| **License display** | License must be prominently displayed at place of business |
| **Contract required** | Written contract with insured required before services |
| **Fee disclosure** | Compensation terms must be clearly stated |
| **No contractor affiliation** | Cannot be a contractor or affiliated with a contractor on same claim |

The 6pm–8am solicitation restriction is particularly important for storm-response scenarios. A public adjuster who contacts a homeowner within 24 hours of a loss, between 6pm and 8am, commits a licensing violation.

### 7.3 Who May Negotiate with Insurance Companies

| Actor | Permitted to Negotiate Claims? | Basis |
|-------|-------------------------------|-------|
| **Licensed Public Adjuster** | Yes, for compensation | NJSA 17:22B |
| **Attorney** | Yes | Attorney-client representation |
| **Insurance Producer/Agent** | Limited (own client's policy only) | Agent authority |
| **Contractor** | **NO** — absolutely prohibited | NJSA 17:22B; NAPIA ethics |
| **Restoration Company** | **NO** — absolutely prohibited | Same restriction |
| **Unlicensed Person** | **NO** — criminal offense | Unauthorized practice |

**Critical Rule:** A contractor who negotiates an insurance claim on behalf of a homeowner — even informally, even with the homeowner's permission — violates New Jersey law. This includes:
- Calling the insurance company to discuss scope of damage
- Submitting estimates or supplements to the adjuster
- Attending adjuster meetings with the homeowner
- Advocating for higher claim payments
- Filing claim documentation

### 7.4 National Association of Public Insurance Adjusters (NAPIA) Prohibition

NAPIA, the leading professional organization for public adjusters, explicitly prohibits contractors from being public adjusters. This industry standard reinforces the statutory prohibition and provides additional ethical guidance.

## Section 8: Mortgagee, Loss Draft & Claims Settlement

### 8.1 Standard Loss Draft Practice in New Jersey

When a property with a mortgage suffers insured damage, the insurance claim check is typically made jointly payable to:
- The **insured homeowner**
- The **mortgagee/loss payee** (the lender or loan servicer)

This joint payee requirement protects the mortgage holder's secured interest in the property and ensures repair funds are used to restore collateral value.

### 8.2 NJ DOBI Guidance on Loss Drafts

DOBI confirms in its Insurance Proceeds Checks FAQ:

> "When claim checks are issued by your insurance company, they will likely be made payable jointly to you (the customer), and the mortgagee."

**Key DOBI Points:**
- Homeowners should contact their mortgage company immediately to determine endorsement and deposit procedures
- For large claims, the full amount may not be available immediately — funds are released in installments
- The mortgage holder may order inspections at its own expense before releasing funds
- The mortgage holder may place insurance proceeds in an escrow account, paying for repairs as work is completed
- Processing times vary significantly by mortgage servicer and claim size

### 8.3 Loss Draft Disbursement Mechanics

| Stage | Action | Timeline |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Initial Payment | Emergency advance for ALE, personal property | Days 1–7 post-claim |
| Structure Payment | Joint check to homeowner + mortgagee | After scope agreement |
| Mortgagee Endorsement | Homeowner sends check to servicer for endorsement | Varies (days to weeks) |
| Escrow Setup | Mortgagee establishes repair escrow account | After endorsement |
| Inspection Schedule | Mortgagee orders inspection at completion milestones | Ongoing |
| Fund Release | Mortgagee releases funds incrementally as work verified | Per milestone |
| Final Release | Remaining funds released upon certificate of completion | End of project |

### 8.4 Additional Living Expenses (ALE)

ALE coverage is typically 20% of dwelling coverage in New Jersey. ALE covers:
- Hotel/temporary housing
- Incremental meal costs
- Storage fees
- Laundry/dry cleaning
- Pet boarding
- Transportation (incremental costs only)

ALE payments are typically made directly to the insured (not jointly to the mortgagee) and may be advanced before expenses are incurred.

### 8.5 Unfair Claims Settlement Practices (NJSA 17B:30-2 et seq.)

New Jersey prohibits specific unfair claim settlement practices:

- Misrepresenting pertinent policy provisions or facts
- Failing to acknowledge and act reasonably promptly on communications
- Failing to adopt and implement reasonable standards for prompt investigation
- Refusing to pay claims without a reasonable basis
- Failing to attempt in good faith to effectuate prompt, fair, and equitable settlements
- Compelling insureds to institute litigation by offering substantially less than amounts ultimately recovered

### 8.6 Implications for SmartContractor ClaimBridge

| Feature | Status | Rationale |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| Directing insurance payments without mortgagee consent | **PROHIBITED** | Violates mortgagee's secured interest |
| Claim advance before mortgagee endorsement | Permitted with proper structure | Advance provider funds initial work; repaid when mortgagee releases funds |
| Escrow coordination with mortgagee | Permitted | Requires mortgagee cooperation agreement |
| Bypassing mortgagee entirely | **PROHIBITED** | Only possible if no mortgage exists |
| Automated claim status tracking | Permitted | Informational only |

## Section 9: Token Collateral & Digital Asset Regulation

### 9.1 Current Regulatory Framework: Money Transmitter Act

New Jersey currently regulates cryptocurrency and digital asset activities under its Money Transmitter Act (NJSA 17:15C-1 et seq.), not under a digital-asset-specific statute.

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Governing Statute | NJSA 17:15C-1 et seq. |
| Regulator | DOBI — Banking Division |
| Definition | "Payment instrument" broadly defined as "instrument or written order for the transmission or payment of money" |
| Core Requirement | NJSA 17:15C-4: "No person shall engage in the business of money transmission without a license" |
| Historical Practice | DOBI has regulated crypto firms as money transmitters |
| Triggering Activities | "Receipt of money for transmission" or "transmitting money" |

### 9.2 Proposed Digital Asset and Blockchain Technology Act (A2371/S1756)

New Jersey's legislature has considered but **NOT ENACTED** comprehensive digital asset legislation:

| Element | Status |
|---------|--------|
| Bill Numbers | A2371 / S1756 |
| Proposed Framework | "Digital Asset Service Provider" (DASP) licensing — similar to New York's BitLicense |
| Proposed Regulator | NJ Bureau of Securities (within DOBI/Law & Public Safety) |
| Licensing Scope | Receiving/transmitting digital assets; storage/custody; buying/selling; exchange; issuing; borrowing/lending/facilitating |
| Penalties (Proposed) | $500/day unlicensed operation; $10,000 first offense; $20,000 second offense |
| Application Fee (Proposed) | $1,000 non-refundable; 120-day review |
| **Legislative Status** | **NOT ENACTED** — passed Senate Appropriations Committee January 2023, referred |

### 9.3 Token Collateral Status Under NJ Law

Using cryptocurrency as collateral for a loan presents significant unresolved questions in New Jersey:

| Question | Status |
|----------|--------|
| Is crypto collateral legally enforceable? | No clear NJ statute; general contract principles likely apply |
| Does token custody require money transmitter license? | **LIKELY YES** — DOBI has regulated similar activities as money transmission |
| Does smart contract-based collateral lock require licensure? | **UNKNOWN — requires NJ counsel review** |
| Does automated liquidation trigger regulatory requirements? | **UNKNOWN — requires NJ counsel review** |
| Is the proposed DASP Act retroactive? | Would not be; applies only if enacted |
| Can a non-NY BitLicense holder operate in NJ? | Only if separately compliant with NJ money transmitter law |

### 9.4 Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act (NJSA 3B:14-61.1 et seq.)

New Jersey has enacted the Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act, allowing estates to manage digital assets under certain circumstances. This statute has limited direct relevance to SmartContractor's lending operations but demonstrates NJ's engagement with digital asset legal frameworks.
