## Section 1. State Summary & Key Findings

Connecticut presents a **complex and restrictive regulatory environment** for SmartContractor products. The state layers overlapping regimes — small-loan licensing, strict usury, money-transmission controls, public-adjuster prohibitions, and strong consumer-protection laws — that together create significant compliance risk for any contractor-linked financing product.

| Area | Key Finding |
|------|-------------|
| **Post-Loss AOB** | Connecticut has **no comprehensive Assignment of Benefits statute** for property insurance claims. HB 6967 (2025), which would have created a detailed post-loss AOB framework effective January 2026, **died in committee**. Under current common law (*Peck v. Public Service Mut. Ins. Co.*, 114 F.Supp.2d 51 (D. Conn. 2000)), post-loss assignments are generally permitted and anti-assignment clauses in insurance policies cannot bar post-loss assignments. |
| **Small-Loan Licensing** | **Required** for any person making loans to Connecticut borrowers under CGS SS 36a-555 to 36a-573. License required for loans to Connecticut borrowers regardless of lender location. |
| **Usury** | **12% general usury cap** (CGS S 37-4). Business loans over $10,000 to corporations are exempt. Penalties include **criminal prosecution and complete forfeiture of both principal and interest** (CGS SS 37-7, 37-8). |
| **Money Transmission / Virtual Currency** | Connecticut extensively regulates virtual currency under its Money Transmission Act (CGS SS 36a-595 to 36a-612). **PA 25-66** (effective October 1, 2025) expands coverage to digital wallets, imposes **one-to-one reserve requirements**, **prohibits encumbrance of customer virtual currency without direction**, and treats customer virtual currency as trust property. |
| **Public Adjusters** | Licensed by the CT Insurance Department (CGS S 38a-788 et seq.). Home improvement contractors are **expressly prohibited** from engaging in public adjuster activities. |
| **Contractor Licensing** | Home Improvement Contractors must register with the Department of Consumer Protection (DCP). No state-level general contractor license exists; local municipal licensing may apply. |
| **Consumer Protection** | The Connecticut Unfair Trade Practices Act (CUTPA, CGS SS 42-110a to 42-110q) provides broad remedies including actual damages, punitive damages, and attorney's fees. |

**Bottom Line**: All SmartContractor products — token collateral equipment credit, claim advances, and assignment of benefits financing — are treated as **BLOCKED** in Connecticut pending comprehensive legal review. Multiple overlapping regulatory regimes create significant compliance risk.

## Section 3. Lending / Finance Licensing

### 3.1 Small Loan Lender Licensing (CGS SS 36a-555 to 36a-573)

A **license is required** for any person who, by any method (mail, telephone, Internet, electronic means):

- Makes a small loan to a Connecticut borrower (CGS S 36a-556(a)(1))
- Offers, solicits, brokers, arranges, places, or finds a small loan for a Connecticut borrower (S 36a-556(a)(2))
- Engages in any activity intended to assist a prospective Connecticut borrower in obtaining a small loan, including lead generation (S 36a-556(a)(3))
- Receives payments of principal and interest on a small loan to a Connecticut borrower (S 36a-556(a)(4))
- Purchases, acquires, or receives assignment of a small loan to a Connecticut borrower (S 36a-556(a)(5))
- Advertises small loan services in Connecticut (S 36a-556(a)(6))

**Key requirements:**

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Minimum capital | $50,000 continuously available per licensed location (S 36a-565(d)) |
| Licensing channel | Via NMLS |
| License fee | $800 |
| Renewal | Annual; expires December 31 each year |
| Exemptions | CGS S 36a-557 — limited exemptions for banks, credit unions, certain supervised financial organizations |

### 3.2 Sales Finance Company License (CGS S 36a-771 et seq.)

- Required for persons purchasing retail installment contracts or otherwise engaging in sales finance
- License required via NMLS
- Foreign qualification required for out-of-state entities

### 3.3 Mortgage Lender / Broker / Servicer Licensing

- Connecticut requires NMLS licensing for mortgage brokers, mortgage lenders, mortgage correspondent lenders, and mortgage servicers
- Bonds required ($50,000 to $100,000 depending on license type)

### 3.4 Usury and Interest Rate Limitations (CGS S 37-4)

| Rate / Rule | Citation | Detail |
|-------------|----------|--------|
| **General usury cap** | CGS S 37-4 | **12% per annum** |
| Judgment rate | CGS S 37-1 | 8% when no agreement exists |
| **Business loan exemption** | CGS S 37-9 (as modified by PA 81-267) | Loans over $10,000 to corporations exempt |
| **Criminal penalty** | CGS S 37-7 | Fine up to $1,000, imprisonment up to 6 months, or both |
| **Civil forfeiture** | CGS S 37-8 | Lender who violates usury law **cannot bring court action to recover principal or interest** — complete forfeiture |
| Void loans | CGS S 36a-573 | Small loans made in violation are void and unenforceable |

### 3.5 Small Loan Rate Caps (CGS 36a-563)

| Loan Amount | Maximum Rate |
|-------------|-------------|
| Under $1,000 | **11.99%** |
| $1,000 – $5,000 | **36%** |

### 3.6 Connecticut Unfair Trade Practices Act (CUTPA) — CGS SS 42-110a to 42-110q

- Applies broadly to any "unfair methods of competition and unfair or deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce"
- Any person suffering an "ascertainable loss" may sue for actual damages, punitive damages, and attorney's fees
- Applies to both consumer AND commercial transactions
- Remedies include actual damages, injunctive relief, punitive damages (discretionary), and attorney's fees

## Section 4. Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules

### 4.1 Overview

Connecticut's real estate escrow regulations and consumer protection framework create a unique compliance landscape for **escrow-backed contractor advances** — any arrangement in which a contractor's advance payment or project deposit is held in a regulated escrow account pending project completion. SmartContractor must treat these structures as a distinct product category requiring standalone analysis.

### 4.2 Escrow Regulatory Framework

Connecticut regulates escrow activities through the Department of Banking and the Department of Consumer Protection:

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Escrow licensing** | Persons engaged in the business of receiving escrows for real estate transactions must hold appropriate licensure or registration under CGS Title 36a |
| **Escrow agent duties** | Fiduciary duties apply; commingling of escrow funds with operational funds is prohibited |
| **Record-keeping** | Escrow agents must maintain detailed records of all funds received and disbursed, subject to DOB examination |
| **Interest on escrow** | Connecticut does not mandate interest on escrow deposits unless agreed in writing |

### 4.3 Contractor Advance / Deposit Escrow Requirements

Under Connecticut's Home Improvement Act and DCP regulations:

- **Contract deposits are limited**: A home improvement contractor may not accept a deposit exceeding **$1,000 or 10% of the contract price, whichever is less**, unless the contract specifies special-order materials (CGS S 20-427)
- **Escrow of consumer deposits**: When a deposit exceeds the statutory threshold, DCP strongly recommends — and certain local ordinances may require — that funds be held in an escrow account until work commences or materials are delivered
- **Written contract required**: All home improvement contracts must be in writing and must specify payment schedules tied to work progress (CGS S 20-429)
- **Payment timing**: Homeowner payments should be tied to **completed phases of work**, not calendar dates

### 4.4 Escrow-Backed Advance Product Compliance for SmartContractor

If SmartContractor facilitates or intermediates contractor advances held in escrow, the following apply:

| Product Feature | Compliance Requirement |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| **Escrow holding** | SmartContractor or its designee may require a **Connecticut escrow agent license** or must partner with a licensed escrow agent or fiduciary institution (bank / credit union) |
| **Deposit cap** | Any consumer deposit must comply with the lesser-of-$1,000-or-10% rule unless a narrow special-materials exception applies |
| **Disbursement schedule** | Escrow release must be tied to verified completion of work phases, not automatic calendar triggers |
| **Commingling prohibition** | Consumer advance funds must be segregated from SmartContractor operational funds; separate escrow account required |
| **Fiduciary duty disclosure** | Escrow holder owes fiduciary duties to the consumer; this must be disclosed in all platform materials |
| **CUTPA applicability** | Any misrepresentation about escrow protection, premature disbursement, or failure to release funds on completion may trigger CUTPA liability |

### 4.5 Insurance Claim Proceeds Escrow

When a mortgaged property suffers damage, insurance claim checks are typically issued jointly to the homeowner and the mortgagee. For large claims (typically >$40,000) or delinquent loans, the mortgagee holds funds in escrow and disburses incrementally as repairs progress:

- **Mortgagee controls disbursement**: The lender's loss draft department manages inspection-based incremental releases
- **Contractor access to funds**: Contractors receive payment only after passing lender inspections at each phase
- **No specific Connecticut statute**: This process is governed by mortgage contract terms and federal mortgage servicing regulations
- **Escrow duration**: May extend 30–180+ days depending on repair scope and lender workload

### 4.6 Intersection with Small-Loan Licensing

An escrow-backed advance structured as a **loan to the contractor** (rather than a deposit held for the homeowner) may trigger small-loan licensing if:

- The advance is under $10,000
- The borrower is a Connecticut resident or the work is performed in Connecticut
- Interest or fees exceed the 12% general usury cap (or applicable small-loan tier rate)

**Recommended approach**: SmartContractor should treat all escrow-backed contractor advances as potentially subject to dual regulation (escrow licensing + small-loan licensing) until Connecticut counsel provides a definitive product opinion.

### 4.7 Status for SmartContractor

| Product | Status |
|---------|--------|
| Consumer deposit escrow | **BLOCKED** — Requires licensed escrow agent partnership; deposit caps; phase-based disbursement |
| Contractor working-capital advance (escrow-secured) | **BLOCKED** — Likely triggers small-loan licensing if <$10,000; usury analysis required |
| Insurance-proceeds escrow coordination | **BLOCKED** — Mortgagee loss draft process is lender-controlled; SmartContractor cannot guarantee fund availability |

## Section 5. Token Collateral / Crypto Notes

### 5.1 Connecticut Money Transmission Act (CGS SS 36a-595 to 36a-612)

**CRITICAL UPDATE**: Public Act 25-66 (signed June 30, 2025, effective **October 1, 2025**) makes Connecticut one of the most restrictive states for virtual currency activities.

#### Key Provisions Affecting SmartContractor Token Collateral:

1. **Virtual Currency Definition** (CGS S 36a-596(18)):
   - "Any type of digital unit that is used as a medium of exchange or a form of digitally stored value or that is incorporated into payment system technology"
   - Includes centralized, decentralized, and mined/created digital units
   - Expressly does NOT include online gaming currency or consumer affinity/rewards points

2. **License Required for Digital Wallet Activities** (S 36a-597):
   - Money transmission license required for receiving, transmitting, storing, or maintaining custody/control of virtual currency on behalf of another person
   - **Digital wallets are now expressly covered** (PA 25-66 expanded definition of "money transmission")
   - Unlicensed money transmission is a **Class D felony** (S 36a-597(b))

3. **Prohibition on Using Customer Virtual Currency** (PA 25-66, S 36a-613(f)):
   - Licensee **shall not**: "Sell, transfer, assign, lend, hypothecate, pledge or otherwise use or encumber virtual currency stored, held, controlled, maintained by, or under the custody or control of, such licensee on behalf of a person, except for the sale, transfer of ownership or assignment of such virtual currency at the direction of such person"
   - **This directly impacts any token collateral lock/liquidation mechanism** — a money transmitter cannot encumber customer virtual currency without explicit direction

4. **One-to-One Reserve Requirement** (CGS S 36a-603(b)):
   - Each licensee holding virtual currency for customers must hold the **same type and amount** of virtual currency owed to customers
   - Virtual currency held is deemed a **property interest** of claimants on a proportional basis
   - Such virtual currency is held **in trust** for claimants and is immune from creditor attachment

5. **Custody Restrictions** (PA 25-66):
   - Virtual currency may only be held by: (a) licensed money transmitter, (b) FDIC-insured bank/credit union, or (c) third party expressly approved by Banking Commissioner
   - Unapproved third-party custodians are prohibited

6. **Bond Requirements** (CGS S 36a-602):
   - Standard bond: $300,000 to $1,000,000 depending on transmission volume
   - For virtual currency transmitters: Bond amount determined by commissioner based on "current and prospective volatility of the market in such currency"

7. **Disclosure Requirements** (PA 25-66):
   - Extensive pre-transaction disclosures required (24-point font minimum)
   - Risk warnings regarding irreversibility of virtual currency transactions
   - Fee transparency
   - Receipts required for all transactions

8. **State Prohibition on Virtual Currency** (PA 25-66, new S 5):
   - Neither the state nor any political subdivision shall accept payment in virtual currency or invest in virtual currency

### 5.2 Implications for SmartContractor Token Collateral Product

- **TOKEN_COLLATERAL_BLOCKED_REQUIRES_COUNSEL_REVIEW**
- Any platform that holds, stores, or maintains custody of virtual currency on behalf of Connecticut users likely requires a **Connecticut money transmission license**
- The prohibition on encumbering customer virtual currency without explicit direction raises fundamental questions about whether automated collateral locks and liquidations are permissible
- One-to-one reserve requirements would apply to any customer virtual currency held
- Smart contract-based collateral mechanisms may or may not be characterized as "custody" — this requires specific legal analysis
- The Class D felony for unlicensed transmission creates extreme risk

### 5.3 Exemptions from Money Transmission (CGS S 36a-609)

- Federally insured banks and credit unions
- US Postal Service
- Electronic funds transfer of governmental benefits
- **No general exemption for lending activities or collateral holding**

## Section 6. Insurance Claim Advance Notes

### 6.1 Prompt Claims Payment (CGS S 38a-816)

- Connecticut's Unfair Insurance Practices Act requires prompt payment of claims
- For **health claims**: 60 days (paper) / 20 days (electronic) — these timelines apply to health insurance, not property/casualty
- **Property/casualty claims**: No specific statutory deadline found for property insurance claims, but unreasonable delay constitutes an unfair practice

### 6.2 Additional Living Expenses (ALE)

- ALE coverage is standard in Connecticut homeowners policies
- Typically covers up to 24 months while home is repaired/rebuilt
- Insurer reimburses actual additional expenses above normal living costs
- Documentation required (receipts, rental agreements)

### 6.3 Insurance Claim Proceeds and Mortgages

- When a home secures a mortgage, insurance claim checks ("loss drafts") are typically issued to **both the homeowner and the mortgagee** (lender)
- Mortgagee must endorse the check
- For large claims (typically >$40,000) or delinquent loans, mortgagee may hold funds in escrow and disburse incrementally
- Lender loss draft departments manage this process
- **No specific Connecticut statute found mandating particular procedures** — this appears to be governed by contract (mortgage/deed of trust terms) and federal mortgage servicing rules

### 6.4 Assignment of Insurance Claim Proceeds

- Under Connecticut common law, post-loss assignment of insurance claim benefits is generally **permitted**
- *Peck v. Public Service Mut. Ins. Co.*, 114 F.Supp.2d 51 (D. Conn. 2000): Anti-assignment clauses in insurance policies **cannot bar post-loss assignments**
- Assignee may bring suit for both breach of contract AND bad faith (breach of covenant of good faith and fair dealing)
- Regs. Conn. State Agencies S 38a-334-8: Policy may contain provision requiring insurer consent to assignment of "interest under the policy," but this does not apply post-loss

### 6.5 Status of AOB Legislation

- **HB 6967 (2025) died in committee** — would have created comprehensive AOB framework effective January 2026
- Until comprehensive legislation passes, AOB is governed by common law principles
- No specific prohibition on assignment of property insurance claim benefits to contractors/service providers

## Section 7. Assignment of Benefits Notes

### 7.1 Current Legal Status: ALLOWED (Common Law) / UNREGULATED STATUTORY

Connecticut currently has **NO comprehensive AOB statute for property insurance claims.** AOB is permitted under common law principles.

### 7.2 Common Law Rules (Currently in Effect)

1. Post-loss assignment of insurance benefits is **permitted** despite anti-assignment clauses
2. *Peck v. Public Service Mut. Ins. Co.* established that post-loss assignments cannot be barred by policy language
3. Assignee has standing to sue insurer directly for both breach of contract and bad faith
4. No specific statutory cancellation window, font-size requirements, or form requirements currently exist

### 7.3 What HB 6967 Would Have Required (for reference, as future legislation may incorporate these)

- Written agreement executed by both assignor and assignee
- Only covers post-loss repair, inspection, remediation, or mitigation services
- **14-day rescission window** (or 30 days if services haven't begun, or 30 days if no start date specified)
- Itemized per-unit cost estimate required
- Assignee must deliver executed agreement to insurance company
- **Emergency cap**: $3,000 or 1% of coverage limit, whichever is greater, for urgent/emergency circumstances
- **Prohibited fees**: Bank check/mortgage processing fees, cancellation penalties, administrative fees
- **Assignee prohibitions**: Cannot sue assignor for payment, cannot collect from assignor, cannot claim lien, cannot report to credit agencies
- **Assignee cannot negotiate/adjust/settle claim** (no public adjuster authority)
- Written notice of intent to sue required at least 10 days before filing action
- Pre-suit settlement offer or appraisal/ADR required
- Insurance company must investigate assignee's claimed damages
- **Non-compliant agreements void and unenforceable**

### 7.4 Practical Implications

- AOB is currently legal in Connecticut but **unregulated** for property insurance claims
- A future legislature may reintroduce HB 6967 or similar legislation
- SmartContractor should **NOT rely on AOB mechanisms in Connecticut without legal counsel**
- Any AOB arrangement must ensure the contractor/finance provider does not engage in public adjuster activities

## Section 8. Public Adjuster / Insurance Representation

### 8.1 Public Adjuster Licensing (CGS S 38a-788 et seq.)

**Definition** (CGS S 38a-788):

> "Public adjuster" means any person, partnership, association, LLC or corporation who:
> 1. On behalf of an insured and for monetary or other compensation: (A) prepares, documents and submits a first-party property claim to an insurance company, or (B) negotiates, adjusts or effects the settlement of such claim; OR
> 2. Advertises or solicits business as a public adjuster; OR
> 3. Holds himself/itself out as engaging in such activities as a business

**Key Exclusion**: "Lawyers settling claims of clients shall not be deemed to be public adjusters"

### 8.2 Licensing Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Licensing authority | Connecticut Insurance Department (CID) |
| Examination | Required |
| Background check | Criminal background check with fingerprinting |
| Bond | Surety bond required |
| Renewal | Licenses expire and require renewal |

### 8.3 Prohibited Activities for Non-Adjusters

- **Home improvement contractors CANNOT engage in public adjuster activities** (this prohibition was strengthened in pending legislation and is enforced under existing law)

**A contractor MAY:**
- Explain or discuss a bid for construction/repair with the property owner or insurer (for usual and customary fees)
- Recommend that the property owner contact their insurer about coverage

**A contractor CANNOT:**
- Prepare, document, or submit insurance claims on behalf of the insured
- Negotiate or effect settlement of claims
- Hold themselves out as claim experts or insurance representatives
- Advertise "we handle your claim" or similar

### 8.4 Critical SmartContractor Implication

- **SmartContractor, its contractors, and any financing parties MUST NOT engage in public adjuster activities**
- Any platform feature that assists homeowners in preparing, documenting, or submitting claims is HIGH RISK
- Any fee structure tied to claim outcomes may be characterized as public adjusting
- If SmartContractor provides claim assistance, it must be purely informational and not involve negotiation with insurers
- The line between "contractor estimating" and "claim preparation" is fact-specific and requires legal analysis

### 9.3 Specific Smart Contract Risks

1. **Automated Collateral Lock**: Connecticut law may characterize automated collateral locking as "maintaining custody or control of virtual currency" requiring a money transmission license. The one-to-one reserve requirement and prohibition on encumbrance without customer direction create fundamental conflicts with typical DeFi collateral mechanisms.

2. **Automated Liquidation**: Liquidation of customer collateral without explicit customer direction for each transaction may violate CGS S 36a-613(f), which prohibits "sell, transfer, assign, lend, hypothecate, pledge or otherwise use or encumber virtual currency" without the person's direction.

3. **Oracle-Based Repayment**: Routing repayment from insurance proceeds involves coordination with mortgagees, insurers, and loss draft departments. Smart contracts cannot practically enforce this without off-chain legal mechanisms.

4. **On-Chain AOB**: Recording assignment of benefits on-chain does not substitute for legal compliance with Connecticut's (future) AOB requirements or public adjuster prohibitions.

5. **Escrow Smart Contracts**: Automated escrow disbursement triggered by oracle-verified work completion may conflict with Connecticut's requirement that escrow agents exercise fiduciary discretion and maintain detailed records subject to DOB examination.

| Insurance Claim Risk | **HIGH** | No specific statutory framework for claim advances. Common law allows post-loss AOB but with significant uncertainty. Mortgagee/loss draft requirements complicate claim proceeds access. Pending legislation (HB 6967 died) may be reintroduced. |
| AOB Risk | **MEDIUM** | Post-loss AOB currently permitted under common law (*Peck v. Public Service Mut. Ins. Co.*). However, no statutory framework provides certainty. Future legislation likely. Non-compliant agreements could be voided if legislation passes retroactively. |
| Public Adjuster Risk | **HIGH** | Strict licensing required. Contractors explicitly prohibited from public adjuster activities. Any claim assistance feature creates risk of unlicensed public adjusting. Criminal and civil penalties may apply. |
| Token Collateral Risk | **HIGH** | Money transmission license likely required for virtual currency custody. PA 25-66 (eff. 10/1/2025) creates severe restrictions on using customer virtual currency. Class D felony for unlicensed transmission. One-to-one reserve requirements. Automated smart contract mechanisms face fundamental legal conflicts. |
| Consumer Protection Risk | **HIGH** | CUTPA (CGS S 42-110a) applies broadly to all trade/commerce. Three-day cancellation right for home improvement contracts. Home Improvement Guaranty Fund provides additional homeowner remedies. DCP actively enforces contractor registration requirements. Advertising and disclosure requirements are extensive. |
| Escrow-Backed Advance Risk | **HIGH** | CT escrow licensing requirements; deposit caps ($1,000 or 10%); fiduciary duty obligations; phase-based disbursement requirements; intersection with small-loan licensing; mortgagee loss draft coordination adds third-party control. |
