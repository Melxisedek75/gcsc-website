## 1. State Summary & Key Facts

Nebraska presents a **mixed regulatory environment** for SmartContractor products. The state is notably **crypto-friendly** — it was the first state to adopt UCC Article 12 (controllable electronic records) and enacted the Nebraska Financial Innovation Act (LB 649, 2021), creating a charter framework for Digital Asset Depository Institutions (DADIs). This provides a favorable foundation for token collateral concepts.

However, Nebraska maintains a **regulated but not prohibited** stance on Assignment of Benefits (AOB) through the Insured Homeowners Protection Act (Neb. Rev. Stat. SS 44-8601 to 44-8608), which imposes strict form, notice, and procedural requirements on post-loss assignments to residential contractors. A proposed bill (LB1137, February 2026) would ban AOBs for residential contractors but remains in committee.

Consumer lending is regulated by the Nebraska Department of Banking and Finance (NDBF) under the Installment Loan Act (SS 45-1004 et seq.) and the Delayed Deposit Services Licensing Act (SS 45-901 to 45-930). The Installment Loan Act was recently expanded by LB 717 (2026), raising the threshold from $25,000 to $100,000. Money transmission requires an NMLS license under the Nebraska Money Transmitters Act (Neb. Rev. Stat. 8-2701 et seq., as updated by LB 474, 2025).

Nebraska imposes a **16% usury cap** for loans under $2,000 unless the lender is licensed under the Installment Loan Act (Neb. Rev. Stat. 45-101.03). Licensed installment lenders may charge rates above the usury cap pursuant to their license.

Public adjuster licensing is required (Neb. Rev. Stat. SS 44-9201 to 44-9219), with a $20,000 bond and examination requirement. Contractors must register with the Nebraska Department of Labor; general contractor licensing is primarily local. Escrow activities may be regulated by the NDBF.

**Bottom line:** SmartContractor products face significant licensing and regulatory requirements in Nebraska. All live operations remain **BLOCKED** pending legal review.

## 3. Lending / Finance Licensing Notes

### 3.1 Installment Loan Licensing
- **Statute:** Nebraska Installment Loan Act, Neb. Rev. Stat. S 45-1004 et seq.
- **Regulator:** Nebraska Department of Banking and Finance (NDBF)
- **License Required:** Yes, for engaging in the business of making installment loans, AND for holding/acquiring rights of ownership, servicing, or participation in installment loans, OR engaging with installment loan borrowers.
- **Key Requirements:**
  - $50,000 surety bond
  - Audited corporate financial statement (current within 6 months)
  - $500 non-refundable application fee
  - Public hearing may be required
  - License valid through December 31 of issuance year, then annual renewal
  - Applications submitted via NMLS

### 3.2 LB 717 (2026) — Expanded Scope
- Signed February 25, 2026; effective approximately July 2026
- **Removes the $25,000 loan amount threshold** from the definition of "loan" / "installment loan"
- **Raises the general usury exemption threshold from $25,000 to $100,000**
- Result: ILSA license will be required for consumer-purpose loans under $100,000 with terms of 6+ months and interest exceeding the statutory usury rate
- New disclosure requirements for refinancing residential mortgage loans
- New prohibition on loans exceeding 145 months (except mobile home-secured loans)

### 3.3 Usury Cap — Neb. Rev. Stat. 45-101.03
- **16% per annum** maximum interest rate for loans under $2,000 made by unlicensed lenders
- Licensed installment lenders are exempt from the usury cap for loans within their licensed authority
- Violations may result in forfeiture of interest and potential criminal penalties
- All consumer-purpose loans under $2,000 must stay within the usury cap OR be made by a licensed lender

### 3.4 Delayed Deposit Services (Payday Lending)
- **Statute:** Neb. Rev. Stat. SS 45-901 to 45-930
- **Regulator:** NDBF
- **License Required:** Yes, $50,000 bond
- **Rate Cap:** 36% APR maximum (Nebraska Initiative 428, approved November 2020)
- Transactions exceeding 36% APR are void and uncollectable

### 3.5 Money Transmission
- **Statute:** Nebraska Money Transmitters Act (updated by LB 474, 2025, effective October 1, 2025); Neb. Rev. Stat. 8-2701 et seq.
- **Regulator:** NDBF
- **License Required:** Yes, through NMLS
- **Key Requirements:**
  - Minimum net worth: greater of $100,000 or 3% of first $100M in assets, 2% of $100M-$1B, 0.5% above $1B
  - Surety bond: $100,000 minimum, up to $500,000 maximum
  - Application fee: $1,500; renewal fee: $750
  - Audited financial statements required annually
  - CSBS model language incorporated

### 3.6 Commercial / Business-Purpose Loans
- Business-purpose loans may fall outside consumer lending licensing requirements
- However, any entity "holding or acquiring" rights in consumer loans needs a license
- **Contractor financing:** If structured as commercial/business-purpose, may avoid consumer lending licensing, but structure must be bona fide commercial
- **UNKNOWN_REQUIRES_COUNSEL_REVIEW** for specific product structuring

### 3.7 Loan Brokering
- Nebraska requires licensing for marketers, servicers, and purchasers of interests in certain loans (2023 amendments)
- Any entity marketing, owning, holding, acquiring, servicing, or participating in a loan made by a financial institution or ILSA licensee may need a license

## 4. Escrow-Backed Contractor Advance Rules

### 4.1 Escrow Regulation in Nebraska
- **Regulator:** Nebraska Department of Banking and Finance (NDBF) may regulate escrow activities
- No standalone "escrow agent" license exists at the state level; escrow services are typically provided by:
  - Licensed title insurers and title agents
  - Licensed attorneys
  - Federally insured financial institutions
  - Real estate brokers (for transaction-related escrow)
- Entities holding third-party funds in connection with construction or insurance claim disbursements should consult NDBF on licensing applicability

### 4.2 Escrow-Backed Advance Product Structure
A contractor advance product that relies on escrow-held insurance proceeds or mortgagee loss drafts must account for:

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| **Escrow Agent** | Must be a licensed/authorized escrow provider (title company, bank, attorney) |
| **Escrow Agreement** | Must clearly define conditions for release of funds to contractor |
| **Mortgagee Consent** | Required if mortgagee holds loss draft; AOB cannot impair mortgagee rights (SS 44-8605) |
| **Insurer Notice** | If AOB is used, 5-day insurer notice requirement applies |
| **Lien Waivers** | Standard industry practice; contractor must provide waivers at each draw |
| **Inspection Triggers** | Mortgage servicers typically require inspection before each draw release |

### 4.3 Licensing Implications for Escrow-Backed Advances
- If the advance is structured as a **purchase of escrow receivables** (factoring), may avoid Installment Loan Act licensing
- If structured as a **loan** secured by escrow proceeds, Installment Loan Act licensing likely applies for consumer purposes
- If the escrow holder is also the advance provider, dual licensing questions (escrow + lending) may arise
- **Money transmission:** Routing funds from escrow to contractor to advance provider may trigger Money Transmitter Act (Neb. Rev. Stat. 8-2701 et seq.) if not conducted through a licensed bank

## 5. Token Collateral / Crypto Notes

### 5.1 Nebraska Financial Innovation Act (LB 649, 2021)
- **Effective:** October 1, 2021 (banking provisions); July 1, 2022 (UCC provisions)
- Nebraska is the **most crypto-friendly state** in the U.S. for SmartContractor's purposes
- First state to adopt UCC Article 12 (controllable electronic records)
- Created **Digital Asset Depository Institutions (DADIs)**

### 5.2 Digital Asset Depository Institutions (DADIs)
- Charter required from NDBF
- Capital requirement: $10 million in capital stock
- May provide: digital asset custody, stablecoin issuance, payment services, lending against digital assets
- Must maintain unencumbered liquid assets = 100% of digital assets in custody (S 8-3009)
- Must maintain surety bond or pledged assets for liquidation costs
- Subject to NDBF examination and reporting
- **Telcoin Digital Asset Bank** received first DADI charter in November 2025

### 5.3 UCC Article 12 — Controllable Electronic Records (CERs)
- **Statute:** Neb. Rev. Stat. U.C.C. SS 12-101 et seq.
- **Effective:** July 1, 2022
- Defines CERs: cryptocurrencies, NFTs, and other digital assets with embedded payment rights
- Establishes "control" as: power to enjoy substantially all benefit + exclusive power to prevent others + exclusive power to transfer
- Secured party may perfect by **filing UCC-1** or by **obtaining control**
- **Control gives priority** over filing-only perfection
- Qualifying purchaser who obtains control for value, in good faith, without notice takes FREE of competing claims

### 5.4 Money Transmission — Digital Assets
- LB 474 (2025) updated the Money Transmitters Act (Neb. Rev. Stat. 8-2701 et seq.)
- Exemptions codified for registered broker-dealers, futures commission merchants, and third-party service providers
- Digital asset transmission may trigger money transmitter licensing depending on specifics

### 5.5 Token Collateral Assessment for SmartContractor
- **Nebraska law provides the strongest U.S. state-level framework for digital asset collateral**
- UCC Article 12 + Article 9 amendments provide clear perfection and priority rules
- Digital asset custody services available through regulated DADIs
- Secured lending against digital assets explicitly authorized under Nebraska law
- However, **token collateral lending to consumers** may trigger Installment Loan Act licensing
- **Stablecoin payments** may be facilitated through DADI partnerships

### 5.6 Token Collateral Status

## 6. Insurance Claim Advance Notes

### 6.1 Additional Living Expenses (ALE)
- ALE is a standard component of Nebraska homeowners insurance policies
- Typically covers: temporary housing, excess food costs, storage, laundry, pet boarding, transportation
- Usually limited to 20-30% of dwelling coverage or a specified dollar/time limit
- Most insurers provide ALE on a **reimbursement basis** (receipts required)
- Policyholders may request a **cash advance** for ALE, deducted from total coverage
- No specific Nebraska statute mandating emergency claim advances was found

### 6.2 Claim Payment Process
- Initial claim payments: insurer may issue partial payments during claim adjustment
- Mortgagee/loss payee named on checks if mortgage exists
- Loss draft checks under $40,000 (industry standard) may be endorsed and released to homeowner
- Loss draft checks over $40,000 typically held in escrow by mortgage servicer, disbursed in draws as repairs progress

### 6.3 SmartContractor Claim Advance Product
- No Nebraska statute explicitly authorizes third-party insurance claim advances
- Any product providing advances against anticipated claim proceeds raises lending/licensing questions
- If structured as a loan: Installment Loan Act licensing may apply
- If repayment comes from insurance proceeds: Assignment of Benefits rules apply (see Section 7)
- **Status: BLOCKED pending legal review of lending and AOB compliance**

## 7. Assignment of Benefits Notes

### 7.1 Overview: AOB is REGULATED (not prohibited)
- **Statute:** Insured Homeowners Protection Act, Neb. Rev. Stat. SS 44-8601 to 44-8608
- **Enacted:** 2012 (LB 943); amended 2017 (LB 220), 2018 (LB 743)
- Post-loss assignments of insurance rights/benefits to residential contractors are **permitted** but **heavily regulated**

### 7.2 Key AOB Requirements
1. **Written Agreement Required:** Post-loss assignment must be in writing
2. **Specific Disclosure Language:** Must include in **14-point, ALL-CAPS font**:
   - Warning that homeowner is assigning rights under their insurance policy
   - Statement that contractor may pursue rights/remedies the homeowner has
   - Statement that insurer may only pay for covered perils
   - Warning that deductible rebating is illegal
   - Statement that homeowner is personally responsible for deductible
3. **Homeowner Signature:** Must sign and date below the disclosure language
4. **Insurer Notice:** Contractor must provide copy to insurer within **5 business days**
5. **No Coverage Assurances:** Assignment must include statement that contractor has not assured loss will be fully covered
6. **No Deductible Rebating:** Prohibited; civil and criminal penalties apply
7. **Cannot Impair Mortgagee:** Assignment cannot impair rights of mortgagee
8. **Communication Rights:** Cannot prohibit insured or mortgagee from communicating directly with insurer
9. **Itemized Estimate:** Contractor must provide itemized description of work, materials, labor, and fees before beginning work

### 7.3 Consequences of Non-Compliance
- **Contract is VOID** if residential contractor violates any provision (S 44-8608)
- Violations may subject contractor to civil and criminal penalties

### 7.4 Pending Legislation
- **LB1137 (introduced January 2026):** Would ban AOBs for residential contractors and prohibit them from negotiating with insurance companies
- As of the research date, LB1137 remains in committee and has not advanced
- Nebraska's existing AOB framework still applies

### 7.5 AOB Status for SmartContractor
- AOBs are **regulated but currently allowed** in Nebraska
- SmartContractor or contractors facilitating AOBs must comply with ALL statutory requirements
- 14-point all-caps disclosure language is mandatory
- 5-day insurer notice is mandatory
- Failure = void contract
- **Status: LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED for any AOB component**

## 8. Public Adjuster / Insurance Representation Notes

### 8.1 Licensing Required
- **Statute:** Nebraska Public Adjusters Licensing Act, Neb. Rev. Stat. SS 44-9201 to 44-9219
- **Regulator:** Nebraska Department of Insurance
- **Effective:** July 19, 2018 (Insurance Consultant License no longer includes public adjuster authority)

### 8.2 Resident Public Adjuster Requirements
- At least 18 years old
- Principal place of residence or business in Nebraska
- Pass public adjuster licensing examination (Series 13-10)
- $50 application fee
- $20,000 surety bond
- Trustworthy, reliable, good reputation
- Maintain office in Nebraska with public access
- 24 hours continuing education every 2 years (including 3 hours ethics)

### 8.3 Non-Resident Public Adjuster
- Must hold active public adjuster license in home state
- $50 application fee

### 8.4 Public Adjuster Agencies
- Business entities acting as public adjusters must also obtain a public adjuster agency license (S 44-9207)

### 8.5 Fee Limitations
- Fee must be listed in written contract
- **Maximum 10% of insurance settlement** in catastrophic disasters

### 8.6 Key Restriction for SmartContractor
- **Contractors and SmartContractor personnel CANNOT act as public adjusters without a license**
- Negotiating with insurance companies on behalf of homeowners is a regulated activity
- A contractor may negotiate repairs/pricing but cannot represent the insured in claim settlement without a public adjuster license
- LB1137 (pending) would explicitly prohibit contractors from negotiating with insurance companies on behalf of homeowners

### 8.7 Public Adjuster Status
```
PUBLIC_ADJUSTER_ACTIVITY: PROHIBITED_WITHOUT_LICENSE
- SmartContractor employees/contractors cannot negotiate claims on behalf of homeowners
- Contractors can discuss scope of repairs but cannot act as claim representatives
- Separate public adjuster license required for claim negotiation/representation
- Violation = unlicensed insurance activity = criminal/administrative penalties
```

## 9. Mortgage / Loss Draft Notes

### 9.1 Mortgagee/Loss Payee Rights
- Standard homeowners insurance policies include a mortgage clause creating separate contract rights for mortgagee
- Mortgagee typically named as "loss payee" on policy
- Insurance claim checks for real property damage typically issued jointly to insured and mortgagee

### 9.2 Loss Draft Check Disbursement (Industry Standard)
- **Under $40,000** (or loan current): Mortgage servicer may endorse and return check to homeowner
- **Over $40,000** (or loan delinquent): Mortgage servicer deposits check into escrow, disburses in draws as repairs progress
- Servicers require: adjuster's report, contractor estimates, signed affidavit, W-9 from contractor, lien waivers
- Inspections may be required before each draw release

### 9.3 Nebraska-Specific Notes
- AOB cannot impair mortgagee rights under Nebraska law (S 44-8605)
- Mortgagee retains right to communicate directly with insurer regardless of any AOB
- Mortgage servicers have significant control over insurance proceeds disbursement
- Any SmartContractor product must account for mortgagee involvement in claim proceeds

### 9.4 Loss Draft Status for SmartContractor
```
LOSS_DRAFT/CLAIM_PROCEEDS: MORTGAGEE_CONTROLS_DISBURSEMENT
- Mortgagee named on claim checks when mortgage exists
- Mortgage servicer controls timing and method of disbursement
- SmartContractor cannot bypass mortgagee to obtain claim proceeds
- Repayment routing from insurance proceeds subject to mortgagee priority
```
